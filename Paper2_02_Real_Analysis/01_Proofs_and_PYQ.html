§ Proofs — Series Convergence

Integral Test — Proof

Let \(f:[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\) be positive, continuous, and decreasing. Then \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty f(n)\) converges iff \(\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx<\infty\).

Proof

Since \(f\) is decreasing: on \([n,n+1]\), \(f(n+1)\le f(x)\le f(n)\). Integrate over \([n,n+1]\): \(f(n+1)\le\int_n^{n+1}f\le f(n)\).

Sum from \(n=1\) to \(N\): \(\sum_{n=2}^{N+1}f(n)\le\int_1^{N+1}f\le\sum_{n=1}^N f(n)\).

If integral converges: partial sums \(\sum f(n)\le f(1)+\int_1^\infty f<\infty\). Bounded increasing → converges.

If series converges: \(\int_1^{N+1}f\le\sum_{n=1}^N f(n)\le\sum_{n=1}^\infty f(n)<\infty\). Integral converges. \(\blacksquare\)

Radius of Convergence — Cauchy-Hadamard Formula

\(R=\dfrac{1}{\limsup_{n\to\infty}|a_n|^{1/n}}\).

Proof

Let \(L=\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}\). For the power series \(\sum a_n x^n\), apply Root test: \(\limsup|a_nx^n|^{1/n}=|x|L\). If \(|x|L<1\) (i.e. \(|x|<1/L=R\)): series converges absolutely. If \(|x|L>1\): terms \(\not\to0\), series diverges. So radius is \(R=1/L\). \(\blacksquare\)

Uniform Continuity Theorem (Cantor)

Every continuous function on a closed bounded interval is uniformly continuous.

Proof (by contradiction using BW)

Suppose not uniformly continuous: \(\exists\epsilon_0>0\) and sequences \(x_n,y_n\in[a,b]\) with \(|x_n-y_n|<1/n\) but \(|f(x_n)-f(y_n)|\ge\epsilon_0\).

By BW: \((x_n)\) has a subsequence \(x_{n_k}\to c\in[a,b]\). Then \(y_{n_k}\to c\) too (since \(|x_{n_k}-y_{n_k}|<1/n_k\to0\)). By continuity: \(f(x_{n_k})\to f(c)\) and \(f(y_{n_k})\to f(c)\). So \(|f(x_{n_k})-f(y_{n_k})|\to0\). Contradicts \(\ge\epsilon_0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem — Proof

If \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\), then \(\exists c\in(a,b)\) s.t. \(f'(c)=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\).

Proof (via Rolle's Theorem)

Define \(g(x)=f(x)-\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}(x-a)\). Then \(g(a)=f(a)\) and \(g(b)=f(b)-\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}(b-a)=f(a)\). So \(g(a)=g(b)\). Since \(g\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\), by Rolle's theorem: \(\exists c\) with \(g'(c)=0\). \(g'(c)=f'(c)-\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

\(f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n\dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}\) for some \(\xi\) between \(a\) and \(x\).

Define \(R_n(x)=f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^n\dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k\) and \(g(t)=(x-t)^{n+1}\). Apply Cauchy MVT to \(F(t)=f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^n\dfrac{f^{(k)}(t)}{k!}(x-t)^k\) and \(G(t)=(x-t)^{n+1}\) on \([a,x]\).

\(F(x)=0\), \(G(x)=0\). Cauchy MVT: \(\dfrac{F(a)-F(x)}{G(a)-G(x)}=\dfrac{F'(\xi)}{G'(\xi)}\). Compute \(F'(t)=-\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(t)}{n!}(x-t)^n\), \(G'(t)=-(n+1)(x-t)^n\). So \(\dfrac{F(a)}{G(a)}=\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}\), giving \(R_n=\dfrac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Uniform Convergence

Weierstrass M-test — Proof

If \(|f_n(x)|\le M_n\) for all \(x\in S\) and \(\sum M_n<\infty\), then \(\sum f_n\) converges uniformly on \(S\).

Proof

Since \(\sum M_n<\infty\): the tails \(\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty M_n\to0\) as \(N\to\infty\). For any \(x\in S\):

\(\left|\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty f_n(x)\right|\le\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty|f_n(x)|\le\sum_{n=N+1}^\infty M_n\to0\) uniformly (bound is independent of \(x\)). So the series converges uniformly. \(\blacksquare\)

Uniform Convergence Preserves Continuity — Proof

If \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) and each \(f_n\) is continuous, then \(f\) is continuous.

Proof (ε/3 argument)

Fix \(x_0\). For \(\epsilon>0\): by uniform convergence, \(\exists N\) s.t. \(\sup|f_n-f|<\epsilon/3\) for all \(n>N\). Fix such \(n\). By continuity of \(f_n\): \(\exists\delta\) s.t. \(|x-x_0|<\delta\Rightarrow|f_n(x)-f_n(x_0)|<\epsilon/3\). Then:

\(|f(x)-f(x_0)|\le|f(x)-f_n(x)|+|f_n(x)-f_n(x_0)|+|f_n(x_0)-f(x_0)|<\dfrac{\epsilon}{3}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{3}+\dfrac{\epsilon}{3}=\epsilon\). \(\blacksquare\)

Uniform Convergence ⟹ Term-by-Term Integration — Proof

If \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) and each \(f_n\) is Riemann integrable, then \(\int_a^b f=\lim\int_a^b f_n\).

Proof

\(\left|\int_a^b f-\int_a^b f_n\right|\le\int_a^b|f-f_n|\le\sup|f-f_n|\cdot(b-a)\to0\) by uniform convergence. \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Bounded Variation & Metric Spaces

Jordan Decomposition — BV = Difference of Monotone Functions
Proof

Let \(f\in BV[a,b]\). Define \(p(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}[V_a^x(f)+f(x)-f(a)]\) and \(q(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}[V_a^x(f)-f(x)+f(a)]\). Then \(f(x)-f(a)=p(x)-q(x)\), and \(p,q\ge0\). Both are non-decreasing: \(p(y)-p(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}[V_x^y(f)+(f(y)-f(x))]\ge0\) since \(|f(y)-f(x)|\le V_x^y(f)\). Similarly for \(q\). So \(f=f(a)+p-q\) with \(p,q\) non-decreasing. \(\blacksquare\)

Banach Contraction Principle — Proof

If \((X,d)\) is a complete metric space and \(T:X\to X\) is a contraction (\(d(Tx,Ty)\le kd(x,y)\), \(k<1\)), then \(T\) has a unique fixed point.

Proof

Step 1 Existence: Pick any \(x_0\). Define \(x_n=T^n x_0\). Then \(d(x_{n+1},x_n)\le k^n d(x_1,x_0)\). For \(m>n\): \(d(x_m,x_n)\le\dfrac{k^n}{1-k}d(x_1,x_0)\to0\). So \((x_n)\) is Cauchy.

Step 2 By completeness: \(x_n\to x^*\). By continuity of \(T\) (it's a contraction): \(Tx^*=T(\lim x_n)=\lim Tx_n=\lim x_{n+1}=x^*\).

Step 3 Uniqueness: If \(Tx^*=x^*\) and \(Ty^*=y^*\): \(d(x^*,y^*)=d(Tx^*,Ty^*)\le k\,d(x^*,y^*)\Rightarrow(1-k)d(x^*,y^*)\le0\Rightarrow d=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Heine-Borel Theorem (in ℝⁿ)

A subset of \(\mathbb{R}^n\) is compact iff it is closed and bounded.

Proof (ℝ case)

Compact ⟹ closed and bounded: Every compact metric space is complete (closed in any metric space) and totally bounded (bounded in \(\mathbb{R}\)).

Closed and bounded ⟹ compact: Let \(K\subset[a,b]\) be closed and bounded. Let \((x_n)\subset K\). By BW, has subsequence \(x_{n_k}\to L\). Since \(K\) is closed: \(L\in K\). So every sequence has a convergent subsequence in \(K\) → \(K\) is sequentially compact → compact (in \(\mathbb{R}\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Series Convergence

Test for convergence: \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{n!}{n^n}\).

Solution (Ratio Test)

\(\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\dfrac{(n+1)!}{(n+1)^{n+1}}\cdot\dfrac{n^n}{n!}=\dfrac{(n+1)n^n}{(n+1)^{n+1}}=\dfrac{n^n}{(n+1)^n}=\left(\dfrac{n}{n+1}\right)^n=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)^n\to e^{-1}<1\). Series converges.

PYQ 2022 — Bounded Variation

Show that \(f(x)=x\sin(\pi/x)\), \(f(0)=0\) is NOT of bounded variation on \([0,1]\).

Solution

Consider partition points \(x_k=2/(2k+1)\) for \(k=0,1,2,\ldots\) At these points \(\sin(\pi/x_k)=\sin((2k+1)\pi/2)=\pm1\) alternately, so \(f(x_k)=x_k\cdot(\pm1)=\pm x_k\). The variation over this partition: \(\sum|f(x_{k+1})-f(x_k)|\ge\sum(x_k+x_{k+1})\ge\sum 2/(2k+2)\sim\sum 1/k=\infty\). So \(V_0^1(f)=\infty\). Not BV. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Series with AM-GM

Test \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots(2n)}\) for convergence.

Solution (Raabe's Test)

Let \(a_n=\dfrac{(2n-1)!!}{(2n)!!}\). Ratio: \(\dfrac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}=\dfrac{2n+2}{2n+1}=1+\dfrac{1}{2n+1}\). Raabe: \(n\!\left(\dfrac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}-1\right)=\dfrac{n}{2n+1}\to\dfrac{1}{2}<1\). Series diverges.

PYQ 2020 — Power Series Radius

Find the radius of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^2}x^n\).

Solution (Ratio Test)

\(\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}=\dfrac{(2n+2)!}{((n+1)!)^2}\cdot\dfrac{(n!)^2}{(2n)!}\cdot|x|=\dfrac{(2n+2)(2n+1)}{(n+1)^2}|x|=\dfrac{4n+2}{n+1}|x|\to4|x|\). Converges if \(4|x|<1\Rightarrow R=1/4\).

PYQ 2019 — lim sup and lim inf

Find \(\limsup\) and \(\liminf\) of \(a_n=(-1)^n\!\left(1+\dfrac{1}{n}\right)\).

Solution

For odd \(n\): \(a_n=-(1+1/n)\to-1\). For even \(n\): \(a_n=1+1/n\to1\). \(\limsup=1\), \(\liminf=-1\).

PYQ 2018 — Uniform Convergence of Sequence

Discuss uniform convergence of \(f_n(x)=\dfrac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\) on \([0,1]\).

Solution

Pointwise limit: \(f(x)=0\) for all \(x\in[0,1]\) (for \(x=0\): trivially 0; for \(x>0\): \(nx/(n^2x^2+1)\sim1/(nx)\to0\)).

\(\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|f_n(x)-0|=\sup\dfrac{nx}{1+n^2x^2}\). Maximum at \(x=1/n\): value \(=\dfrac{n/n}{1+1}=1/2\). So \(\sup=1/2\not\to0\). NOT uniformly convergent on \([0,1]\).

On \([\delta,1]\) for any \(\delta>0\): \(\sup\le\dfrac{1}{n\delta^2}\to0\). Uniformly convergent on \([\delta,1]\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Absolute & Conditional Convergence

Test \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^p}\) for absolute and conditional convergence for various \(p>0\).

Solution

Absolute convergence: \(\sum 1/n^p\) converges iff \(p>1\). So series absolutely convergent for \(p>1\).

Conditional convergence: By Leibniz alternating test (terms decrease monotonically to 0 for \(p>0\)): series converges for all \(p>0\). For \(01\): absolutely convergent. For \(p\le0\): diverges (terms don't tend to 0).

PYQ 2015 — Radius of Convergence (simpler)

Find the radius of convergence and region of convergence of \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{x^n}{n^2}\).

Solution

By root test: \(\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}=\limsup(1/n^2)^{1/n}=1\). So \(R=1\). At \(x=1\): \(\sum 1/n^2\) converges (p-series, \(p=2>1\)). At \(x=-1\): \(\sum(-1)^n/n^2\) converges (absolutely). Converges on \([-1,1]\).

PYQ 2013 — Integral Test Application

Show that \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^\infty\frac{1}{n\ln n}\) diverges.

Solution

Apply integral test: \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x\ln x}\) is positive, continuous, decreasing on \([2,\infty)\). \(\int_2^\infty\dfrac{dx}{x\ln x}=[\ln(\ln x)]_2^\infty=\infty\). By integral test, the series diverges. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Cauchy Sequence Proof

Show that the sequence \(x_n=1+\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2^n}\) is a Cauchy sequence and find its limit.

Solution

For \(m>n\): \(|x_m-x_n|=\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}+\cdots+\dfrac{1}{2^m}<\dfrac{1}{2^{n+1}}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-1/2}=\dfrac{1}{2^n}\to0\). Cauchy. Limit = \(\sum_{k=0}^\infty 1/2^k = 2\).

PYQ 2008 — Comparison Test

Test \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\) for convergence.

Solution

Since \(\sin(1/n)\sim 1/n\) as \(n\to\infty\) (limit comparison: \(\dfrac{\sin(1/n)}{1/n}\to1>0\)), and \(\sum 1/n\) diverges, by limit comparison test: \(\sum\sin(1/n)\) diverges.

PYQ 2005 — Series Convergence (Root Test)

Test \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\frac{n}{n+1}\right)^{n^2}\) for convergence.

Solution

Root test: \(|a_n|^{1/n}=\left(\dfrac{n}{n+1}\right)^n=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{n+1}\right)^n\to e^{-1}<1\). Series converges absolutely.

PYQ 2019 — Discontinuity Classification

Classify the discontinuity of \(f(x)=\dfrac{\sin x}{|x|}\) at \(x=0\).

Solution
