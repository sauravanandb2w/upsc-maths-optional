§1 · Real Number System

Completeness Axiom (LUB Property)

Every non-empty subset of \(\mathbb{R}\) that is bounded above has a least upper bound (supremum) in \(\mathbb{R}\).

Equivalently: every Cauchy sequence of reals converges in \(\mathbb{R}\).

Archimedean Property

For any \(x \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\exists\, n \in \mathbb{N}\) such that \(n > x\).

Corollary: \(\inf\left\{\dfrac{1}{n} : n \in \mathbb{N}\right\} = 0\).

Density of \(\mathbb{Q}\) in \(\mathbb{R}\)

Between any two distinct reals \(a < b\), there exists a rational \(r\) with \(a < r < b\).

Also: between any two reals there is an irrational.

Sup / Inf Key Facts
  • \(\sup(-A) = -\inf(A)\)
  • If \(s = \sup A\), then \(\forall \varepsilon > 0\), \(\exists\, a \in A\) with \(s - \varepsilon < a \le s\).
  • \(\sup(A+B) = \sup A + \sup B\)

§2 · Sequences

Convergence (ε-N definition)

\(\lim_{n\to\infty} a_n = L\) iff \(\forall\,\varepsilon > 0\), \(\exists\, N \in \mathbb{N}\) such that \(n \ge N \Rightarrow |a_n - L| < \varepsilon\).

Monotone Convergence Theorem

A monotone bounded sequence of reals always converges.

  • Monotone increasing + bounded above → converges to \(\sup\{a_n\}\).
  • Monotone decreasing + bounded below → converges to \(\inf\{a_n\}\).
Bolzano–Weierstrass Theorem ★ exam favourite

Every bounded sequence of real numbers has a convergent subsequence.

Cauchy Sequence

\((a_n)\) is Cauchy iff \(\forall\,\varepsilon > 0\), \(\exists\, N\) s.t. \(m,n \ge N \Rightarrow |a_m - a_n| < \varepsilon\).

Cauchy ⟺ Convergent in \(\mathbb{R}\) (completeness).

Limit Superior & Limit Inferior

\(\limsup_{n\to\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\ge n} a_k\)  |  \(\liminf_{n\to\infty} a_n = \lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{k\ge n} a_k\)

  • \(\liminf \le \limsup\) always.
  • \(\lim a_n\) exists iff \(\liminf = \limsup\), and equals that common value.
  • \(\limsup(a_n + b_n) \le \limsup a_n + \limsup b_n\)
Standard Important Limits
  • \(\lim_{n\to\infty} n^{1/n} = 1\)
  • \(\lim_{n\to\infty} x^{1/n} = 1\) for \(x > 0\)
  • \(\lim_{n\to\infty} \left(1+\dfrac{x}{n}\right)^n = e^x\)
  • \(\lim_{n\to\infty} \dfrac{n!^{1/n}}{n} = \dfrac{1}{e}\)  (Stirling's idea)
  • \(\lim_{n\to\infty} \dfrac{a^n}{n!} = 0\) for any fixed \(a \in \mathbb{R}\)
Sandwich / Squeeze Theorem

If \(a_n \le c_n \le b_n\) and \(\lim a_n = \lim b_n = L\), then \(\lim c_n = L\).

§3 · Series

Convergence of Series

\(\sum a_n\) converges iff the sequence of partial sums \(S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n a_k\) converges.

Necessary condition: \(a_n \to 0\). (But not sufficient — harmonic series!)

Comparison Test

If \(0 \le a_n \le b_n\) eventually:

  • \(\sum b_n\) converges \(\Rightarrow \sum a_n\) converges.
  • \(\sum a_n\) diverges \(\Rightarrow \sum b_n\) diverges.

Limit form: If \(\lim \dfrac{a_n}{b_n} = L \in (0,\infty)\), then both converge or both diverge.

Ratio Test (D'Alembert) ★

Let \(L = \lim_{n\to\infty} \left|\dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|\).

  • \(L < 1\): absolutely convergent.
  • \(L > 1\) (or \(L=\infty\)): divergent.
  • \(L = 1\): inconclusive.
Root Test (Cauchy) ★

Let \(L = \limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n}\).

  • \(L < 1\): absolutely convergent.
  • \(L > 1\): divergent.   \(L=1\): inconclusive.
Raabe's Test when ratio test fails

Let \(R = \lim_{n\to\infty} n\!\left(1 - \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right)\).

  • \(R > 1\): converges.   \(R < 1\): diverges.   \(R = 1\): inconclusive.
Integral Test

If \(f\) is positive, continuous, decreasing on \([1,\infty)\) and \(a_n = f(n)\), then \(\sum a_n\) and \(\int_1^\infty f(x)\,dx\) both converge or both diverge.

p-series: \(\sum \dfrac{1}{n^p}\) converges iff \(p > 1\).

Alternating Series Test (Leibniz) ★

If \((b_n)\) is positive, decreasing, and \(b_n \to 0\), then \(\sum (-1)^{n-1} b_n\) converges.

Error bound: \(|S - S_n| \le b_{n+1}\).

Absolute vs Conditional Convergence
  • Absolutely convergent: \(\sum |a_n| < \infty\). Absolute convergence ⟹ convergence.
  • Conditionally convergent: \(\sum a_n\) converges but \(\sum |a_n|\) diverges.

Riemann Rearrangement: A conditionally convergent series can be rearranged to converge to any real number (or ±∞).

Power Series & Radius of Convergence ★★

\(\sum_{n=0}^\infty a_n (x-c)^n\) converges absolutely for \(|x-c| < R\), diverges for \(|x-c| > R\).

\[R = \frac{1}{\limsup |a_n|^{1/n}} = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}\right|\]

Behaviour at endpoints \(x = c \pm R\) must be checked separately.

Important Series Sums
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty x^n = \frac{1}{1-x}\),   \(|x|<1\)
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{x^n}{n!} = e^x\)
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n} = \ln 2\)
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{2n+1} = \frac{\pi}{4}\)  (Leibniz formula)
  • \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}\)  (Basel problem)

§4 · Limits & Continuity

ε–δ Definition of Limit

\(\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L\) iff \(\forall\,\varepsilon>0\), \(\exists\,\delta>0\) s.t. \(0<|x-a|<\delta \Rightarrow |f(x)-L|<\varepsilon\).

Types of Discontinuity
  • Removable: \(\lim_{x\to a} f(x)\) exists but \(\ne f(a)\) (or \(f(a)\) undefined).
  • Jump (First kind): Both one-sided limits exist but are unequal.
  • Infinite / Oscillatory (Second kind): At least one one-sided limit is \(\pm\infty\) or doesn't exist.
Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) ★★

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0\), then \(\exists\, c \in (a,b)\) with \(f(c) = 0\).

More generally: \(f\) takes every value between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\).

Extreme Value Theorem

If \(f\) is continuous on a closed bounded interval \([a,b]\), then \(f\) attains its maximum and minimum on \([a,b]\).

Uniform Continuity ★★

\(f\) is uniformly continuous on \(D\) iff \(\forall\,\varepsilon>0\), \(\exists\,\delta>0\) s.t. for all \(x,y\in D\): \(|x-y|<\delta \Rightarrow |f(x)-f(y)|<\varepsilon\).

Key theorem: Continuous on a compact (closed bounded) set ⟹ uniformly continuous.

Lipschitz ⟹ Uniform Continuity. \(|f(x)-f(y)|\le K|x-y|\).

Common Pitfall

\(f(x) = x^2\) is continuous on \(\mathbb{R}\) but NOT uniformly continuous on \(\mathbb{R}\).
It IS uniformly continuous on any bounded interval \([a,b]\).

§5 · Differentiability & Mean Value Theorems

Rolle's Theorem ★★★

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), differentiable on \((a,b)\), and \(f(a)=f(b)\), then \(\exists\, c\in(a,b)\) with \(f'(c)=0\).

Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem ★★★

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\), then \(\exists\, c\in(a,b)\) s.t.:

\[f'(c) = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}\]

Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem ★★

If \(f,g\) are continuous on \([a,b]\), differentiable on \((a,b)\), and \(g'(x)\ne 0\) on \((a,b)\), then \(\exists\, c\in(a,b)\) s.t.:

\[\frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)} = \frac{f(b)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(a)}\]

Taking \(g(x)=x\) recovers Lagrange's MVT.

Taylor's Theorem with Remainder ★★★

\[f(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \cdots + \frac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n + R_n(x)\]

Lagrange's form of remainder: \(\displaystyle R_n(x) = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}\) for some \(c\) between \(a\) and \(x\).

Cauchy's form: \(\displaystyle R_n(x) = \frac{(x-c)^n(x-a)}{n!}f^{(n+1)}(c)\).

Standard Maclaurin Series (a=0)
  • \(e^x = 1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \cdots\),   all \(x\)
  • \(\sin x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \dfrac{x^5}{5!} - \cdots\)
  • \(\cos x = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^4}{4!} - \cdots\)
  • \(\ln(1+x) = x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} + \dfrac{x^3}{3} - \cdots\),   \(-1 < x \le 1\)
  • \((1+x)^m = 1 + mx + \dfrac{m(m-1)}{2!}x^2 + \cdots\),   \(|x|<1\)
  • \(\tan^{-1}x = x - \dfrac{x^3}{3} + \dfrac{x^5}{5} - \cdots\),   \(|x|\le 1\)
L'Hôpital's Rule ★★

If \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x) = \lim_{x\to a}g(x) = 0\) (or \(\pm\infty\)) and \(g'(x)\ne 0\) near \(a\), then:

\[\lim_{x\to a}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x\to a}\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}\] (provided the right limit exists).

Differentiability Implies Continuity — NOT vice versa

\(f(x)=|x|\): continuous at 0, not differentiable.   \(f(x) = x\sin(1/x)\): differentiable but \(f'\) not continuous.

§6 · Riemann Integration

Darboux Sums & Riemann Integrability ★★★

For partition \(P\) of \([a,b]\):

\[L(f,P) = \sum m_i \Delta x_i \le \int_a^b f \le \sum M_i \Delta x_i = U(f,P)\]

\(f\) is Riemann integrable iff \(\forall\,\varepsilon>0\), \(\exists\, P\) s.t. \(U(f,P)-L(f,P)<\varepsilon\).

Conditions for Integrability
  • Continuous on \([a,b]\) ⟹ Riemann integrable.
  • Monotone on \([a,b]\) ⟹ Riemann integrable.
  • Bounded with finitely many discontinuities ⟹ Riemann integrable.
  • A function may be integrable with countably many discontinuities (Lebesgue criterion: discontinuity set has measure zero).
Fundamental Theorem of Calculus ★★★

Part I: If \(f\) is integrable on \([a,b]\) and \(F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt\), then \(F\) is continuous. If \(f\) is continuous at \(x_0\), then \(F'(x_0)=f(x_0)\).

Part II: If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(F'=f\), then \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b)-F(a)\).

Mean Value Theorem for Integrals

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), then \(\exists\, c\in(a,b)\) s.t. \(\displaystyle\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = f(c)(b-a)\).

Properties of Riemann Integral
  • Linearity: \(\int(αf+βg) = α\int f + β\int g\)
  • If \(f \le g\), then \(\int_a^b f \le \int_a^b g\)
  • \(\left|\int_a^b f\right| \le \int_a^b |f|\)
  • Additivity: \(\int_a^b f = \int_a^c f + \int_c^b f\)
Improper Integrals — Convergence

\(\displaystyle\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^p}\) converges iff \(p>1\).   \(\displaystyle\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x^p}\) converges iff \(p<1\).

\(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty e^{-ax} dx = \frac{1}{a}\),  \(a>0\).   \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-x^2}dx = \sqrt{\pi}\).

§7 · Uniform Convergence of Sequences/Series of Functions

Pointwise vs Uniform Convergence ★★★

Pointwise: \(f_n \to f\) pointwise if for each \(x\), \(f_n(x)\to f(x)\).

Uniform: \(f_n \to f\) uniformly if \(\sup_x |f_n(x)-f(x)| \to 0\) as \(n\to\infty\).

Uniform ⟹ Pointwise. Converse fails.

Weierstrass M-test ★★★

If \(|f_n(x)| \le M_n\) for all \(x\) and \(\sum M_n < \infty\), then \(\sum f_n\) converges absolutely and uniformly.

Uniform Convergence Preserves Continuity ★★

If each \(f_n\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(f_n \to f\) uniformly, then \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\).

Term-by-Term Integration ★★

If \(f_n \to f\) uniformly on \([a,b]\) and each \(f_n\) is integrable, then:

\[\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_a^b f_n = \int_a^b f = \int_a^b \lim_{n\to\infty} f_n\]

Term-by-Term Differentiation

If \(f_n \to f\) pointwise, \(f_n'\) are continuous, and \(f_n' \to g\) uniformly, then \(f\) is differentiable and \(f' = g\).

Classic Counterexample

\(f_n(x) = x^n\) on \([0,1]\): pointwise limit is \(f(x)=0\) for \(x\in[0,1)\) and \(f(1)=1\) — not continuous. So convergence is NOT uniform (each \(f_n\) is continuous).

§8 · Functions of Bounded Variation

Total Variation ★★

For \(f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}\), the total variation is:

\[V_a^b(f) = \sup_P \sum_{i=1}^n |f(x_i)-f(x_{i-1})|\]

\(f\) is of bounded variation (BV) if \(V_a^b(f) < \infty\).

Key BV Results
  • Monotone functions are BV.   BV functions are bounded.
  • Every BV function is a difference of two monotone increasing functions.
  • BV on \([a,b]\) ⟹ Riemann integrable on \([a,b]\).
  • \(f\) Lipschitz ⟹ \(f\) is BV.
  • BV functions have at most countably many discontinuities (all of jump type).
Not BV: Classic Example

\(f(x) = x\cos(\pi/x)\) for \(x\in(0,1]\), \(f(0)=0\) is continuous but not BV on \([0,1]\).

§9 · Metric Spaces

Metric Space Axioms

A metric \(d: X\times X \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}\) satisfies:

  • \(d(x,y) = 0 \Leftrightarrow x=y\)  (identity)
  • \(d(x,y) = d(y,x)\)  (symmetry)
  • \(d(x,z) \le d(x,y)+d(y,z)\)  (triangle inequality)
Open & Closed Sets

Open ball: \(B(x,r) = \{y \in X : d(x,y) < r\}\).

Open set: Every point is an interior point (contains an open ball around it).

Closed set: Contains all its limit points. Complement of an open set.

Complete Metric Space ★★

A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence converges in \(X\).

\(\mathbb{R}^n\) with Euclidean metric is complete. \(\mathbb{Q}\) is not.

Banach Fixed Point Theorem (Contraction Mapping) ★★★

If \((X,d)\) is complete and \(T:X\to X\) satisfies \(d(Tx,Ty) \le k\,d(x,y)\) for some \(k\in[0,1)\) (contraction), then \(T\) has a unique fixed point \(x^*\), and the iterates \(T^n x_0 \to x^*\) for any starting point.

Error estimate: \(d(T^n x_0, x^*) \le \dfrac{k^n}{1-k}\,d(x_0, Tx_0)\).

Compactness in Metric Spaces ★★

A subset \(K\) of a metric space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover.

In \(\mathbb{R}^n\): Heine–Borel: \(K\) is compact iff \(K\) is closed and bounded.

Compact ⟹ complete and totally bounded.

Connectedness

A metric space is connected if it cannot be partitioned into two disjoint non-empty open sets.

In \(\mathbb{R}\): connected subsets are exactly the intervals.

Continuous image of a connected set is connected.

Quick Hierarchy to Remember

Lipschitz ⟹ Uniformly Continuous ⟹ Continuous  |  Differentiable ⟹ Continuous  |  Absolutely Convergent ⟹ Convergent  |  Uniform Convergence ⟹ Pointwise Convergence
