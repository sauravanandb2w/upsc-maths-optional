Taylor at \(x=a\)

\[f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k + R_n, \qquad R_n = \frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}.\]

Maclaurin = Taylor at \(a=0\). Coefficient of \(x^n\): \(f^{(n)}(0)/n!\).

§2 · Euler’s identity (\(e^{i\pi}+1=0\))

Videos

1 · Taylor series proof — plug Maclaurin series for \(e^x\), \(\cos x\), \(\sin x\); compare real and imaginary parts → \(e^{ix}=\cos x+i\sin x\), then \(x=\pi\).

▶ Taylor proof

2 · Intuition (Mathologer) — e to the pi i for dummies: geometric / conceptual path to \(e^{i\pi}=-1\) without getting lost in symbols.

▶ Mathologer — \(e^{\pi i}\)
Steps (Taylor proof — video 1)
  1. \(e^x = 1 + x + \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \cdots\)
  2. Replace \(x\) by \(ix\): \(e^{ix} = 1 + ix - \dfrac{x^2}{2!} - i\dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \dfrac{x^4}{4!} + \cdots\)
  3. Group real / imaginary: \(\mathrm{Re}(e^{ix}) = 1 - \dfrac{x^2}{2!} + \dfrac{x^4}{4!} - \cdots = \cos x\)
  4. \(\mathrm{Im}(e^{ix}) = x - \dfrac{x^3}{3!} + \dfrac{x^5}{5!} - \cdots = \sin x\)
  5. Euler’s formula: \(e^{ix} = \cos x + i\sin x\)
  6. At \(x=\pi\): \(e^{i\pi} = \cos\pi + i\sin\pi = -1\) ⇒ \(e^{i\pi}+1=0\)
Useful corollaries (optional / complex analysis)
  • \(e^{x+iy} = e^x(\cos y + i\sin y)\)
  • \(\cos x = \dfrac{e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2}\),   \(\sin x = \dfrac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}\)
  • De Moivre: \((\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)^n = \cos n\theta + i\sin n\theta\)
