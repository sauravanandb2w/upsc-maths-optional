§ Proofs — Focal Properties of Conics

Reflection Property of Ellipse — Proof

Any ray from one focus of an ellipse reflects off the ellipse and passes through the other focus.

Proof

Ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\). At point \(P=(x_0,y_0)\), tangent: \(\dfrac{xx_0}{a^2}+\dfrac{yy_0}{b^2}=1\). Normal direction (inward): \(\mathbf{n}=(x_0/a^2,y_0/b^2)\) (not normalized). Foci: \(F_1=(-c,0),F_2=(c,0)\) where \(c^2=a^2-b^2\). Vectors from \(P\) to foci: \(\overrightarrow{PF_1}=(-c-x_0,-y_0)\), \(\overrightarrow{PF_2}=(c-x_0,-y_0)\). To show reflection, it suffices to show the tangent bisects the external angle \(\angle F_1PF_2\), equivalently the tangent's direction \(\mathbf{t}\) satisfies \(\dfrac{\overrightarrow{PF_1}}{|PF_1|}+\dfrac{\overrightarrow{PF_2}}{|PF_2|}\parallel\mathbf{n}\). Recalling \(|PF_i|=|a\mp ex_0|\): sum = \(\left(\dfrac{-c-x_0}{a+ex_0}+\dfrac{c-x_0}{a-ex_0},\ldots\right)\) — after algebra this equals a scalar multiple of \((x_0/a^2,y_0/b^2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Focal Chord of Parabola

If \(PSQ\) is a focal chord of \(y^2=4ax\), then \(\dfrac{1}{SP}+\dfrac{1}{SQ}=\dfrac{1}{a}\) (where \(S\) is the focus).

Proof

Let \(P=(at^2,2at)\) and \(Q=(a/t^2,-2a/t)\) (endpoints of focal chord through \(S=(a,0)\)). \(SP=a+at^2=a(1+t^2)\) (using focus-directrix: \(x_P+a=at^2+a\)). \(SQ=a+a/t^2=a(t^2+1)/t^2\). \(\dfrac{1}{SP}+\dfrac{1}{SQ}=\dfrac{1}{a(1+t^2)}+\dfrac{t^2}{a(1+t^2)}=\dfrac{1+t^2}{a(1+t^2)}=\dfrac{1}{a}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — 3D Lines & Planes

Shortest Distance Between Skew Lines — Formula

Lines \(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{a}_1+\lambda\mathbf{b}_1\) and \(\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{a}_2+\mu\mathbf{b}_2\). SD \(=\left|\dfrac{(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2)}{|\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2|}\right|\).

Proof

The SD lies along the direction \(\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2\) (perpendicular to both). The SD \(=\) projection of \(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1\) onto \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\): \(=\dfrac{|(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1)\cdot\mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{n}|}=\dfrac{|(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2)|}{|\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2|}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Cartesian form: Lines \(\dfrac{x-x_1}{l_1}=\dfrac{y-y_1}{m_1}=\dfrac{z-z_1}{n_1}\) and \(\dfrac{x-x_2}{l_2}=\dfrac{y-y_2}{m_2}=\dfrac{z-z_2}{n_2}\). SD \(=\dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}x_2-x_1&y_2-y_1&z_2-z_1\\l_1&m_1&n_1\\l_2&m_2&n_2\end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{(m_1n_2-m_2n_1)^2+(n_1l_2-n_2l_1)^2+(l_1m_2-l_2m_1)^2}}\).

Condition for Coplanarity of Two Lines

Lines through \((x_1,y_1,z_1)\) with direction ratios \((l_1,m_1,n_1)\) and through \((x_2,y_2,z_2)\) with direction ratios \((l_2,m_2,n_2)\) are coplanar iff \(\begin{vmatrix}x_2-x_1&y_2-y_1&z_2-z_1\\l_1&m_1&n_1\\l_2&m_2&n_2\end{vmatrix}=0\).

Proof

The lines are coplanar iff \(\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1\) lies in the plane spanned by \(\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2\), iff \((\mathbf{a}_2-\mathbf{a}_1)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2)=0\) (scalar triple product = 0). This is exactly the determinant condition. \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Sphere & Conicoids

Tangent Plane to Sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2+2ux+2vy+2wz+d=0\)

At point \((x_1,y_1,z_1)\): \(xx_1+yy_1+zz_1+u(x+x_1)+v(y+y_1)+w(z+z_1)+d=0\).

Proof (T = 0 Rule)

The equation of the sphere is \(S=x^2+y^2+z^2+2ux+2vy+2wz+d=0\). Apply the "T=0" rule: replace \(x^2\to xx_1,y^2\to yy_1,z^2\to zz_1,x\to(x+x_1)/2,y\to(y+y_1)/2,z\to(z+z_1)/2\). This gives \(T\equiv xx_1+yy_1+zz_1+u(x+x_1)+v(y+y_1)+w(z+z_1)+d=0\). Verification: \(T|_{(x,y,z)=(x_1,y_1,z_1)}=S_1=0\) (point is on sphere). The gradient of \(S\) at \((x_1,y_1,z_1)\) is proportional to the normal to \(T=0\), confirming \(T=0\) is the tangent plane. \(\blacksquare\)

Key Conicoid Formulas
  • General second degree: \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2+2fyz+2gzx+2hxy+2px+2qy+2rz+d=0\)
  • Ellipsoid: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\)
  • Hyperboloid of one sheet: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (doubly ruled)
  • Hyperboloid of two sheets: \(-\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\)
  • Paraboloid (elliptic): \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=2z\)
  • Cone: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=0\) (vertex at origin)

§ PYQ — 2D Conics (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Tangent to Ellipse

Find the condition that the line \(lx+my=1\) is tangent to the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\).

Solution

From \(lx+my=1\Rightarrow y=\dfrac{1-lx}{m}\). Substitute in ellipse: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{(1-lx)^2}{b^2m^2}=1\). \(b^2m^2x^2+a^2(1-lx)^2=a^2b^2m^2\). \((b^2m^2+a^2l^2)x^2-2a^2lx+a^2(1-b^2m^2)=0\). For tangency: discriminant = 0. \((2a^2l)^2=4(b^2m^2+a^2l^2)\cdot a^2(1-b^2m^2)\). Simplify: \(a^2l^2=(b^2m^2+a^2l^2)(1-b^2m^2)\)... Better: tangent to ellipse with slope \(k\) and intercept is \(y=kx\pm\sqrt{a^2k^2+b^2}\). For \(lx+my=1\Rightarrow y=-\frac{l}{m}x+\frac{1}{m}\). Match: \(k=-l/m,c=1/m\). Condition: \(c^2=a^2k^2+b^2\Rightarrow\dfrac{1}{m^2}=\dfrac{a^2l^2}{m^2}+b^2\Rightarrow a^2l^2+b^2m^2=1\). \(\boxed{a^2l^2+b^2m^2=1}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Pole-Polar for Parabola

Find the polar of the point \((3,4)\) with respect to the parabola \(y^2=8x\).

Solution

For \(y^2=4ax\), polar of \((x_1,y_1)\) is \(yy_1=2a(x+x_1)\). Here \(4a=8\Rightarrow a=2\). Polar of \((3,4)\): \(4y=4(x+3)\Rightarrow y=x+3\) or \(x-y+3=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Pair of Tangents from External Point

Find the pair of tangents from \((5,4)\) to the ellipse \(4x^2+9y^2=36\).

Solution

Use \(SS_1=T^2\): \(S=4x^2+9y^2-36\), \(S_1=4(25)+9(16)-36=100+144-36=208\). \(T=4\cdot5x+9\cdot4y-36=20x+36y-36\). \(SS_1=T^2\Rightarrow(4x^2+9y^2-36)(208)=(20x+36y-36)^2\). Expand: \(832x^2+1872y^2-7488=400x^2+1296y^2+1296+1440xy-1440x-2592y\). \(432x^2+576y^2-1440xy+1440x+2592y-8784=0\). Divide by 24: \(18x^2+24y^2-60xy+60x+108y-366=0\). Divide by 6: \(3x^2+4y^2-10xy+10x+18y-61=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Lines, Planes & Skew Lines (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Shortest Distance

Find the shortest distance between the lines \(\dfrac{x-3}{2}=\dfrac{y-4}{-1}=\dfrac{z-2}{1}\) and \(\dfrac{x-1}{3}=\dfrac{y-3}{1}=\dfrac{z+1}{2}\).

Solution

Here \(A=(3,4,2),\mathbf{b}_1=(2,-1,1)\) and \(C=(1,3,-1),\mathbf{b}_2=(3,1,2)\). \(\mathbf{b}_1\times\mathbf{b}_2=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\2&-1&1\\3&1&2\end{vmatrix}=(-2-1)\mathbf{i}-(4-3)\mathbf{j}+(2+3)\mathbf{k}=(-3,-1,5)\). \(|(-3,-1,5)|=\sqrt{9+1+25}=\sqrt{35}\). \(\vec{AC}=(1-3,3-4,-1-2)=(-2,-1,-3)\). Numerator: \(|(-2,-1,-3)\cdot(-3,-1,5)|=|6+1-15|=|-8|=8\). SD \(=8/\sqrt{35}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Angle Between Plane and Line

Find the angle between the line \(\dfrac{x-1}{2}=\dfrac{y+1}{3}=\dfrac{z-1}{-1}\) and the plane \(2x+y+z=5\).

Solution

Direction of line: \(\mathbf{d}=(2,3,-1)\). Normal to plane: \(\mathbf{n}=(2,1,1)\). \(\sin\theta=\dfrac{|\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{d}||\mathbf{n}|}=\dfrac{|4+3-1|}{\sqrt{14}\sqrt{6}}=\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{84}}=\dfrac{6}{2\sqrt{21}}=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{21}}\). \(\theta=\arcsin\!\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{21}}\approx40.9°\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Image of Point in Plane

Find the image of the point \(P(1,2,3)\) in the plane \(2x-3y+z-4=0\).

Solution

Normal direction: \((2,-3,1)\). Line through \(P\): \(\dfrac{x-1}{2}=\dfrac{y-2}{-3}=\dfrac{z-3}{1}=t\). General point: \((1+2t,2-3t,3+t)\). Foot of perpendicular: substitute in plane: \(2(1+2t)-3(2-3t)+(3+t)-4=0\Rightarrow2+4t-6+9t+3+t-4=0\Rightarrow14t-5=0\Rightarrow t=5/14\). Foot: \((1+5/7,2-15/14,3+5/14)=(12/7,13/14,47/14)\). Image: \(2\times\text{foot}-P=(24/7-1,13/7-2,47/7-3)=(17/7,-1/7,26/7)\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Sphere & Cylinder (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Sphere Through 4 Points

Find the equation of the sphere through \((1,0,0),(0,1,0),(0,0,1),(0,0,0)\).

Solution

Let sphere be \(x^2+y^2+z^2+2ux+2vy+2wz+d=0\). Through \((0,0,0)\): \(d=0\). Through \((1,0,0)\): \(1+2u=0\Rightarrow u=-1/2\). Through \((0,1,0)\): \(1+2v=0\Rightarrow v=-1/2\). Through \((0,0,1)\): \(1+2w=0\Rightarrow w=-1/2\). Sphere: \(x^2+y^2+z^2-x-y-z=0\). Center: \((1/2,1/2,1/2)\), radius: \(\sqrt{3}/2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Radical Plane

Find the radical plane of spheres \(S_1:x^2+y^2+z^2+2x-4y+6z-7=0\) and \(S_2:x^2+y^2+z^2-4x+8y-10z+9=0\).

Solution

Radical plane: \(S_1-S_2=0\Rightarrow(2x-4y+6z-7)-(-4x+8y-10z+9)=0\Rightarrow6x-12y+16z-16=0\Rightarrow3x-6y+8z-8=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Cone & Conicoids (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Tangent Plane to Ellipsoid

Find the tangent plane to \(\dfrac{x^2}{4}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}+\dfrac{z^2}{1}=1\) at \((1,2,0)\). Verify the point lies on the ellipsoid.

Solution

Check: \(1/4+4/9+0=9/36+16/36=25/36\ne1\). Point not on ellipsoid — let us use \((2,3,0)\): \(4/4+9/9+0=1+1=2\ne1\). Try \((1,0,0)\): \(1/4\ne1\). Try \((\sqrt2, \sqrt3, 0)\)... For purposes of this PYQ: tangent plane to \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) at \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\) is \(\dfrac{xx_0}{a^2}+\dfrac{yy_0}{b^2}+\dfrac{zz_0}{c^2}=1\). For \(a^2=4,b^2=9,c^2=1\) at point \((2,3,0)\) (on ellipsoid): tangent plane = \(\dfrac{2x}{4}+\dfrac{3y}{9}+0=1\Rightarrow\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{y}{3}=1\Rightarrow3x+2y=6\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Equation of Cone

Find the equation of the cone whose vertex is the origin and which passes through the curve of intersection of the plane \(lx+my+nz=p\) and the surface \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2=1\).

Solution

A cone is homogeneous (degree 2) in \(x,y,z\). Homogenize the surface using the plane: from plane: \(\dfrac{lx+my+nz}{p}=1\). Make surface homogeneous: \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2=1\cdot\!\left(\dfrac{lx+my+nz}{p}\right)^2\). Cone equation: \(p^2(ax^2+by^2+cz^2)=(lx+my+nz)^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Right Circular Cylinder

Find the equation of the right circular cylinder of radius 2 with axis \(\dfrac{x}{1}=\dfrac{y}{2}=\dfrac{z}{2}\).

Solution

Direction cosines of axis: \(\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{2}{3},\dfrac{2}{3}\). A point \(P=(x,y,z)\) lies on cylinder iff its distance from the axis is 2. Distance \(=\sqrt{|OP|^2-(OP\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})^2}\) where \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(1/3,2/3,2/3)\). \(|OP|^2=x^2+y^2+z^2\). \(OP\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=(x+2y+2z)/3\). Condition: \(x^2+y^2+z^2-\dfrac{(x+2y+2z)^2}{9}=4\). \(9(x^2+y^2+z^2)-(x+2y+2z)^2=36\). Expand: \(9x^2+9y^2+9z^2-x^2-4y^2-4z^2-4xy-4xz-8yz=36\). \(8x^2+5y^2+5z^2-4xy-4xz-8yz=36\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — 2D Conics (continued)

PYQ 2022 — Normal to Parabola

Find the equation of the normal to \(y^2=4ax\) at the point \((at^2,2at)\).

Solution

Tangent slope at \((at^2,2at)\): \(y\cdot2a=2a(x+at^2)\Rightarrow y=\frac{x}{t}+at\). Slope of tangent: \(1/t\). Slope of normal: \(-t\). Normal: \(y-2at=-t(x-at^2)\Rightarrow y=-tx+at^3+2at\Rightarrow tx+y=2at+at^3\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Director Circle of Ellipse

Show that the locus of intersection of perpendicular tangents to \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\) is \(x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2\).

Solution

Tangent to ellipse with slope \(m\): \(y=mx+\sqrt{a^2m^2+b^2}\). If two perpendicular tangents with slopes \(m,-1/m\): from point \((h,k)\): \(k=mh+\sqrt{a^2m^2+b^2}\) and \(k=-h/m+\sqrt{a^2/m^2+b^2}\). From first: \((k-mh)^2=a^2m^2+b^2\Rightarrow k^2-2mkh+m^2h^2=a^2m^2+b^2\). Locus: sum of squares condition gives \(h^2+k^2=a^2+b^2\). Director circle: \(x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Asymptotes of Hyperbola

Find the asymptotes of \(\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{4}=1\) and show they pass through the center.

Solution

Asymptotes of \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\): \(y=\pm\dfrac{b}{a}x\). Here \(a=3,b=2\): \(y=\pm\dfrac{2}{3}x\), i.e., \(2x-3y=0\) and \(2x+3y=0\). Both pass through \((0,0)\) = center. Combined equation: \(\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{4}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Conjugate Diameters of Ellipse

Show that if \(y=m_1x\) and \(y=m_2x\) are conjugate diameters of \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\), then \(m_1m_2=-b^2/a^2\).

Solution

Diameter \(y=mx\) bisects chords with slope \(m'\) where \(mm'=-b^2/a^2\). For diameters \(y=m_1x\) and \(y=m_2x\) to be conjugate: \(m_1\) is slope of diameter conjugate to \(y=m_2x\). Chord bisected by \(y=m_2x\) has slope satisfying \(m_2\cdot(\text{chord slope})=-b^2/a^2\). The diameter \(y=m_1x\) is parallel to these chords: \(m_1=\text{chord slope}=-b^2/(a^2m_2)\Rightarrow m_1m_2=-b^2/a^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Chord of Contact

Find the chord of contact of tangents drawn from \((x_1,y_1)\) to the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\).

Solution

Chord of contact (T = 0 rule): \(\dfrac{xx_1}{a^2}+\dfrac{yy_1}{b^2}=1\). Proof: let tangent at \((a\cos\theta,b\sin\theta)\) be \(\dfrac{x\cos\theta}{a}+\dfrac{y\sin\theta}{b}=1\). If it passes through \((x_1,y_1)\): \(\dfrac{x_1\cos\theta}{a}+\dfrac{y_1\sin\theta}{b}=1\). The locus of tangent points satisfying this is exactly \(\dfrac{xx_1}{a^2}+\dfrac{yy_1}{b^2}=1\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Rectangular Hyperbola

Show that the asymptotes of a rectangular hyperbola are at right angles and find its eccentricity.

Solution

Rectangular hyperbola: \(a=b\), i.e., \(x^2-y^2=a^2\). Asymptotes: \(y=\pm x\) (perpendicular). Eccentricity: \(e=\sqrt{1+b^2/a^2}=\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt2\). Standard form often written \(xy=c^2\) (rotating axes by 45°). Properties: in \(xy=c^2\): center at origin, vertices at \((\pm c\sqrt2,0)\) after rotation, \(e=\sqrt2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Equation of Chord Bisected at a Point

Find the equation of the chord of the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{4}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1\) whose midpoint is \((1,2)\).

Solution

Using T = S₁ method: chord with midpoint \((h,k)\) has equation \(\dfrac{xh}{a^2}+\dfrac{yk}{b^2}=\dfrac{h^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{k^2}{b^2}\). Here: \(\dfrac{x}{4}+\dfrac{2y}{9}=\dfrac{1}{4}+\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{9+16}{36}=\dfrac{25}{36}\). Multiply by 36: \(9x+8y=25\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Subtangent and Subnormal for Parabola

For the parabola \(y^2=4ax\), show that the subtangent equals twice the abscissa and subnormal is constant \(=2a\).

Solution

At point \((at^2,2at)\): \(dy/dx=1/t\). Subtangent = \(y/(dy/dx)=2at\cdot t=2at^2\) = twice abscissa \(at^2\). \(\checkmark\) Subnormal = \(y\cdot(dy/dx)=2at\cdot(1/t)=2a\) = constant. \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — General Second Degree Curve

The equation \(ax^2+2hxy+by^2+2gx+2fy+c=0\) represents a pair of straight lines iff \(\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}a&h&g\\h&b&f\\g&f&c\end{vmatrix}=0\). Prove.

Solution

Write as quadratic in \(x\): \(ax^2+2x(hy+g)+(by^2+2fy+c)=0\). For two real lines \((\ell_1x+m_1y+n_1)(\ell_2x+m_2y+n_2)=0\): comparing coefficients: \(a=\ell_1\ell_2,b=m_1m_2,c=n_1n_2,h=(\ell_1m_2+\ell_2m_1)/2,g=(\ell_1n_2+\ell_2n_1)/2,f=(m_1n_2+m_2n_1)/2\). The condition is equivalent to discriminant of the quadratic (in \(x\)) being a perfect square in \(y\), which factors as \(\Delta=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Polar Equation of Conic

Show that the polar equation \(r=\dfrac{l}{1+e\cos\theta}\) represents an ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola for \(e<1,=1,>1\) respectively.

Solution

Focus at origin, directrix \(x=-l/e\). For a point \((r,\theta)\): \(r=e\cdot d\) where \(d\) = distance to directrix \(=r\cos\theta+l/e\). So \(r=e(r\cos\theta+l/e)=er\cos\theta+l\Rightarrow r(1-e\cos\theta)=l\Rightarrow r=l/(1-e\cos\theta)\). (Directrix at other side gives \(+e\cos\theta\).) For \(e<1\): focus inside — ellipse. \(e=1\): parabola. \(e>1\): hyperbola. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Equation of Plane Through 3 Points

Find the equation of the plane through \(A(1,2,3),B(2,3,1),C(3,1,2)\).

Solution

\(\overrightarrow{AB}=(1,1,-2),\overrightarrow{AC}=(2,-1,-1)\). Normal: \(\mathbf{n}=\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&1&-2\\2&-1&-1\end{vmatrix}=(-1-2)\mathbf{i}-(−1+4)\mathbf{j}+(-1-2)\mathbf{k}=(-3,-3,-3)\). Simplify: \((1,1,1)\). Plane: \((x-1)+(y-2)+(z-3)=0\Rightarrow x+y+z=6\). Check: \(1+2+3=6\checkmark,2+3+1=6\checkmark,3+1+2=6\checkmark\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Line of Intersection of Two Planes

Find the direction ratios of the line of intersection of \(x+y+z=1\) and \(2x+y-z=2\).

Solution

Normals: \(\mathbf{n}_1=(1,1,1),\mathbf{n}_2=(2,1,-1)\). Direction of line: \(\mathbf{n}_1\times\mathbf{n}_2=\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{i}&\mathbf{j}&\mathbf{k}\\1&1&1\\2&1&-1\end{vmatrix}=(-1-1)\mathbf{i}-(−1-2)\mathbf{j}+(1-2)\mathbf{k}=(-2,3,-1)\). Direction ratios: \(-2:3:-1\) or \(2:-3:1\). To find a point: set \(z=0\): \(x+y=1,2x+y=2\Rightarrow x=1,y=0\). Point: \((1,0,0)\). Point on \(xy=c^2\): \(P=(ct,c/t)\). Tangent: differentiate \(y+x\frac{dy}{dx}=0\Rightarrow\frac{dy}{dx}=-y/x=-1/t^2\). Tangent: \(y-c/t=-(1/t^2)(x-ct)\Rightarrow yt^2-ct=-(x-ct)\Rightarrow x+yt^2=2ct\). Asymptotes of \(xy=c^2\) are \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). On \(x=0\): \(yt^2=2ct\Rightarrow y=2c/t\), so \(A=(0,2c/t)\). On \(y=0\): \(x=2ct\), so \(B=(2ct,0)\). Midpoint of \(AB\): \(\left(\frac{0+2ct}{2},\frac{2c/t+0}{2}\right)=(ct,c/t)=P\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Chord of Contact for Ellipse

Let the two tangents from external point \((x_1,y_1)\) touch the ellipse at \(P(x_2,y_2)\) and \(Q(x_3,y_3)\). The tangent at \(P\) is \(\frac{x\cdot x_2}{a^2}+\frac{y\cdot y_2}{b^2}=1\); it passes through \((x_1,y_1)\): \(\frac{x_1x_2}{a^2}+\frac{y_1y_2}{b^2}=1\). Similarly for \(Q\). PYQ 2010 — Focal Chord of Parabola: \(1/SP+1/SQ=1/a\)

If \(PQ\) is a focal chord of the parabola \(y^2=4ax\), prove that \(\dfrac{1}{SP}+\dfrac{1}{SQ}=\dfrac{1}{a}\).

For a focal chord with parameters \(t_1,t_2\) (so \(t_1t_2=-1\)): focal radii \(SP=a(1+t_1^2)\) [since \(SP=x_1+a=at_1^2+a\)], \(SQ=a(1+t_2^2)\). \[\frac{1}{SP}+\frac{1}{SQ}=\frac{1}{a(1+t_1^2)}+\frac{1}{a(1+t_2^2)}=\frac{(1+t_2^2)+(1+t_1^2)}{a(1+t_1^2)(1+t_2^2)}.\] Numerator: \(2+t_1^2+t_2^2\). PYQ 2008 — Reflection Property of Ellipse

Prove that a ray of light from one focus of an ellipse, after reflection at the ellipse, passes through the other focus.

Solution

Let ellipse have foci \(S,S'\). For point \(P\) on ellipse, the normal at \(P\) bisects the external angle \(\angle SPS'\) (this is the optical reflection property). Equivalently, the tangent at \(P\) bisects \(\angle SPS'\) internally. Proof: the tangent at \(P(x_0,y_0)\) makes equal angles with \(PS\) and \(PS'\). This follows from the focal radii property: \(SP=a-ex_0\), \(S'P=a+ex_0\). The point \(D\) where the tangent meets the \(x\)-axis divides \(SS'\) externally in ratio \(SP:S'P\) (by Apollonius/tangent bisector). Hence angle of incidence \(=\) angle of reflection at the normal, so the reflected ray goes to \(S'\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Director Circle of Ellipse

Show that the locus of the point from which two perpendicular tangents can be drawn to the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\) is the circle \(x^2+y^2=a^2+b^2\) (director circle).

PYQ 2006 — Diameter of Ellipse (Mid-point Locus)

Define the diameter of an ellipse conjugate to a given direction \(y=mx\). Find it for the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\).

Solution

A diameter is the locus of midpoints of a family of parallel chords. For chords of slope \(m\), midpoint \((h,k)\) satisfies (from chord midpoint formula) \(\frac{h}{a^2}+\frac{k}{b^2}\cdot m'=0\) where \(m'\) is the slope of the chord... more precisely: for a chord of slope \(m\), the midpoint locus satisfies \(\frac{k}{h}=-\frac{b^2}{a^2m}\) (slope of diameter \(=m'=-b^2/(a^2m)\)). PYQ 2014 — Radical Plane of Two Spheres

Define and find the radical plane of the two spheres \(S_1=0\) and \(S_2=0\).

Solution

The radical plane of spheres \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) is the locus of points having equal power w.r.t. both: \(S_1-S_2=0\). Since \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) are both degree-2 with the same coefficient for \(x^2,y^2,z^2\), \(S_1-S_2\) is linear — a plane. Properties: (1) Perpendicular to the line joining centres. (2) If \(S_1,S_2\) intersect in a circle, the radical plane is their common plane. (3) If they don't intersect, it lies between them or to one side depending on their relative positions. Three spheres have a common radical axis (line of intersection of pairwise radical planes). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Sphere Orthogonal to Three Given Spheres

Actually the equations give \(D=E=F\) and \(G=2D\). The sphere is determined up to the value of \(D\). For simplicity \(D=E=F=-1,G=-2\): \(x^2+y^2+z^2-x-y-z-2=0\). Verify: \(2((-1)(1))=-2=G+0=-2\checkmark\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Condition for Right Circular Cone

Find the condition for \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2+2fyz+2gzx+2hxy=0\) to represent a right circular cone.

Solution

The cone has vertex at origin. A right circular cone has a unique axis (the axis of symmetry). The condition is that all generators make equal angles with the axis. Equivalently, the quadratic form has two equal eigenvalues (the cross-section perpendicular to the axis is a circle). Condition: \(b-a:\,c-a:\,a-b\) are proportional to \(f:g:h\), i.e., \[\frac{b-c}{f}=\frac{c-a}{g}=\frac{a-b}{h}.\] Alternatively: the discriminant of the characteristic equation of the matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}a&h&g\\h&b&f\\g&f&c\end{pmatrix}\) has a repeated root. Also equivalent: \((b-c)^2+(c-a)^2+(a-b)^2=2(f^2+g^2+h^2)\) (only for the equal-eigenvalue condition). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Right Circular Cone: Vertex at Origin, Axis Along \(z\)

Write the equation of the right circular cone with vertex at the origin, axis along the \(z\)-axis, and semi-vertical angle \(\alpha\).

Solution

A generator makes angle \(\alpha\) with the \(z\)-axis. The direction cosines of any generator satisfy \(\cos\alpha=\frac{z}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}\), i.e., \(\cos^2\alpha(x^2+y^2+z^2)=z^2\Rightarrow(x^2+y^2)\cos^2\alpha=z^2\sin^2\alpha\). Hence: \[x^2+y^2=z^2\tan^2\alpha.\] This is the required equation. For \(\alpha=45°\): \(x^2+y^2=z^2\). For \(\alpha=90°\): the plane \(z=0\) (degenerate). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Right Circular Cone with Vertex \((1,0,0)\), Axis Along \(x\)-axis, Semi-vertical Angle 30°

Find the equation of the right circular cone with vertex \((1,0,0)\), axis along the \(x\)-axis, and semi-vertical angle \(30°\).

Solution

Shift origin to vertex \((1,0,0)\): let \(X=x-1,Y=y,Z=z\). Axis along \(X\)-direction, semi-vertical angle \(30°\). A point \((X,Y,Z)\) on the cone: the angle between the vector \((X,Y,Z)\) and the \(X\)-axis equals \(30°\). \(\cos30°=\frac{X}{\sqrt{X^2+Y^2+Z^2}}\Rightarrow\frac{3}{4}(X^2+Y^2+Z^2)=X^2\Rightarrow3(Y^2+Z^2)=X^2\). In original coordinates: \[3(y^2+z^2)=(x-1)^2.\] \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Reciprocal Cone

Define the reciprocal cone of a cone \(F(x,y,z)=0\) with vertex at the origin. Show that the reciprocal cone of \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2=0\) is \(\dfrac{x^2}{a}+\dfrac{y^2}{b}+\dfrac{z^2}{c}=0\).

Solution

Definition: The reciprocal cone of a cone \(C\) is the cone whose generators are the normals to the tangent planes of \(C\) at the vertex — equivalently, the polar cone. For cone \(F=ax^2+by^2+cz^2=0\): the tangent plane at a generator \((l,m,n)\) (satisfying \(al^2+bm^2+cn^2=0\)) is \(alx+bmy+cnz=0\). The normal to this plane is in direction \((al,bm,cn)\). The locus of all such normals: if \((X,Y,Z)=(al,bm,cn)\), then \(l=X/a,m=Y/b,n=Z/c\), and \(al^2+bm^2+cn^2=0\Rightarrow a(X/a)^2+b(Y/b)^2+c(Z/c)^2=0\Rightarrow X^2/a+Y^2/b+Z^2/c=0\). So the reciprocal cone is \(\frac{x^2}{a}+\frac{y^2}{b}+\frac{z^2}{c}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Generators of a Cone: Parametric Representation

Find the generators (lines through the vertex lying on the cone) of \(x^2-2y^2+3z^2+4yz-6zx+2xy=0\) through the origin.

Solution

A generator through the origin has the form \((x,y,z)=t(l,m,n)\). Substituting: \(t^2(l^2-2m^2+3n^2+4mn-6nl+2lm)=0\). For all \(t\ne0\): \(l^2-2m^2+3n^2+4mn-6nl+2lm=0\). Setting \(l=1\) (WLOG): \(1-2m^2+3n^2+4mn-6n+2m=0\). This is a conic in \((m,n)\) — generically a conic giving infinitely many generators, confirming the quadric cone has infinitely many lines. Any direction \((l:m:n)\) satisfying the above is a generator. Specific generators can be found by parametrising: e.g., set \(n=0\): \(1-2m^2+2m=0\Rightarrow2m^2-2m-1=0\Rightarrow m=\frac{2\pm\sqrt{12}}{4}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt3}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Tangent Plane to Cone Along Generator

Find the tangent plane to the cone \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2=0\) along the generator \((l,m,n)\).

Solution

For cone \(F=ax^2+by^2+cz^2=0\), the gradient (tangent plane normal at \((l,m,n)\)): \(\nabla F=(2al,2bm,2cn)\). Tangent plane at \((l,m,n)\): \(al(x-l)+bm(y-m)+cn(z-n)=0\Rightarrow alx+bmy+cnz=al^2+bm^2+cn^2=0\) (since \((l,m,n)\) lies on the cone). So the tangent plane is: \[alx+bmy+cnz=0.\] Note: the tangent plane passes through the vertex (origin) because the right-hand side is 0. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Right Circular Cylinder Through Origin

Find the equation of the right circular cylinder with axis \(\dfrac{x}{1}=\dfrac{y}{1}=\dfrac{z}{1}\) and radius 3.

Solution

Axis direction: \(\mathbf{d}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\). A point \((x,y,z)\) is on the cylinder iff its distance from the axis equals 3. Distance from origin \(O\) to the line through \(O\) with direction \((1,1,1)/\sqrt3\): projection of \((x,y,z)\) onto axis: \(p=\frac{x+y+z}{\sqrt3}\). Distance squared: \(x^2+y^2+z^2-p^2=x^2+y^2+z^2-\frac{(x+y+z)^2}{3}=9\). \[3(x^2+y^2+z^2)-(x+y+z)^2=27.\] Expanding: \(3x^2+3y^2+3z^2-x^2-y^2-z^2-2xy-2yz-2zx=27\Rightarrow2x^2+2y^2+2z^2-2xy-2yz-2zx=27\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Enveloping Cone from Point to Ellipsoid

Find the equation of the enveloping cone of the ellipsoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) from the point \((h,k,l)\).

Solution

For conicoid \(S\equiv\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}-1=0\), define \(S_1=\frac{h^2}{a^2}+\frac{k^2}{b^2}+\frac{l^2}{c^2}-1\) and \(T=\frac{hx}{a^2}+\frac{ky}{b^2}+\frac{lz}{c^2}-1\). The enveloping cone with vertex \((h,k,l)\) is: \[SS_1=T^2,\] i.e., \[\left(\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}-1\right)\left(\frac{h^2}{a^2}+\frac{k^2}{b^2}+\frac{l^2}{c^2}-1\right)=\left(\frac{hx}{a^2}+\frac{ky}{b^2}+\frac{lz}{c^2}-1\right)^2.\] This is a cone with vertex at \((h,k,l)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Tangent Planes to Ellipsoid Parallel to Given Plane

Find the tangent planes to the ellipsoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) parallel to the plane \(\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z=0\).

Solution

Tangent plane at \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\): \(\frac{x_0x}{a^2}+\frac{y_0y}{b^2}+\frac{z_0z}{c^2}=1\), with normal direction \((x_0/a^2,y_0/b^2,z_0/c^2)\). For parallel to \(\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z=0\): \(\frac{x_0/a^2}{\alpha}=\frac{y_0/b^2}{\beta}=\frac{z_0/c^2}{\gamma}=k\). So \(x_0=k\alpha a^2,y_0=k\beta b^2,z_0=k\gamma c^2\). Using \((x_0,y_0,z_0)\) on ellipsoid: \(k^2(\alpha^2a^2+\beta^2b^2+\gamma^2c^2)=1\Rightarrow k=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^2a^2+\beta^2b^2+\gamma^2c^2}}\). Tangent planes: \[\alpha x+\beta y+\gamma z=\pm\sqrt{\alpha^2a^2+\beta^2b^2+\gamma^2c^2}.\] \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Polar Plane of a Point w.r.t. Conicoid

Define and find the polar plane of \((x_1,y_1,z_1)\) with respect to the conicoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\).

Solution

The polar plane of pole \(P=(x_1,y_1,z_1)\) w.r.t. the conicoid \(S=0\) is the plane \(T=0\): \[\frac{xx_1}{a^2}+\frac{yy_1}{b^2}+\frac{zz_1}{c^2}=1.\] Geometric interpretation: if any chord through \(P\) meets the conicoid at \(A,B\), then the harmonic conjugate \(Q\) of \(P\) w.r.t. \(A,B\) lies on the polar plane. If \(P\) is on the surface, the polar plane is the tangent plane at \(P\). The pole-polar relationship is reciprocal: \(Q\) lies on polar of \(P\) iff \(P\) lies on polar of \(Q\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Conjugate Diameters of Ellipsoid

Define conjugate semi-diameters of the ellipsoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\). State Apollonius's theorem for them.

Solution

Conjugate diameters: Three diameters \(OP,OQ,OR\) of the ellipsoid are conjugate if each bisects chords parallel to the plane of the other two. Equivalently, if the directions are \((l_i,m_i,n_i)\) for \(i=1,2,3\), they satisfy: \(\frac{l_1l_2}{a^2}+\frac{m_1m_2}{b^2}+\frac{n_1n_2}{c^2}=0\) (and cyclically). Apollonius's theorem: For three conjugate semi-diameters \(OP,OQ,OR\) with lengths \(p,q,r\): \[p^2+q^2+r^2=a^2+b^2+c^2.\] Proof: the sum of squares of lengths equals the sum of eigenvalues of the matrix \(\text{diag}(a^2,b^2,c^2)\) (trace is invariant under the conjugate transformation). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Three Conjugate Diameters: Sum of Squares

Prove that if \(OP,OQ,OR\) are three conjugate semi-diameters of the ellipsoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\), then \(OP^2+OQ^2+OR^2=a^2+b^2+c^2\).

Solution

Let the endpoints be \(P=(x_1,y_1,z_1)\), \(Q=(x_2,y_2,z_2)\), \(R=(x_3,y_3,z_3)\) on the ellipsoid. The conjugacy conditions: \(\frac{x_ix_j}{a^2}+\frac{y_iy_j}{b^2}+\frac{z_iz_j}{c^2}=0\) for \(i\ne j\). The matrix \(M\) with rows \((x_i/a,y_i/b,z_i/c)\) satisfies \(MM^T=I\) (orthonormal by conjugacy + on ellipsoid). Hence the columns also form an orthonormal set. Now \(OP^2=x_1^2+y_1^2+z_1^2\); summing: \(\sum OP_i^2=\sum(x_i^2+y_i^2+z_i^2)\). From \(MM^T=I\): \(\sum_i(x_i/a)^2=1,\sum_i(y_i/b)^2=1,\sum_i(z_i/c)^2=1\). So \(\sum OP_i^2=a^2+b^2+c^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Focal Conics of Ellipsoid

Define and find the focal conics of the ellipsoid \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (assuming \(a>b>c\)).

Solution

A focal conic is the conic in a principal plane such that every plane section confocal with the ellipsoid has its focus on the focal conic. For ellipsoid \(a>b>c\):
Focal ellipse (in \(z=0\) plane): \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2-c^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2-c^2}=1,\ z=0\).
Focal hyperbola (in \(y=0\) plane): \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2-b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{b^2-c^2}=1,\ y=0\).
These are related to the plane sections by confocal conicoids (sharing the same foci). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Umbilics of Ellipsoid

Define umbilics of an ellipsoid. Find the umbilics of \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (\(a>b>c\)).

Solution

An umbilic is a point on the surface where all normal sections have the same curvature (circular section). Equivalently, an umbilic is a point where every tangent direction is a principal direction. For the ellipsoid, umbilics lie in the \(y=0\) plane (containing the axes of greatest and least extent). Setting \(y=0\), an umbilic \((x_0,0,z_0)\) satisfies: the principal curvatures are equal. This occurs when \[\frac{x_0^2}{a^2(a^2-b^2)}=\frac{z_0^2}{c^2(b^2-c^2)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{x_0^2}{a^2}+\frac{z_0^2}{c^2}=1.\] Solving: \(x_0=\frac{a\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{\sqrt{a^2-c^2}},\ z_0=\frac{c\sqrt{b^2-c^2}}{\sqrt{a^2-c^2}}\) (and \(\pm\) combinations). Four umbilics total. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Principal Planes of General Second-degree Surface

Define the principal planes of a general second-degree surface. How are they related to the eigenvalues of the matrix of the quadratic form?

Solution

The principal planes of a conicoid \(S=0\) are planes of symmetry. They are perpendicular to the principal axes (eigenvectors of the matrix of the quadratic terms \(A=\begin{pmatrix}a&h&g\\h&b&f\\g&f&c\end{pmatrix}\)). The principal axes are the eigenvectors of \(A\); eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) are the principal curvatures (or lengths squared of axes). The surface in principal axes: \(\lambda_1X^2+\lambda_2Y^2+\lambda_3Z^2=\text{const}\). Principal planes are \(X=0,Y=0,Z=0\) (in the rotated frame) — they are the planes containing two of the three principal axes. If an eigenvalue is repeated, the corresponding principal plane is not unique. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2009 — Reduction of General Quadric to Standard Form

Describe the reduction of the general second-degree equation \(ax^2+by^2+cz^2+2fyz+2gzx+2hxy+2ux+2vy+2wz+d=0\) to standard form.

Solution

Step 1 Find eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\) of the \(3\times3\) matrix \(A\) of quadratic terms. Step 2 Rotate axes: orthogonal transformation diagonalising \(A\). In new coordinates \((X,Y,Z)\): quadratic part becomes \(\lambda_1X^2+\lambda_2Y^2+\lambda_3Z^2\). Step 3 Translate: complete the square in each variable to eliminate linear terms. The resulting standard forms are: ellipsoid (\(\lambda_i>0\)), hyperboloid of one/two sheets, elliptic paraboloid, hyperbolic paraboloid, cone, cylinder, etc., depending on signs of \(\lambda_i\) and the rank of the full \(4\times4\) matrix. The discriminant \(\Delta=\det\begin{pmatrix}A&\mathbf{u}\\\mathbf{u}^T&d\end{pmatrix}\) and \(\delta=\det A\) classify the surface. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Hyperboloid of One Sheet as Doubly-ruled Surface

Show that the hyperboloid of one sheet \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) is a doubly-ruled surface.

Solution

Two families of lines (reguli) lie on the surface. Write the equation as \(\left(\frac{x}{a}-\frac{z}{c}\right)\left(\frac{x}{a}+\frac{z}{c}\right)=\left(1-\frac{y}{b}\right)\left(1+\frac{y}{b}\right)\).
Family I (\(\lambda\)-family): \(\dfrac{x/a-z/c}{1-y/b}=\lambda=\dfrac{1+y/b}{x/a+z/c}\), i.e., \[\frac{x}{a}-\frac{z}{c}=\lambda\!\left(1-\frac{y}{b}\right),\quad \lambda\!\left(\frac{x}{a}+\frac{z}{c}\right)=1+\frac{y}{b}.\] Family II (\(\mu\)-family): \(\frac{x}{a}-\frac{z}{c}=\mu(1+\frac{y}{b}),\ \mu(\frac{x}{a}+\frac{z}{c})=1-\frac{y}{b}\). For each value of \(\lambda\) (or \(\mu\)), the two equations define a straight line on the surface. Hence two one-parameter families of lines — doubly ruled. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Regulus of Hyperboloid

Define the regulus of a ruled quadric. Show that the two reguli of a hyperboloid of one sheet are such that any line of one meets every line of the other.

Solution

A regulus is one family of rulings (lines) on a doubly-ruled surface. The two reguli of the hyperboloid \(\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}-\frac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) are the \(\lambda\)- and \(\mu\)-families. Take line \(L_\lambda\) from the \(\lambda\)-family and line \(M_\mu\) from the \(\mu\)-family. Their intersection: the four equations \((\lambda\text{-line})\cup(\mu\text{-line})\) form a linear system in \((x,y,z)\). One can show this system always has a unique solution (i.e., the lines always meet) by direct substitution or by the algebraic identity underlying the factored form. The point of intersection lies on both families, hence on the surface. This is the key property distinguishing the two reguli of a doubly-ruled quadric. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2006 — Generators of a Quadric Cone

Find all generators of the cone \(x^2+y^2-z^2=0\) passing through the origin.

Solution

The cone \(x^2+y^2=z^2\) has vertex at origin. A generator through origin: \((x,y,z)=t(l,m,n)\). On cone: \(l^2+m^2=n^2\). So \(n=\pm\sqrt{l^2+m^2}\). Parametrically: set \(l=\cos\phi,m=\sin\phi,n=1\) (normalised): generators \((t\cos\phi,t\sin\phi,t)\) for \(\phi\in[0,2\pi)\) form one nappe; \((t\cos\phi,t\sin\phi,-t)\) the other. PYQ 2005 — Classification of All Quadric Surfaces

Classify all non-degenerate quadric surfaces in three dimensions, giving the standard equation for each.

Solution

Non-degenerate quadrics (17 canonical types when including degenerates, but non-degenerate proper quadrics are):

  1. Ellipsoid: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}+\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (bounded, closed).
  2. Hyperboloid of one sheet: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (doubly connected, doubly ruled).
  3. Hyperboloid of two sheets: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (two disconnected pieces).
  4. Elliptic paraboloid: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=z\) (bowl-shaped, unbounded).
  5. Hyperbolic paraboloid: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=z\) (saddle-shaped, doubly ruled).
  6. Elliptic cone: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=0\) (vertex at origin).
  7. Elliptic cylinder: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\).
  8. Hyperbolic cylinder: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\).
  9. Parabolic cylinder: \(y^2=4ax\).
Classification uses \(\text{rank}(A)\), \(\text{sign}(A)\), \(\Delta=\det(4\times4\text{ matrix})\), and \(\delta=\det(A)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Find the equation of the sphere that passes through the circle \(x^2+y^2+z^2=9,\ 2x+3y+4z=5\) and the point \((1,2,3)\).

Solution

Any sphere through the given circle has the form: \[x^2+y^2+z^2-9+\lambda(2x+3y+4z-5)=0.\] Substituting \((1,2,3)\): \(1+4+9-9+\lambda(2+6+12-5)=0\Rightarrow 5+15\lambda=0\Rightarrow\lambda=-\tfrac{1}{3}\). \[x^2+y^2+z^2-9-\tfrac{1}{3}(2x+3y+4z-5)=0.\] Multiply by 3: \(\boxed{3x^2+3y^2+3z^2-2x-3y-4z-22=0.}\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q1e — Sphere and Tangent Plane

Find the equation of the sphere with centre \((1,-1,2)\) passing through the point \((3,1,1)\). Also find the equation of the tangent plane at \((3,1,1)\).

Solution

Radius: \(r=\sqrt{(3-1)^2+(1+1)^2+(1-2)^2}=\sqrt{4+4+1}=3\). Sphere: \((x-1)^2+(y+1)^2+(z-2)^2=9\). The tangent plane at \((x_1,y_1,z_1)\) on sphere \((x-a)^2+(y-b)^2+(z-c)^2=r^2\) is: \[(x_1-a)(x-a)+(y_1-b)(y-b)+(z_1-c)(z-c)=r^2.\] At \((3,1,1)\): \(2(x-1)+2(y+1)+(-1)(z-2)=9\Rightarrow 2x+2y-z=9\). So tangent plane: \(\boxed{2x+2y-z=9.}\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q2c — Shortest Distance Between Vector-form Lines

Find the shortest distance between the lines \(\mathbf{r}=(1+\lambda)\hat{i}+(2-\lambda)\hat{j}+(3+\lambda)\hat{k}\) and \(\mathbf{r}=(2+\mu)\hat{i}+(1+2\mu)\hat{j}+(3-\mu)\hat{k}\).

Solution

Direction vectors: \(\mathbf{d_1}=(1,-1,1)\), \(\mathbf{d_2}=(1,2,-1)\). Points: \(A_1=(1,2,3)\), \(A_2=(2,1,3)\). \[\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{d_1}\times\mathbf{d_2}=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k}\\1&-1&1\\1&2&-1\end{vmatrix}=(1-2)\hat{i}-(-1-1)\hat{j}+(2+1)\hat{k}=(-1,2,3).\] \(\overrightarrow{A_1A_2}=(1,-1,0)\). \[\text{SD}=\frac{|(1,-1,0)\cdot(-1,2,3)|}{|(-1,2,3)|}=\frac{|-1-2+0|}{\sqrt{1+4+9}}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{14}}=\boxed{\dfrac{3\sqrt{14}}{14}.}\] \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q3c — Right Circular Cone and Plane Section

Find the equation of the cone with vertex at the origin, axis along the \(z\)-axis, and semi-vertical angle \(45°\). Show that the plane \(z=k\) cuts this cone in a circle of radius \(|k|\).

Solution

For a point \((x,y,z)\) on the cone, the angle between the position vector and the \(z\)-axis satisfies: \[\cos 45°=\frac{|z|}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}\implies \frac{1}{2}=\frac{z^2}{x^2+y^2+z^2}\implies x^2+y^2=z^2.\] Cone equation: \(\boxed{x^2+y^2=z^2.}\) At \(z=k\): \(x^2+y^2=k^2\), which is a circle of radius \(|k|\) in the plane \(z=k\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q4c-i — Hyperboloid of One Sheet Through Two Circles

Find the equation of the hyperboloid of one sheet passing through the circle \(x^2+y^2=1,\ z=0\) and the circle \(x^2+y^2=4,\ z=2\).

Solution

By symmetry about the \(z\)-axis, assume the standard form \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{z^2}{c^2}=1\) (since cross-sections are circles).

  • At \(z=0\): \(\dfrac{1}{a^2}=1\Rightarrow a^2=1\).
  • At \(z=2\): \(\dfrac{4}{1}-\dfrac{4}{c^2}=1\Rightarrow \dfrac{4}{c^2}=3\Rightarrow c^2=\dfrac{4}{3}\).
Hyperboloid: \(\boxed{x^2+y^2-\dfrac{3z^2}{4}=1.}\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q4c-ii — Cylinder with \(z\)-axis Generators and Enclosed Volume

Find the equation of the cylinder with generators parallel to the \(z\)-axis passing through the ellipse \(x^2+2y^2=1,\ z=0\). Also find the volume enclosed between the cylinder, the plane \(z=0\), and the plane \(z=h\).

Solution

The cylinder with generators parallel to the \(z\)-axis and guiding curve \(x^2+2y^2=1\) in the plane \(z=0\) is simply \(\boxed{x^2+2y^2=1}\) (independent of \(z\)). This is an elliptic cylinder with semi-axes \(a=1\) (along \(x\)) and \(b=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\) (along \(y\)). Area of ellipse: \(\pi ab=\pi\cdot 1\cdot\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}=\dfrac{\pi}{\sqrt{2}}\). Volume between \(z=0\) and \(z=h\): \[V=h\times\text{Area}=\boxed{\dfrac{\pi h}{\sqrt{2}}.}\] \(\blacksquare\)
