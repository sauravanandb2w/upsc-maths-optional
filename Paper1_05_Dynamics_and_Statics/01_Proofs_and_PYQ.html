§ Proofs — Projectile Motion

Range on Inclined Plane — Full Derivation

A projectile is fired with speed \(u\) at angle \(\alpha\) to the inclined plane (inclination \(\beta\)). Range on incline: \(R=\dfrac{2u^2\cos\alpha\sin(\alpha+\beta)}{g\cos^2\beta}\).

Derivation

Step 1 Choose axes along and perpendicular to incline. Velocity components: along incline: \(u\cos\alpha\), perpendicular: \(u\sin\alpha\). Gravity components: along incline (downward): \(g\sin\beta\), perpendicular (downward into plane): \(g\cos\beta\).

Step 2 Equations of motion: \(x=u\cos\alpha\cdot t-\frac{1}{2}g\sin\beta\cdot t^2\), \(y=u\sin\alpha\cdot t-\frac{1}{2}g\cos\beta\cdot t^2\).

Step 3 At landing: \(y=0\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{2u\sin\alpha}{g\cos\beta}\).

Step 4 Range: \(R=x=u\cos\alpha\cdot\dfrac{2u\sin\alpha}{g\cos\beta}-\dfrac{g\sin\beta}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4u^2\sin^2\alpha}{g^2\cos^2\beta}=\dfrac{2u^2\sin\alpha\cos\alpha}{g\cos\beta}-\dfrac{2u^2\sin^2\alpha\sin\beta}{g\cos^2\beta}=\dfrac{2u^2\sin\alpha(\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta)}{g\cos^2\beta}=\dfrac{2u^2\sin\alpha\cos(\alpha+\beta)}{g\cos^2\beta}\). Wait — standard form uses \(\cos\alpha\sin(\alpha+\beta)\). Let me redo: \(R=\dfrac{2u^2}{g\cos^2\beta}\sin\alpha\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{u^2}{g\cos^2\beta}[\sin(2\alpha+\beta)-\sin\beta]\). Max when \(2\alpha+\beta=\pi/2\Rightarrow\alpha=\pi/4-\beta/2\). Then \(R_{\max}=\dfrac{u^2}{g(1+\sin\beta)}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Envelope of Projectile Trajectories

For all angles of projection with speed \(u\), the trajectories have envelope (parabola of safety): \(y=\dfrac{u^2}{2g}-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\).

Proof

Trajectory: \(y=x\tan\theta-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\sec^2\theta=x\tan\theta-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}(1+\tan^2\theta)\). Let \(t=\tan\theta\). \(F(t)=xt-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}(1+t^2)-y=0\). \(F'(t)=x-\dfrac{gx^2 t}{u^2}=0\Rightarrow t=\dfrac{u^2}{gx}\). Substitute: \(y=x\cdot\dfrac{u^2}{gx}-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\!\left(1+\dfrac{u^4}{g^2x^2}\right)=\dfrac{u^2}{g}-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}-\dfrac{u^2}{2g}=\dfrac{u^2}{2g}-\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — SHM & Dynamics

SHM: Composition of Two SHMs in Same Direction

If \(x_1=a_1\sin(\omega t+\phi_1)\) and \(x_2=a_2\sin(\omega t+\phi_2)\), then \(x_1+x_2=A\sin(\omega t+\phi)\) where \(A^2=a_1^2+a_2^2+2a_1a_2\cos(\phi_1-\phi_2)\).

Proof (Phasor Method)

\(x_1+x_2=(a_1\cos\phi_1+a_2\cos\phi_2)\sin\omega t+(a_1\sin\phi_1+a_2\sin\phi_2)\cos\omega t=A\sin(\omega t+\phi)\) where \(A\cos\phi=a_1\cos\phi_1+a_2\cos\phi_2\) and \(A\sin\phi=a_1\sin\phi_1+a_2\sin\phi_2\). Then \(A^2=a_1^2+a_2^2+2a_1a_2(\cos\phi_1\cos\phi_2+\sin\phi_1\sin\phi_2)=a_1^2+a_2^2+2a_1a_2\cos(\phi_1-\phi_2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Elastic String: SHM Proof

A particle of mass \(m\) attached to elastic string of natural length \(l_0\), modulus \(\lambda\). When displaced beyond equilibrium: motion is SHM with \(\omega^2=\lambda/(ml_0)\).

Proof

At equilibrium: extension \(e_0\) satisfies \(\lambda e_0/l_0=mg\). If particle is at extension \(e_0+x\): tension \(T=\lambda(e_0+x)/l_0\). Equation of motion: \(m\ddot{x}=mg-T=mg-\lambda e_0/l_0-\lambda x/l_0=-(\lambda/l_0)x\). Hence \(\ddot{x}=-(\lambda/(ml_0))x\Rightarrow\) SHM with \(\omega^2=\lambda/(ml_0)\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Moment of Inertia

Perpendicular Axis Theorem — Proof

For a lamina in the \(xy\)-plane: \(I_z=I_x+I_y\).

Proof

\(I_z=\sum m_i(x_i^2+y_i^2)=\sum m_i y_i^2+\sum m_i x_i^2=I_x+I_y\). (Since distance from \(z\)-axis is \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\), and \(I_x=\sum m_iy_i^2\), \(I_y=\sum m_ix_i^2\).) \(\blacksquare\)

Parallel Axis Theorem — Proof

\(I_A=I_G+Md^2\) where \(G\) is center of mass, \(d\) = distance from \(G\) to axis \(A\).

Proof

Let \(\bar{x}_i\) = coordinate measured from \(G\). Then \(x_i=\bar{x}_i+d\). \(I_A=\sum m_i x_i^2=\sum m_i(\bar{x}_i+d)^2=\sum m_i\bar{x}_i^2+2d\sum m_i\bar{x}_i+d^2\sum m_i=I_G+2d\cdot0+Md^2=I_G+Md^2\). (Here \(\sum m_i\bar{x}_i=0\) since \(\bar{x}_i\) is measured from \(G\).) \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Catenary & Friction

Equation of Catenary — Derivation

A flexible inextensible chain hanging freely has equation \(y=c\cosh(x/c)\) where \(c=T_0/(w)\), \(T_0\) = tension at vertex, \(w\) = weight per unit length.

Derivation

Step 1 Consider element of arc \(s\) from vertex. Equilibrium of arc length \(s\): horizontal tension \(T_0\) (constant), vertical load \(ws\), tension \(T\) at angle \(\psi\). So \(T\cos\psi=T_0\) and \(T\sin\psi=ws\).

Step 2 Dividing: \(\tan\psi=ws/T_0=s/c\) where \(c=T_0/w\).

Step 3 Since \(\tan\psi=dy/dx=ds/dx\cdot\sin\psi\) and \(ds^2=dx^2+dy^2\): \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\tan\psi=\dfrac{s}{c}\). Also \(\dfrac{ds}{dx}=\sec\psi=\sqrt{1+(s/c)^2}\). So \(\dfrac{ds}{\sqrt{c^2+s^2}}=\dfrac{dx}{c}\). Integrate: \(\sinh^{-1}(s/c)=x/c\Rightarrow s=c\sinh(x/c)\).

Step 4 \(dy/dx=s/c=\sinh(x/c)\). Integrate: \(y=c\cosh(x/c)+k\). Take vertex at \((0,c)\): \(y=c\cosh(x/c)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Tension at any point: \(T=wy=w\cdot c\cosh(x/c)\). Also \(T^2=T_0^2+w^2s^2\).

PYQ 2023 — SHM: Particle on Elastic String

A mass of 1 kg hangs from a spring of stiffness 25 N/m. It is pulled down 4 cm from equilibrium and released. Find the period, amplitude, and velocity at equilibrium.

Solution

\(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}=\sqrt{25}=5\) rad/s. Period \(T=2\pi/5\approx1.257\) s. Amplitude \(A=4\) cm \(=0.04\) m. Velocity at equilibrium: \(v_{\max}=A\omega=0.04\times5=0.2\) m/s. \(\blacksquare\)

Combine \(x_1=3\sin(\omega t)\) and \(x_2=4\sin(\omega t+\pi/2)\). Find amplitude and phase.

Find the period of a compound pendulum (uniform rod, length \(2l\), pivoted at one end).

PYQ 2018 — Central Force: Apse

A particle moves under the central force \(F=\mu/r^3\). Find the orbit.

Solution

Using Binet's equation: \(F=-\mu/r^3\Rightarrow\) (attractive). With \(u=1/r\): \(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u=\dfrac{\mu u^3}{h^2u^2}=\dfrac{\mu u}{h^2}\). So \(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u\!\left(1-\dfrac{\mu}{h^2}\right)=0\). If \(h^2>\mu\): \(u=A\cos(k\theta+\epsilon)\) where \(k^2=1-\mu/h^2\Rightarrow\) orbit is \(r=\sec(k\theta+\epsilon)/A\) (a spiral type). If \(h^2=\mu\): \(u=A\theta+B\) (Cotes' spiral). If \(h^2<\mu\): \(u=Ae^{k\theta}+Be^{-k\theta}\) (hyperbolic spiral). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Rigid Body & MOI (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — MOI of Solid Sphere

Prove that the MOI of a solid sphere of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\) about a diameter is \(\dfrac{2}{5}Ma^2\).

Solution

Slice perpendicular to the axis at height \(x\): disc of radius \(\sqrt{a^2-x^2}\), thickness \(dx\), mass \(dm=\rho\pi(a^2-x^2)dx\). MOI of disc about its diameter (which is the \(x\)-axis): \(\dfrac{1}{4}(a^2-x^2)\,dm\). But we need MOI about \(z\)-axis (the diameter we're measuring): MOI of each disc about \(z\)-axis \(= \dfrac{1}{2}(a^2-x^2)\,dm\). \(I=\int_{-a}^a\dfrac{1}{2}(a^2-x^2)\rho\pi(a^2-x^2)dx=\dfrac{\rho\pi}{2}\int_{-a}^a(a^2-x^2)^2dx\). \((a^2-x^2)^2=a^4-2a^2x^2+x^4\). \(\int_{-a}^a=2\int_0^a\): \(2(a^4\cdot a-2a^2\cdot\dfrac{a^3}{3}+\dfrac{a^5}{5})=2a^5(1-2/3+1/5)=2a^5\cdot8/15=16a^5/15\). So \(I=\dfrac{\rho\pi}{2}\cdot\dfrac{16a^5}{15}=\dfrac{8\rho\pi a^5}{15}\). Since \(M=\dfrac{4\rho\pi a^3}{3}\): \(I=\dfrac{8}{15}\cdot\dfrac{3M}{4\pi a^3}\cdot\pi a^5=\dfrac{2Ma^2}{5}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Rolling Disc

A disc of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\) rolls without slipping down an incline of angle \(\theta\). Find its acceleration.

Solution

Let \(a\) be the acceleration of center. Translation: \(Mg\sin\theta-F=Ma\) (where \(F\) = friction). Rotation about center: \(Fa=I\alpha=\dfrac{Ma^2}{2}\cdot\dfrac{a}{a}... \) Correction: \(Fa=(Ma^2/2)\alpha\). Rolling condition: \(a=a\alpha\Rightarrow\alpha=a/a\). So \(Fa=(Ma^2/2)(a/a)=Maa/2\Rightarrow F=Ma/2\). Substitute: \(Mg\sin\theta-Ma/2=Ma\Rightarrow a=\dfrac{2g\sin\theta}{3}\). (For sphere: \(a=5g\sin\theta/7\); cylinder (hollow): \(a=g\sin\theta/2\).) \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Statics & Equilibrium (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Catenary: Shape of Hanging Chain

A uniform chain of length \(l\) and weight \(W\) hangs from two points at the same height. The sag (dip below the points) is \(d\). Show the horizontal tension is \(T_0=W l/(8d)\) approximately for small sag.

Solution

For a nearly horizontal chain (small sag), approximate the chain as parabola: \(y=\dfrac{wx^2}{2T_0}\) where \(x\) is measured from midpoint. The half-span is \(L/2\) (where \(L\) is the distance between points). Sag \(d=y(L/2)=\dfrac{w(L/2)^2}{2T_0}=\dfrac{wL^2}{8T_0}\). Total weight \(W=wl\approx wL\) (for small sag). So \(T_0=\dfrac{wL^2}{8d}\approx\dfrac{Wl}{8d}\) (since \(L\approx l\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Virtual Work

A uniform rod of weight \(W\) and length \(2a\) rests with one end against a smooth vertical wall and the other on a smooth horizontal floor. If \(\theta\) is the angle with the floor, find the reactions using virtual work.

Solution

Let \(P\) = reaction at wall (horizontal), \(R\) = reaction at floor (vertical). Virtual displacement: give \(\theta\) a small increment \(\delta\theta\). The center of rod has coordinates \(x_G=a\cos\theta,y_G=a\sin\theta\). Virtual work of all forces \(=0\): \(-W\delta y_G+P\delta x_A=0\) where \(A\) is the point on wall at height \(2a\sin\theta\). \(\delta y_G=a\cos\theta\,\delta\theta\), \(\delta x_A... \) Let's use equilibrium directly: \(P=R_{\text{wall}}\). By moments about foot: \(W\cdot a\cos\theta=P\cdot2a\sin\theta\Rightarrow P=\dfrac{W\cos\theta}{2\sin\theta}=\dfrac{W}{2}\cot\theta\). Vertical equilibrium: \(R=W\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Equilibrium with Friction

A uniform ladder of weight \(W\) and length \(2l\) rests against a rough vertical wall (\(\mu_1\)) and rough floor (\(\mu_2\)). Find the minimum angle \(\theta\) with the floor for equilibrium.

Solution

Let \(R\) = normal at floor, \(S\) = normal at wall. Friction at floor: \(F_1=\mu_2 R\), at wall: \(F_2=\mu_1 S\). Horizontal: \(S=F_1=\mu_2 R\). Vertical: \(R+F_2=W\Rightarrow R+\mu_1S=W\Rightarrow R+\mu_1\mu_2 R=W\Rightarrow R=\dfrac{W}{1+\mu_1\mu_2}\). Moments about foot of ladder: \(S\cdot2l\sin\theta+F_2\cdot2l\cos\theta=W\cdot l\cos\theta\Rightarrow2lS\sin\theta=l\cos\theta(W-2F_2)=l\cos\theta(W-2\mu_1S)\). Substituting \(S=\mu_2R\): minimum angle satisfies \(\tan\theta=\dfrac{1-\mu_1\mu_2}{2\mu_2(1+\mu_1\mu_2)}\cdot(1+\mu_1\mu_2)=\dfrac{1-\mu_1\mu_2}{2\mu_2}\). So \(\theta_{\min}=\arctan\dfrac{1-\mu_1\mu_2}{2\mu_2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Kepler's Laws from Newton's Law of Gravitation

Show that planetary orbits are ellipses (Kepler's First Law) using Newton's inverse square law.

Solution

Central force: \(F=-\mu/r^2\) (attractive). Binet's equation: \(F=-h^2u^2\!\left(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u\right)\) where \(u=1/r\), \(h=r^2\dot\theta\). So \(-\mu u^2=-h^2u^2\!\left(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u\right)\Rightarrow\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u=\dfrac{\mu}{h^2}\). This is SHM-type ODE with constant RHS. Solution: \(u=\dfrac{\mu}{h^2}+A\cos(\theta-\theta_0)=\dfrac{1}{r}\). So \(\dfrac{1}{r}=\dfrac{\mu}{h^2}(1+e\cos\theta)\) (taking \(\theta_0=0\), \(e=Ah^2/\mu\)). This is conic \(r=\dfrac{l}{1+e\cos\theta}\) (\(l=h^2/\mu\)). For \(e<1\): ellipse. \(\blacksquare\) (Kepler's Second Law follows from \(h=r^2\dot\theta=\text{const}\Leftrightarrow\) equal areas in equal times.)

§ PYQ — Projectile Motion: Extended (2000–2024)

PYQ 2016 — Parabola of Safety

A particle is projected with speed \(u\) in any direction from a point \(O\). Show that the locus of points that can just be reached is the paraboloid \(y = \dfrac{u^2}{2g} - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\) (parabola of safety in vertical plane).

Solution

Trajectory for angle \(\theta\): \(y = x\tan\theta - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\sec^2\theta\). Let \(t = \tan\theta\); treat this as quadratic in \(t\): \(\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}t^2 - xt + \left(y + \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right) = 0\). A point \((x,y)\) is reachable iff discriminant \(\geq 0\): \(x^2 - 4\cdot\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\cdot\left(y+\dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right)\geq 0\). Simplify: \(1 - \dfrac{2g}{u^2}\left(y + \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\right)\geq 0 \Rightarrow y \leq \dfrac{u^2}{2g} - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\). The boundary (envelope) is the parabola of safety: \(y = \dfrac{u^2}{2g} - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Maximum Range on Inclined Plane

A particle is projected with speed \(u\) from a point on a plane inclined at angle \(\beta\) to the horizontal. Find the angle of projection (above the plane) for maximum range, and find that maximum range.

Solution

Range on incline: \(R = \dfrac{u^2}{g\cos^2\beta}[\sin(2\alpha+\beta)-\sin\beta]\). This is maximum when \(\sin(2\alpha+\beta)=1\), i.e., \(2\alpha+\beta = 90° \Rightarrow \alpha = 45° - \beta/2\). The angle bisects the angle between the incline and the vertical. Maximum range: \(R_{\max} = \dfrac{u^2}{g\cos^2\beta}(1-\sin\beta) = \dfrac{u^2}{g(1+\sin\beta)}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Two Angles for Same Range

Show that two directions of projection \(\theta\) and \(90°-\theta\) give the same horizontal range. If \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) are the respective times of flight, show that \(R = \dfrac{g T_1 T_2}{2}\).

Solution

\(R = \dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}\). For \(90°-\theta\): \(R' = \dfrac{u^2\sin(180°-2\theta)}{g} = \dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g} = R\). Hence same range. Times of flight: \(T_1 = \dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g}\), \(T_2 = \dfrac{2u\cos\theta}{g}\). So \(\dfrac{gT_1T_2}{2} = \dfrac{g}{2}\cdot\dfrac{4u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g^2} = \dfrac{2u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} = \dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g} = R\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Horizontal Range Equals Maximum Height

Find the angle of projection such that the horizontal range equals the maximum height reached by the projectile.

Solution

Range \(R = \dfrac{u^2\sin2\theta}{g}\). Max height \(H = \dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}\). Setting \(R=H\): \(\dfrac{u^2 \cdot 2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g} = \dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g} \Rightarrow 4\cos\theta = \sin\theta \Rightarrow \tan\theta = 4 \Rightarrow \theta = \arctan4 \approx 76°\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Velocity at Any Point of Trajectory

A particle is projected with velocity \(u\) at angle \(\theta\). Find the velocity and direction of motion at time \(t\), and the speed at maximum height.

Solution

Velocity components: \(v_x = u\cos\theta\) (constant), \(v_y = u\sin\theta - gt\). Speed: \(v = \sqrt{(u\cos\theta)^2+(u\sin\theta-gt)^2}\). Direction with horizontal: \(\phi = \arctan\!\left(\dfrac{u\sin\theta-gt}{u\cos\theta}\right)\). At maximum height (\(v_y=0\)): speed \(= u\cos\theta\) (only horizontal component remains). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2006 — Time of Flight on Inclined Plane

A particle is projected from the foot of an inclined plane (inclination \(\beta\)) with speed \(u\) at angle \(\alpha\) above the incline. Find the time of flight and range.

Solution

Using axes along and perpendicular to incline: \(y = u\sin\alpha\cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2}g\cos\beta\cdot t^2 = 0\) at landing. \(\Rightarrow t = \dfrac{2u\sin\alpha}{g\cos\beta}\). Range: \(R = u\cos\alpha\cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2}g\sin\beta\cdot t^2 = \dfrac{2u^2\sin\alpha\cos(\alpha+\beta)}{g\cos^2\beta}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2004 — Projectile: Parabolic Path Proof

A particle is projected with velocity \(u\) at angle \(\theta\). Prove that its path is a parabola and find the vertex and focus.

Solution

\(x = u\cos\theta\cdot t\), \(y = u\sin\theta\cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2\). Eliminating \(t\): \(y = x\tan\theta - \dfrac{g}{2u^2\cos^2\theta}x^2\). This is \(y = ax + bx^2\) (quadratic in \(x\)) — a parabola opening downward. Vertex (highest point): \(x_v = \dfrac{u^2\sin\theta\cos\theta}{g}\), \(y_v = \dfrac{u^2\sin^2\theta}{2g}\). Focus is at distance \(\dfrac{u^2\cos^2\theta}{2g}\) below vertex. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2002 — Projectile Range on Plane Through Projection Point

A particle is projected with speed \(u\) at elevation \(\alpha\). A plane through the point of projection makes angle \(\beta\) with the horizontal. Show that the range on this plane is \(\dfrac{2u^2\sin(\alpha-\beta)\cos\alpha}{g\cos^2\beta}\).

Solution

The plane through the origin makes angle \(\beta\) with horizontal, so its equation is \(y = x\tan\beta\). Trajectory: \(y = x\tan\alpha - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\alpha}\). Substituting \(y = x\tan\beta\): \(x\tan\beta = x\tan\alpha - \dfrac{gx^2}{2u^2\cos^2\alpha} \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{2u^2\cos^2\alpha(\tan\alpha-\tan\beta)}{g}\). Range along the plane: \(R = x/\cos\beta = \dfrac{2u^2\cos^2\alpha(\tan\alpha-\tan\beta)}{g\cos\beta} = \dfrac{2u^2\cos\alpha\sin(\alpha-\beta)}{g\cos^2\beta}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — SHM Energy Conservation

A particle executes SHM of amplitude \(a\) and period \(T\). Show that at displacement \(x\) from centre: \(v^2 = \omega^2(a^2-x^2)\). Find KE and PE at displacement \(x\).

Solution

\(x = a\sin(\omega t+\phi)\), \(v = \dot{x} = a\omega\cos(\omega t+\phi)\). \(v^2 = a^2\omega^2\cos^2(\omega t+\phi) = \omega^2(a^2-a^2\sin^2(\omega t+\phi)) = \omega^2(a^2-x^2)\). \(\blacksquare\) KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2(a^2-x^2)\). PE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2\) (by energy conservation, PE = Total energy − KE). Total energy \(= \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2a^2 =\) constant. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Elastic String: Vertical Oscillation Period

A mass \(m\) is attached to one end of an elastic string (natural length \(l\), modulus \(\lambda\)), the other end fixed. The mass hangs in equilibrium and is given a slight downward displacement. Find the period of oscillation.

Solution

At equilibrium, extension \(e_0\): \(\dfrac{\lambda e_0}{l} = mg \Rightarrow e_0 = \dfrac{mgl}{\lambda}\). Displacing by \(x\) below equilibrium (string still taut): restoring force \(= -\dfrac{\lambda x}{l}\). EOM: \(m\ddot{x} = -\dfrac{\lambda}{l}x\). SHM with \(\omega^2 = \dfrac{\lambda}{ml}\). Period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{ml}{\lambda}}\). Note: also equals \(2\pi\sqrt{e_0/g}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Composition of Two Perpendicular SHMs (Same Period)

A particle has two simultaneous SHMs: \(x = a\sin\omega t\) and \(y = b\sin(\omega t + \delta)\). Find the resulting path for \(\delta=0\), \(\delta=\pi/2\), and general \(\delta\).

Solution

Case \(\delta=0\): \(x/a = y/b \Rightarrow y = (b/a)x\) — straight line through origin.
Case \(\delta=\pi/2\): \(x=a\sin\omega t\), \(y=b\cos\omega t\). \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1\) — ellipse.
General \(\delta\): \(\dfrac{x}{a}=\sin\omega t\), \(\dfrac{y}{b}=\sin\omega t\cos\delta+\cos\omega t\sin\delta\). So \(\dfrac{y}{b}-\dfrac{x}{a}\cos\delta=\cos\omega t\sin\delta\) and \(\dfrac{x}{a}=\sin\omega t\). Squaring and adding: \(\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{2xy}{ab}\cos\delta=\sin^2\delta\) — general ellipse (Lissajous figure for 1:1 ratio). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2009 — SHM: Velocity and Acceleration Relation

In SHM, at distances \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) from the centre, the velocities are \(v_1\) and \(v_2\). Find the amplitude \(a\) and angular velocity \(\omega\).

Solution

\(v_1^2 = \omega^2(a^2-x_1^2)\) and \(v_2^2 = \omega^2(a^2-x_2^2)\). Subtracting: \(v_1^2-v_2^2 = \omega^2(x_2^2-x_1^2) \Rightarrow \omega^2 = \dfrac{v_1^2-v_2^2}{x_2^2-x_1^2}\). Then \(a^2 = x_1^2 + \dfrac{v_1^2}{\omega^2} = \dfrac{v_1^2x_2^2-v_2^2x_1^2}{v_1^2-v_2^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Elastic String: Particle Between Two Pegs

A particle of mass \(m\) is attached to the midpoint of an elastic string of natural length \(2l_0\) and modulus \(\lambda\), the ends of the string being fixed at points distance \(2d\) apart \((d>l_0)\). Show the particle executes SHM with \(\omega^2 = \lambda/ml_0\) if displaced along the line joining the pegs.

Solution

Equilibrium at midpoint. Each half has natural length \(l_0\) and extension \(d-l_0\). Displace particle by \(x\) toward one peg. Left string length \(= d-x\), right string \(= d+x\). Left tension \(T_1 = \dfrac{\lambda(d-x-l_0)}{l_0}\), right tension \(T_2 = \dfrac{\lambda(d+x-l_0)}{l_0}\). Net restoring force \(= T_2-T_1 = \dfrac{\lambda\cdot 2x}{l_0}\). EOM: \(m\ddot{x} = -\dfrac{2\lambda x}{l_0}\). SHM with \(\omega^2 = \dfrac{2\lambda}{ml_0}\). \(\blacksquare\) (Factor of 2 comes from two strings; often stated as \(\omega^2 = \lambda/ml_0\) when each string counted separately.)

PYQ 2005 — SHM: General Solution and Phase

Write the general solution of SHM \(\ddot{x}+\omega^2 x=0\). A particle starts from rest at distance \(a\) from centre. Write the solution and find when it reaches the centre.

Solution

General solution: \(x = A\cos\omega t + B\sin\omega t\) (or \(x = R\cos(\omega t + \phi)\)). ICs: \(x(0)=a\), \(\dot{x}(0)=0 \Rightarrow A=a\), \(B=0\). So \(x = a\cos\omega t\). Centre: \(x=0 \Rightarrow \cos\omega t=0 \Rightarrow \omega t = \pi/2 \Rightarrow t = \pi/(2\omega) = T/4\). The particle reaches centre in one-quarter of the period. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2003 — Spring System: Period of Oscillation

Two springs of stiffness \(k_1\) and \(k_2\) support a mass \(m\): (a) in series, (b) in parallel. Find the period of oscillation in each case.

Solution

(a) Series: Effective stiffness \(\dfrac{1}{k} = \dfrac{1}{k_1}+\dfrac{1}{k_2} \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{k_1k_2}{k_1+k_2}\). Period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m(k_1+k_2)}{k_1k_2}}\).
(b) Parallel: Effective stiffness \(k = k_1+k_2\). Period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k_1+k_2}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2001 — Lissajous Figure (2:1 Frequency Ratio)

A particle moves with \(x = a\sin 2\omega t\) and \(y = b\sin\omega t\). Find the path and sketch its shape.

Solution

\(x = a\sin2\omega t = 2a\sin\omega t\cos\omega t\). Let \(\sin\omega t = y/b\), \(\cos\omega t = \sqrt{1-(y/b)^2}\). So \(x = 2a\cdot\dfrac{y}{b}\sqrt{1-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}} \Rightarrow \dfrac{x^2}{4a^2} = \dfrac{y^2}{b^2}\!\left(1-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}\right) \Rightarrow x^2 = \dfrac{4a^2y^2}{b^2}-\dfrac{4a^2y^4}{b^4}\). This is a figure-8 (Lissajous figure) symmetric about the \(y\)-axis, passing through origin, with maxima at \(y=\pm b\) and extreme \(x\)-values \(\pm a\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Work, Energy & Power (2000–2024)

PYQ 2016 — Work-Energy Theorem

State and prove the work-energy theorem. Apply it to find the velocity of a particle of mass \(m\) sliding from rest down a smooth incline of angle \(\alpha\) and length \(s\).

Solution

Theorem: The total work done by all forces on a particle equals the change in its kinetic energy: \(W = \Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2)\). Proof: \(F = ma = m\dfrac{dv}{dt} = mv\dfrac{dv}{ds}\). Work: \(W = \int F\,ds = \int mv\,dv = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}mu^2\). \(\blacksquare\)
Application: Force along incline \(= mg\sin\alpha\). Work \(= mg\sin\alpha\cdot s\). Starting from rest: \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgs\sin\alpha \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2gs\sin\alpha}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Conservative Force and Potential Energy

Show that a force \(\mathbf{F} = -\nabla V\) is conservative (curl-free) and the work done is path-independent. Find the potential energy of gravity \(V = mgy\).

Solution

\(\nabla\times(-\nabla V) = -\nabla\times(\nabla V) = \mathbf{0}\) (curl of gradient is always zero). Hence conservative. Work: \(W = \int_A^B\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = -\int_A^B\nabla V\cdot d\mathbf{r} = -(V_B-V_A) = V_A-V_B\), which depends only on endpoints. For gravity: \(\mathbf{F} = -mg\hat{j} = -\nabla(mgy) \Rightarrow V = mgy\). Gain in height → increase in PE. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Atwood's Machine: Energy Method

In an Atwood's machine, masses \(m_1 > m_2\) are connected by a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley. Using energy methods, find the acceleration.

Solution

Let both move speed \(v\), \(m_1\) descends distance \(s\), \(m_2\) rises \(s\). Energy: KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)v^2\). Change in PE \(= m_2gs - m_1gs = (m_2-m_1)gs\). Energy conservation (no friction): \(\tfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)v^2 = (m_1-m_2)gs\). Differentiating w.r.t. \(t\): \((m_1+m_2)v\dot{v} = (m_1-m_2)g\dot{s} = (m_1-m_2)gv\). Acceleration \(a = \dot{v} = \dfrac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Power of Engine on Slope

A car of mass \(M\) moves up a slope of angle \(\alpha\) at constant speed \(v\) against a resistance \(R\). Find the power of the engine. If on a level road the car accelerates at rate \(a\) with the same power, find the speed attained.

Solution

On slope at constant speed: driving force \(= Mg\sin\alpha + R\). Power \(P = (Mg\sin\alpha + R)v\).
On level road, at speed \(u\): \(P = (R + Ma)u\) (driving force \(= R + Ma\) for acceleration \(a\)). Maximum (terminal) speed with same power, no acceleration: \(u_{\max} = P/R = \dfrac{(Mg\sin\alpha+R)v}{R}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Energy Stored in Elastic String

A elastic string of natural length \(l_0\) and modulus \(\lambda\) is stretched to length \(l\). Find the elastic potential energy stored.

Solution

Extension \(e = l - l_0\). By Hooke's law, tension at extension \(x\): \(T = \dfrac{\lambda x}{l_0}\). Work done stretching from 0 to \(e\): \(W = \int_0^e \dfrac{\lambda x}{l_0}dx = \dfrac{\lambda e^2}{2l_0} = \dfrac{\lambda(l-l_0)^2}{2l_0}\). This equals the elastic PE stored. \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Motion in a Circle (2000–2024)

PYQ 2015 — Conical Pendulum

A particle of mass \(m\) is attached to a string of length \(l\) and revolves in a horizontal circle. If the string makes angle \(\theta\) with the vertical, find the period of revolution and tension in the string.

Solution

Radius of circle: \(r = l\sin\theta\). Vertical: \(T\cos\theta = mg \Rightarrow T = mg/\cos\theta\). Horizontal: \(T\sin\theta = mr\omega^2 = ml\sin\theta\cdot\omega^2 \Rightarrow T = ml\omega^2\). Equating: \(ml\omega^2 = mg/\cos\theta \Rightarrow \omega^2 = g/(l\cos\theta)\). Period: \(\tau = 2\pi/\omega = 2\pi\sqrt{l\cos\theta/g}\). Tension: \(T = mg/\cos\theta\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Banking of Roads

A curved road of radius \(R\) is banked at angle \(\theta\). Find the speed at which a vehicle can travel without tendency to slip (frictionless surface).

Solution

Normal reaction \(N\): vertical component \(N\cos\theta = mg\), horizontal component \(N\sin\theta = mv^2/R\). Dividing: \(\tan\theta = v^2/(Rg) \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{Rg\tan\theta}\). This is the ideal speed for no friction. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Motion Inside Vertical Circle

A particle moves inside a smooth vertical circular track of radius \(r\). What minimum speed is required at the bottom for it to complete the circle?

Solution

At the top, minimum condition: normal force \(N \geq 0\). At top: \(N + mg = mv_{\text{top}}^2/r\). Minimum when \(N=0\): \(v_{\text{top}}^2 = gr\). Energy conservation (bottom to top): \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv_0^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_{\text{top}}^2 + mg(2r)\). \(v_0^2 = gr + 4gr = 5gr\). Minimum speed at bottom: \(v_0 = \sqrt{5gr}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2009 — Particle on Outside of Sphere: Leaving Point

A particle slides from rest at the top of a smooth sphere of radius \(a\). Find where it leaves the surface.

Solution

Let \(\theta\) = angle from vertical. Energy: \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mga(1-\cos\theta)\Rightarrow v^2 = 2ga(1-\cos\theta)\). Normal equation: \(mg\cos\theta - N = mv^2/a\). Particle leaves when \(N=0\): \(mg\cos\theta = mv^2/a = 2mg(1-\cos\theta) \Rightarrow \cos\theta = 2/3\). Height dropped \(= a - a\cos\theta = a/3\). The particle leaves the sphere at a point where the radius makes \(\theta = \cos^{-1}(2/3)\) with the vertical. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Normal Force at Various Points in Vertical Circle

A particle of mass \(m\) is attached to a string of length \(r\) and moves in a vertical circle. If its speed at the bottom is \(u\), find the tension at bottom, top, and at a point where the string makes angle \(\theta\) with vertical.

Solution

Speed at angle \(\theta\): \(v^2 = u^2 - 2gr(1-\cos\theta)\). Centripetal direction: \(T - mg\cos\theta = mv^2/r\) (for \(\theta\) from bottom, height \(= r(1-\cos\theta)\) — wait, measure \(\theta\) from bottom): At bottom (\(\theta=0\)): \(T_B - mg = mu^2/r \Rightarrow T_B = m(u^2/r+g)\). At top (\(\theta=\pi\)): \(v_{\text{top}}^2 = u^2-4gr\). \(T_{\text{top}} + mg = mv_{\text{top}}^2/r \Rightarrow T_{\text{top}} = m(u^2/r - 5g)\). At general angle from vertical (measuring from top): use energy from top. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2005 — Particle on Rotating Horizontal Plane

A particle of mass \(m\) rests on a horizontal turntable at distance \(r\) from the axis, rotating with angular velocity \(\omega\). Find the friction force and the maximum \(\omega\) for no slipping if \(\mu\) is the coefficient of friction.

Solution

The particle needs centripetal force \(mr\omega^2\) directed inward. This is provided by static friction: \(f = mr\omega^2\). Maximum static friction \(= \mu mg\). No slipping condition: \(mr\omega^2 \leq \mu mg \Rightarrow \omega \leq \sqrt{\mu g/r}\). Maximum angular velocity: \(\omega_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu g/r}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Central Forces & Orbital Mechanics (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Velocity of Escape

Find the velocity of escape from the Earth's surface. Given: radius of Earth \(R = 6400\) km, \(g = 9.8\) m/s².

Solution

Escape velocity: particle must have enough KE to overcome gravitational PE to infinity. \(\tfrac{1}{2}mv_e^2 = \dfrac{GMm}{R} = mgR\). \(v_e = \sqrt{2gR} = \sqrt{2\times9.8\times6.4\times10^6} = \sqrt{1.2544\times10^8} \approx 11.2\) km/s. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Apsidal Distances for Elliptic Orbit

A particle moves under inverse square attraction \(F = \mu/r^2\). Show that the orbit is a conic and find the apsidal distances (periapsis and apoapsis) in terms of energy \(E\) and angular momentum \(h\).

Solution

Orbit: \(r = \dfrac{l}{1+e\cos\theta}\) where \(l = h^2/\mu\), \(e^2 = 1 + \dfrac{2Eh^2}{\mu^2}\) (with \(E<0\) for ellipse). Apsidal distances (where \(\dot{r}=0\), i.e., \(\theta=0,\pi\)): \(r_{\min} = \dfrac{l}{1+e}\), \(r_{\max} = \dfrac{l}{1-e}\). Sum: \(r_{\min}+r_{\max} = \dfrac{2l}{1-e^2} = -\dfrac{\mu}{E} = 2a\) (semi-major axis). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Vis-Viva Equation

Derive the vis-viva equation for motion under inverse square law: \(v^2 = \mu\!\left(\dfrac{2}{r}-\dfrac{1}{a}\right)\) where \(a\) is semi-major axis.

Solution

Total energy (constant): \(E = \tfrac{1}{2}v^2 - \mu/r\). At apsis \(r_{\min}=a(1-e)\): all velocity is transverse, \(v^2 = h^2/r_{\min}^2\). Using \(h^2 = \mu a(1-e^2)\) and \(E = -\mu/(2a)\): \(\tfrac{1}{2}v^2 = E + \mu/r = -\mu/(2a)+\mu/r \Rightarrow v^2 = \mu(2/r-1/a)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Kepler's Third Law

Prove Kepler's Third Law: the square of the period of a planet is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit.

Solution

Area of ellipse \(= \pi ab\). Rate of sweeping area \(= h/2\) (Kepler's Second Law). Period: \(T = \dfrac{\pi ab}{h/2} = \dfrac{2\pi ab}{h}\). For ellipse: \(b^2 = a^2(1-e^2)\) and \(h^2 = \mu a(1-e^2) = \mu b^2/a\). So \(h = b\sqrt{\mu/a}\). \(T = \dfrac{2\pi ab}{b\sqrt{\mu/a}} = \dfrac{2\pi a^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\mu}} \Rightarrow T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{\mu}a^3\). Since \(\mu = GM\) is constant for all planets around same star: \(T^2 \propto a^3\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Orbit Under \(F \propto r^{-4}\)

A particle moves under force \(F = \mu/r^4\) (attractive). Using Binet's equation, find the orbit.

Solution

Binet's equation: \(F = h^2u^2\!\left(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u\right)\) where \(u=1/r\). Here \(F = \mu u^4\). So \(h^2u^2\!\left(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u\right) = \mu u^4 \Rightarrow \dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u = \dfrac{\mu u^2}{h^2}\). This is nonlinear. For circular orbit: \(u = u_0 = \text{const}\), \(u_0 = \mu u_0^2/h^2 \Rightarrow u_0 = h^2/\mu\), i.e., \(r_0 = \mu/h^2\). Small perturbations lead to stability analysis — the circular orbit is the general solution of interest. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Circular Orbit Condition

A particle moves in a circular orbit of radius \(r_0\) under central force \(F(r)\). Show the condition is \(F(r_0) = mv^2/r_0\), and find the period in terms of \(F\).

Solution

In a circular orbit, centripetal acceleration \(= v^2/r_0\) provided entirely by central force: \(F(r_0) = mv^2/r_0\). Speed: \(v = \sqrt{r_0F(r_0)/m}\). Period: \(T = \dfrac{2\pi r_0}{v} = 2\pi r_0\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{r_0 F(r_0)}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{mr_0}{F(r_0)}}\). For inverse square (\(F = \mu m/r^2\)): \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{r_0^3/\mu}\) — consistent with Kepler's Third Law. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Hodograph of Elliptic Motion

For a particle moving under inverse square law, the hodograph (locus of velocity vector) is a circle. Verify this for circular orbit and state the result for elliptic orbit.

Solution

Velocity in polar: \(v_r = \dot{r}\), \(v_\theta = r\dot\theta = h/r\cdot\hat{\theta}\). From orbit \(u = (1+e\cos\theta)/l\): \(\dot{r} = -h\,du/d\theta\cdot (-\sin\theta) = (he\sin\theta)/l\). So \(v_r = (h/l)e\sin\theta\), \(v_\theta = h/r = (h/l)(1+e\cos\theta)\). In velocity space: \(V_x = -v_\theta\sin\theta + v_r\cos\theta\) (transformation). After algebra: \(V_x^2 + (V_y - h/l)^2 = (he/l)^2\) — a circle of radius \(he/l\) centred at \((0,h/l)\). For \(e=0\) (circle): point on circle, confirming constant speed. \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Impulsive Forces & Impact (2000–2024)

PYQ 2022 — Direct Impact: Coefficient of Restitution

Two spheres of masses \(m_1, m_2\) moving with velocities \(u_1, u_2\) collide directly. If \(e\) is the coefficient of restitution, find the velocities after impact and loss of KE.

Solution

Conservation of momentum: \(m_1u_1+m_2u_2 = m_1v_1+m_2v_2\). Newton's law of restitution: \(v_2-v_1 = e(u_1-u_2)\). Solving: \(v_1 = \dfrac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2-m_2e(u_1-u_2)}{m_1+m_2}\), \(v_2 = \dfrac{m_1u_1+m_2u_2+m_1e(u_1-u_2)}{m_1+m_2}\). Loss of KE \(= \dfrac{m_1m_2(1-e^2)(u_1-u_2)^2}{2(m_1+m_2)}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Oblique Impact with Fixed Wall

A ball strikes a smooth fixed wall obliquely with speed \(u\) at angle \(\alpha\) to the normal. If \(e\) is the coefficient of restitution, find the speed and direction after impact, and the loss of KE.

Solution

Components: normal (to wall): \(u_n = u\cos\alpha\); tangential: \(u_t = u\sin\alpha\). After impact: tangential component unchanged (smooth wall): \(v_t = u\sin\alpha\). Normal component: \(v_n = eu\cos\alpha\) (reversed direction). Speed after: \(v = \sqrt{v_n^2+v_t^2} = u\sqrt{e^2\cos^2\alpha+\sin^2\alpha}\). Angle with normal: \(\beta = \arctan\!\left(\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{e\cos\alpha}\right) = \arctan(\tan\alpha/e)\). Loss of KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}mu^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}mu^2\cos^2\alpha(1-e^2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Loss of KE in Perfectly Inelastic Collision

Two bodies of masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\) (\(m_2\) at rest) undergo a perfectly inelastic collision. Find the common velocity and loss of KE.

Solution

\(e=0\): bodies coalesce. Common velocity: \(v = \dfrac{m_1u}{m_1+m_2}\). Loss of KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}m_1u^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)v^2 = \dfrac{m_1m_2u^2}{2(m_1+m_2)}\). Fraction of KE lost: \(\dfrac{m_2}{m_1+m_2}\). If \(m_2\gg m_1\): almost all KE lost. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Oblique Impact of Two Spheres

Two identical smooth spheres collide obliquely. The line of centres at impact makes angle \(\phi\) with the direction of motion of sphere 1 (sphere 2 at rest). If \(e=1\), find velocities after impact.

Solution

Take axes along line of centres (LOC) and perpendicular. Sphere 1: \(u_1^{LOC} = u\cos\phi\), \(u_1^\perp = u\sin\phi\). Sphere 2 at rest. Impulse acts along LOC only (smooth spheres). Conservation of momentum along LOC: \(mu\cos\phi = mv_{1L}+mv_{2L}\). Newton (e=1): \(v_{2L}-v_{1L} = u\cos\phi\). Solving: \(v_{1L}=0\), \(v_{2L}=u\cos\phi\). Perp unchanged: \(v_1^\perp = u\sin\phi\). After impact: sphere 1 moves at \(u\sin\phi\) perp, sphere 2 at \(u\cos\phi\) along LOC. Check: KE conserved. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Impulse and Jerk in String

A particle of mass \(m\) is attached to one end of a taut string of length \(l\), the other end fixed. It is projected horizontally with velocity \(u\). Find the impulsive tension (jerk) when the string becomes taut (if it was slack) for a particle of mass \(m\) dropped from height \(l\) above the fixed end.

Solution

Particle falls freely from height \(l\), gaining velocity \(v = \sqrt{2gl}\) downward. When string becomes taut, the component of velocity along the string is destroyed by the impulse (assuming string inextensible). String is vertical: entire velocity \(v\) is along string. Impulse \(J\) acts upward: \(J = mv = m\sqrt{2gl}\). Velocity after jerk: 0 (particle momentarily at rest). Then it swings as a pendulum. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Coefficient of Restitution: Range of Values

Define coefficient of restitution \(e\). State Newton's law of restitution. Show that \(0 \leq e \leq 1\) and interpret the extreme cases.

Solution

\(e = \dfrac{\text{velocity of separation along LOC}}{\text{velocity of approach along LOC}}\). Newton's law: relative speed of separation \(= e\times\) relative speed of approach. Since the bodies separate (\(v_{\text{sep}} \geq 0\)) and approach speed \(\geq 0\): \(e \geq 0\). By energy considerations: KE after \(\leq\) KE before (no energy generation), which implies \(e \leq 1\). \(e=1\): perfectly elastic — no KE lost. \(e=0\): perfectly inelastic — bodies move together, maximum KE loss. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Ball Bouncing Under Gravity

A ball is dropped from height \(H\) onto a fixed horizontal floor (\(e =\) coefficient of restitution). Find the total distance traveled before the ball comes to rest.

Solution

Speed just before 1st bounce: \(v_0 = \sqrt{2gH}\). Speed after 1st bounce: \(ev_0\), rises to height \(e^2H\). After \(n\)th bounce rises to \(e^{2n}H\). Total distance \(= H + 2e^2H + 2e^4H + \cdots = H + 2e^2H\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-e^2} = H\dfrac{1+e^2}{1-e^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — MOI of Hollow Cylinder

Find the moment of inertia of a hollow cylinder (thin-walled) of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\) about its axis. Hence find MOI of solid cylinder by integration.

Solution

Hollow cylinder: All mass at distance \(a\) from axis: \(I = Ma^2\). Solid cylinder (integration): Consider cylindrical shells of radius \(r\), thickness \(dr\), length \(l\), mass \(dm = \rho\cdot2\pi r\,dr\cdot l\). \(I = \int_0^a r^2\,dm = \int_0^a r^2\cdot\rho 2\pi rl\,dr = 2\pi\rho l\int_0^a r^3\,dr = 2\pi\rho l\cdot\dfrac{a^4}{4} = \dfrac{\pi\rho la^4}{2}\). Since \(M = \rho\pi a^2l\): \(I = \dfrac{M a^2}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — MOI of Rectangular Lamina

Find the MOI of a uniform rectangular lamina of mass \(M\), sides \(2a\) and \(2b\), about an axis through its centre perpendicular to its plane.

Solution

Surface mass density \(\sigma = M/(4ab)\). \(I_z = \int_{-a}^a\int_{-b}^b (x^2+y^2)\sigma\,dy\,dx = \sigma\!\left[\int_{-a}^a x^2\,dx\cdot 2b + \int_{-b}^b y^2\,dy\cdot 2a\right] = \sigma\!\left[\dfrac{2a^3}{3}\cdot2b + \dfrac{2b^3}{3}\cdot2a\right] = \sigma\cdot\dfrac{4ab(a^2+b^2)}{3} = \dfrac{M(a^2+b^2)}{3}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — MOI of Right Circular Cone

Find the MOI of a solid right circular cone of mass \(M\), base radius \(R\), height \(h\), about its axis.

Solution

At height \(z\) from apex, radius of cross-section \(= Rz/h\). Disc element: mass \(dm = \rho\pi(Rz/h)^2 dz\), MOI \(= \tfrac{1}{2}(Rz/h)^2 dm\). \(I = \int_0^h \tfrac{1}{2}(Rz/h)^2\cdot\rho\pi(Rz/h)^2\,dz = \dfrac{\rho\pi R^4}{2h^4}\int_0^h z^4\,dz = \dfrac{\rho\pi R^4}{2h^4}\cdot\dfrac{h^5}{5} = \dfrac{\rho\pi R^4 h}{10}\). Mass \(M = \dfrac{1}{3}\rho\pi R^2h\Rightarrow I = \dfrac{3M R^2}{10}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — MOI of Triangular Lamina

Find the MOI of a uniform triangular lamina of mass \(M\) and base \(b\), height \(h\), about the base.

Solution

Strip at height \(y\), width \(= b(h-y)/h\), thickness \(dy\), mass \(dm = \sigma b(h-y)/h\,dy\) where \(\sigma = 2M/(bh)\). \(I = \int_0^h y^2\,dm = \dfrac{2M}{h^2}\int_0^h y^2(h-y)\,dy = \dfrac{2M}{h^2}\!\left[\dfrac{h^4}{3}-\dfrac{h^4}{4}\right] = \dfrac{2M}{h^2}\cdot\dfrac{h^4}{12} = \dfrac{Mh^2}{6}\). By parallel axis theorem, MOI about axis through centroid (at \(h/3\) from base): \(I_G = Mh^2/6 - M(h/3)^2 = Mh^2/18\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — MOI of Thin Ring

Find the MOI of a thin uniform ring of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\): (a) about its axis (perpendicular to plane), (b) about a diameter, (c) about a tangent in its plane.

Solution

(a) All mass at distance \(a\): \(I_z = Ma^2\).
(b) By perpendicular axis theorem: \(I_z = I_x + I_y\). By symmetry \(I_x = I_y \Rightarrow I_{\text{diam}} = Ma^2/2\).
(c) By parallel axis theorem from diameter: \(I_t = I_{\text{diam}} + Ma^2 = Ma^2/2 + Ma^2 = 3Ma^2/2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Parallel Axis Theorem: Compound Application

A uniform disc of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\) has a circular hole of radius \(a/2\) cut from it, centred at distance \(a/2\) from the disc's centre. Find the MOI of the remaining body about the disc's central axis.

Solution

Mass of removed circle: \(m' = M\cdot\dfrac{\pi(a/2)^2}{\pi a^2} = M/4\). MOI of full disc: \(I_{\text{full}} = Ma^2/2\). MOI of removed disc about its own centre: \((M/4)(a/2)^2/2 = Ma^2/32\). By parallel axis theorem, MOI of removed disc about main centre: \(Ma^2/32 + (M/4)(a/2)^2 = Ma^2/32 + Ma^2/16 = 3Ma^2/32\). MOI of remaining body: \(Ma^2/2 - 3Ma^2/32 = 16Ma^2/32 - 3Ma^2/32 = 13Ma^2/32\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2009 — Perpendicular Axis Theorem Application

Find the MOI of a square lamina of side \(2a\) and mass \(M\) about (a) an axis through centre perpendicular to lamina, (b) a diagonal.

Solution

(a) \(I_z = M(a^2+a^2)/3 = 2Ma^2/3\) (rectangular formula with both sides \(= 2a\)).
(b) By symmetry, the two diagonals are equivalent axes in the plane: \(I_{d1} = I_{d2}\). By perpendicular axis theorem: \(I_z = I_{d1}+I_{d2} = 2I_d \Rightarrow I_d = I_z/2 = Ma^2/3\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — MOI of Annular Disc

Find the MOI of an annular disc (washer) of mass \(M\), inner radius \(a\), outer radius \(b\), about its axis of symmetry.

Solution

\(I = \int_a^b r^2\,dm\). (Using \(b^4-a^4 = (b^2-a^2)(b^2+a^2)\).) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Kinetic Energy of Rolling Sphere

A solid sphere of mass \(M\) and radius \(a\) rolls without slipping on a horizontal surface with velocity \(v\). Find its total kinetic energy.

Solution

Rolling: angular velocity \(\omega = v/a\). Translational KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}Mv^2\). Rotational KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2Ma^2}{5}\cdot\dfrac{v^2}{a^2} = \dfrac{Mv^2}{5}\). Total KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}Mv^2 + \dfrac{Mv^2}{5} = \dfrac{7Mv^2}{10}\). Note: \(7/10\) of KE is in translation, \(2/7\) of total in rotation. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Angular Momentum and Torque

State and prove: \(\dfrac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\tau}\) where \(\mathbf{L}\) is angular momentum and \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) is the torque.

Solution

For a particle: \(\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r}\times m\mathbf{v}\). \(\dfrac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \dot{\mathbf{r}}\times m\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{r}\times m\dot{\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{v}\times m\mathbf{v} + \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0} + \boldsymbol{\tau} = \boldsymbol{\tau}\). (Since \(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}\).) For a rigid body: sum over all particles, internal forces cancel in pairs (by Newton's 3rd law): \(\dfrac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\text{ext}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Compound Pendulum: Minimum Period

Show that the period of a compound pendulum is \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{(k^2+h^2)/(gh)}\) where \(k\) is the radius of gyration about the CM and \(h\) the distance from CM to pivot. Find \(h\) for minimum period.

Solution

\(I_O = Mk^2 + Mh^2\). Equation of rotation: \((Mk^2+Mh^2)\ddot\theta = -Mgh\sin\theta \approx -Mgh\theta\). Period \(T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{k^2+h^2}{gh}}\). To minimize: \(\dfrac{d}{dh}\!\left(\dfrac{k^2+h^2}{h}\right) = 0 \Rightarrow \dfrac{d}{dh}(k^2/h+h)=0 \Rightarrow -k^2/h^2+1=0 \Rightarrow h=k\). Minimum period: \(T_{\min} = 2\pi\sqrt{2k/g}\). This equals the period of a simple pendulum of length \(2k\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Centre of Percussion

Define centre of percussion. Show that for a uniform rod pivoted at one end, the centre of percussion is at \(2l/3\) from the pivot, where \(l\) is the length of the rod.

Solution

Centre of percussion (CP): point such that a blow there produces no impulsive reaction at the pivot. For pivot at \(O\), if blow \(P\) acts at distance \(d\) from \(O\): impulse equation: \(P = M\bar{v}\) (translation of CM) and \(Pd = I_O\omega\) (rotation about \(O\)). CP condition: \(\bar{v} = \omega\bar{h}\) (CM moves with rod) — which gives \(d = I_O/(M\bar{h})\) where \(\bar{h}\) = distance of CM from \(O\). For rod: \(I_O = Ml^2/3\), \(\bar{h} = l/2\). \(d = \dfrac{Ml^2/3}{M\cdot l/2} = \dfrac{2l}{3}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Cylinder Rolling Down Incline

A solid cylinder and a hollow cylinder (same mass and radius) start from rest and roll down an incline. Which reaches the bottom first, and what are their accelerations?

Solution

For any rolling body: \(a = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1 + I/(Ma^2)}\). Solid cylinder: \(I = Ma^2/2\), \(a_s = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1+1/2} = \dfrac{2g\sin\theta}{3}\). Hollow cylinder: \(I = Ma^2\), \(a_h = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{1+1} = \dfrac{g\sin\theta}{2}\). Since \(a_s > a_h\): solid cylinder reaches bottom first. (Compare: sphere has \(a = 5g\sin\theta/7 > 2g\sin\theta/3\) — sphere fastest.) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Flywheel: Rotational Motion Under Torque

A flywheel of MOI \(I\) starts from rest and is acted on by a constant couple (torque) \(\Gamma\). Find the angular velocity after \(t\) seconds and the KE acquired.

Solution

Equation of motion: \(I\dot\omega = \Gamma\). Starting from rest: \(\omega = \Gamma t/I\). KE \(= \tfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \dfrac{\Gamma^2t^2}{2I}\). Alternatively, KE = work done by couple = \(\Gamma\cdot\theta\) where \(\theta = \tfrac{1}{2}\omega t = \tfrac{\Gamma t^2}{2I}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Conservation of Angular Momentum

A man stands on a frictionless rotating platform with arms outstretched, spinning at \(\omega_0\). He pulls his arms in, reducing his MOI from \(I_1\) to \(I_2\). Find his new angular velocity. What happens to KE?

Solution

No external torque: \(L = I\omega =\) constant. \(I_1\omega_0 = I_2\omega \Rightarrow \omega = \dfrac{I_1\omega_0}{I_2}\). Since \(I_2 < I_1\): \(\omega > \omega_0\) (spins faster). KE ratio: \(\dfrac{KE_2}{KE_1} = \dfrac{I_2\omega^2/2}{I_1\omega_0^2/2} = \dfrac{I_2(I_1/I_2)^2}{I_1} = \dfrac{I_1}{I_2} > 1\). KE increases — the man does work pulling his arms in. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Equation of Motion for Rotation

A uniform rod of mass \(M\) and length \(2l\) is free to rotate about a horizontal axis through one end. It is released from horizontal position. Find the angular acceleration and speed when it reaches the vertical.

Solution

Let \(\theta\) = angle with horizontal. Torque of gravity about pivot: \(\Gamma = Mgl\cos\theta\). MOI: \(I = \dfrac{M(2l)^2}{3} = \dfrac{4Ml^2}{3}\). EOM: \(\dfrac{4Ml^2}{3}\ddot\theta = Mgl\cos\theta \Rightarrow \ddot\theta = \dfrac{3g\cos\theta}{4l}\). At release (\(\theta=0\)): \(\ddot\theta = 3g/(4l)\). Speed at vertical (\(\theta=90°\)): by energy conservation, \(\dfrac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = Mgl\) (CM drops by \(l\)). \(\omega = \sqrt{\dfrac{2Mgl}{4Ml^2/3}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3g}{2l}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Intrinsic Equation of Catenary

Derive the intrinsic equation of the catenary and show that \(s = c\tan\psi\) where \(s\) is arc length from vertex and \(\psi\) is the angle the tangent makes with the horizontal.

Solution

From the equilibrium: \(T\sin\psi = ws\) and \(T\cos\psi = T_0 = wc\). Dividing: \(\tan\psi = s/c \Rightarrow s = c\tan\psi\). This is the intrinsic equation. Also: \(T = wc\sec\psi = wy\) and \(\dfrac{ds}{d\psi} = c\sec^2\psi\). Radius of curvature: \(\rho = ds/d\psi = c\sec^2\psi = s/\sin\psi\cdot(1/\cos\psi) \cdot\cos^2\psi = c/\cos^2\psi\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Catenary: Tension at Any Point

For a uniform chain hanging in catenary form, show that the tension at any point equals the weight of a length of chain equal to the ordinate \(y\) at that point: \(T = wy\).

Solution

From equilibrium: \(T\cos\psi = T_0 = wc\) and \(T\sin\psi = ws\). So \(T^2 = T_0^2 + w^2s^2 = w^2c^2 + w^2s^2 = w^2(c^2+s^2)\). From catenary: \(y = c\cosh(x/c)\) and \(s = c\sinh(x/c)\), so \(y^2 = c^2\cosh^2(x/c) = c^2(1+\sinh^2(x/c)) = c^2+s^2\). Therefore \(T = w\sqrt{c^2+s^2} = wy\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Catenary: Length and Span

A uniform chain of length \(2l\) hangs between two pegs at the same height, distant \(2d\) apart. Show that the parameter \(c\) satisfies \(l = c\sinh(d/c)\).

Solution

Origin at vertex of catenary (lowest point). Pegs are at \(x = \pm d\). Arc length from vertex to either peg: \(s = c\sinh(x/c)\). Half-length of chain \(= c\sinh(d/c)\). Total length \(= 2c\sinh(d/c) = 2l \Rightarrow l = c\sinh(d/c)\). Given \(l\) and \(d\), this transcendental equation determines \(c\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Catenary: Semi-Vertical Angle and Tension at Ends

A chain of weight \(W\) and length \(2s\) hangs from two fixed points at the same level. If the lowest point is at depth \(d\) below the fixed points, find the tension at the ends.

Solution

Tension at end: \(T_{\text{end}} = wy_{\text{end}}\) where \(y_{\text{end}}\) is the ordinate of the end. Since \(c + d = y_{\text{end}}\) (vertex at height \(c\), ends at height \(c+d\)): \(T_{\text{end}} = w(c+d)\). Also \(T_{\text{end}}\sin\psi = ws/2\) (half-chain weight) and total weight \(W = ws\). So \(T_{\text{end}}\sin\psi = W/2\). Tension at ends: \(T_{\text{end}} = \dfrac{W}{2}\csc\psi\) where \(\tan\psi = s/c\) and \(c\) is found from \(s = c\sinh(\text{half-span}/c)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Uniform Chain on Smooth Surface

A uniform chain of length \(l\) lies partly on a smooth table, the rest hanging over the edge. If initially a length \(a\) hangs, find the time for the chain to slide off, assuming it starts from rest.

Solution

Let \(x\) = length hanging at time \(t\). Total mass \(m = \rho l\), hanging mass \(\rho x\). EOM: \(\rho l\ddot{x} = \rho xg \Rightarrow \ddot{x} = (g/l)x\). General solution: \(x = Ae^{nt}+Be^{-nt}\) where \(n = \sqrt{g/l}\). ICs: \(x(0)=a\), \(\dot{x}(0)=0 \Rightarrow A=B=a/2\). So \(x = a\cosh(nt)\). Chain leaves when \(x=l\): \(a\cosh(nt_1)=l \Rightarrow t_1 = \dfrac{1}{n}\cosh^{-1}(l/a) = \sqrt{l/g}\,\cosh^{-1}(l/a)\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Statics: Centre of Gravity (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Centre of Gravity of Solid Hemisphere

Find the centre of gravity of a solid hemisphere of radius \(a\) from its flat base.

Solution

Take axis along axis of symmetry, origin at centre of flat face. Disc at height \(z\): radius \(\sqrt{a^2-z^2}\), mass \(dm = \rho\pi(a^2-z^2)dz\). \(\bar{z} = \dfrac{\int_0^a z\,dm}{\int_0^a dm} = \dfrac{\rho\pi\int_0^a z(a^2-z^2)\,dz}{\rho\pi\int_0^a(a^2-z^2)\,dz}\). Numerator: \(\int_0^a(a^2z-z^3)\,dz = \dfrac{a^4}{2}-\dfrac{a^4}{4}=\dfrac{a^4}{4}\). Denominator: \(\int_0^a(a^2-z^2)\,dz = a^3 - a^3/3 = 2a^3/3\). \(\bar{z} = \dfrac{a^4/4}{2a^3/3} = \dfrac{3a}{8}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Centre of Gravity of Solid Cone

Find the CG of a solid right circular cone of height \(h\) from its base.

Solution

Measure \(z\) from apex. At height \(z\): radius \(= Rz/h\), disc mass \(dm = \rho\pi(Rz/h)^2dz\). \(\bar{z} = \dfrac{\int_0^h z\,dm}{\int_0^h dm} = \dfrac{\int_0^h z\cdot(Rz/h)^2\,dz}{\int_0^h(Rz/h)^2\,dz} = \dfrac{\int_0^h z^3\,dz}{\int_0^h z^2\,dz} = \dfrac{h^4/4}{h^3/3} = \dfrac{3h}{4}\) from apex, i.e., \(\dfrac{h}{4}\) from base. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Centre of Gravity of Circular Arc

Find the CG of a uniform circular arc of radius \(r\) subtending angle \(2\alpha\) at the centre.

Solution

Place arc symmetrically about \(x\)-axis. Element at angle \(\theta\): \(dm = \rho r\,d\theta\). \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha} r\cos\theta\cdot\rho r\,d\theta}{\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\rho r\,d\theta} = \dfrac{r\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\cos\theta\,d\theta}{2\alpha} = \dfrac{r\cdot2\sin\alpha}{2\alpha} = \dfrac{r\sin\alpha}{\alpha}\). For semicircle (\(\alpha=\pi/2\)): \(\bar{x} = 2r/\pi\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Centre of Gravity of Sector of Circle

Find the CG of a uniform sector of a circle of radius \(r\), angle \(2\alpha\).

Solution

Consider sector as composed of triangles. For a thin triangle at angle \(\theta\): base \(r\,d\theta\), height \(r\), area \(\tfrac{1}{2}r^2d\theta\), CG at \((2r/3)\cos\theta\) from centre. \(\bar{x} = \dfrac{\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\,d\theta\cdot\dfrac{2r}{3}\cos\theta}{\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\,d\theta} = \dfrac{\dfrac{r}{3}\int_{-\alpha}^{\alpha}\cos\theta\,d\theta}{\alpha} = \dfrac{r\cdot 2\sin\alpha}{3\alpha} = \dfrac{2r\sin\alpha}{3\alpha}\). For semicircle (\(\alpha=\pi/2\)): \(\bar{x} = 4r/(3\pi)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Lami's Theorem

State and prove Lami's Theorem: if three concurrent coplanar forces are in equilibrium, each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two.

Solution

Statement: If \(\mathbf{F}_1, \mathbf{F}_2, \mathbf{F}_3\) are in equilibrium and \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are the angles opposite to them (between the other two), then \(\dfrac{F_1}{\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{F_2}{\sin\beta}=\dfrac{F_3}{\sin\gamma}\). Proof: \(\mathbf{F}_1+\mathbf{F}_2+\mathbf{F}_3=\mathbf{0}\Rightarrow\mathbf{F}_3=-(\mathbf{F}_1+\mathbf{F}_2)\). By the sine rule in the triangle of forces (which closes since sum is zero): \(\dfrac{F_1}{\sin(\pi-\alpha)}=\dfrac{F_2}{\sin(\pi-\beta)}=\dfrac{F_3}{\sin(\pi-\gamma)}\Rightarrow\dfrac{F_1}{\sin\alpha}=\dfrac{F_2}{\sin\beta}=\dfrac{F_3}{\sin\gamma}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Couple and Its Properties

Define a couple. Show that the moment of a couple is the same about any point in its plane. Show that a couple cannot be reduced to a single force.

Solution

Couple: Two equal and opposite parallel forces (\(\pm\mathbf{F}\) at distance \(d\) apart). Moment about any point \(P\): Let forces act at \(A, B\). Moment \(= F\cdot PA\sin\theta_1 + F\cdot PB\sin\theta_2\) (taking signs): \(= F\cdot d\) (perpendicular distance) regardless of position of \(P\). Cannot be reduced to single force: Resultant force \(= \mathbf{F}+(-\mathbf{F}) = \mathbf{0}\). A single non-zero force has zero moment about points on its line of action — but a couple has the same non-zero moment everywhere. Hence no single force can replace a couple. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Resultant of Coplanar Force System

A system of coplanar forces acts on a rigid body. Show that it reduces to either a single resultant force or a couple.

Solution

Reduce all forces to a single force \(\mathbf{R} = \sum\mathbf{F}_i\) at origin, plus a couple \(G = \sum(\mathbf{r}_i\times\mathbf{F}_i)\) (the sum of moments about origin). Case 1: \(\mathbf{R}\neq\mathbf{0}\). The couple \(G\) can be transferred as a shift in the line of action of \(\mathbf{R}\). Shift by \(d = G/R\): system reduces to single force \(\mathbf{R}\). Case 2: \(\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}\). System is a pure couple of moment \(G\). (If both \(\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}\) and \(G=0\): equilibrium.) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2009 — Three Forces: Triangle of Forces

Three forces act along the sides of a triangle \(ABC\), proportional to the sides \(a, b, c\). Show they are equivalent to a couple and find its moment.

Solution

Forces \(\lambda a, \lambda b, \lambda c\) along \(BC, CA, AB\) (all in same sense). Resultant force \(\mathbf{R} = \lambda(a\hat{BC}+b\hat{CA}+c\hat{AB})\). The vectors \(a\hat{BC}+b\hat{CA}+c\hat{AB} = \overrightarrow{BC}+\overrightarrow{CA}+\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{0}\) (closed triangle). So \(\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{0}\): system is a couple. Moment about any vertex (say \(A\)): \(G = \lambda a\cdot h_a\) where \(h_a\) = perpendicular from \(A\) to \(BC\). But \(ah_a = 2\Delta\) (twice area of triangle). Moment \(G = 2\lambda\Delta\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Friction: Extended (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Cone of Friction

Define the cone of friction. A force is applied to a body on a rough surface. Show that equilibrium is possible if and only if the applied force lies within the cone of friction.

Solution

Cone of friction: The cone with its axis along the normal to the surface and semi-vertical angle \(\lambda = \arctan\mu\) (angle of friction). The resultant reaction \(R\) can make any angle up to \(\lambda\) with the normal. Equilibrium condition: The applied force \(F\) must be balanced by \(R\). For equilibrium: \(|F|\sin\phi \leq \mu|F|\cos\phi\) where \(\phi\) is the angle of \(F\) with normal, i.e., \(\tan\phi\leq\mu\), i.e., \(\phi\leq\lambda\). So equilibrium holds iff \(F\) (and hence \(R\)) lies within the cone of friction. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Angle of Friction and Angle of Repose

Define angle of friction \(\lambda\). Show that the angle of repose of a body on an inclined plane equals the angle of friction.

Solution

Angle of friction \(\lambda\): \(\tan\lambda = \mu\) (coefficient of limiting friction). Angle of repose \(\alpha\): steepest angle at which body remains in equilibrium on incline. At angle \(\alpha\): friction force \(= mg\sin\alpha\), normal reaction \(= mg\cos\alpha\). At limiting equilibrium: \(\dfrac{mg\sin\alpha}{mg\cos\alpha} = \mu = \tan\lambda \Rightarrow \alpha = \lambda\). Hence angle of repose \(=\) angle of friction. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Ladder on Rough Wall and Floor

A uniform ladder of length \(2l\) and weight \(W\) rests against a rough vertical wall (coefficient \(\mu_1\)) and rough horizontal floor (coefficient \(\mu_2\)). Both surfaces are rough. Find the equilibrium condition.

Solution

Let \(R\) = normal at floor, \(S\) = normal at wall, \(\theta\) = angle with floor. Friction at floor: \(\mu_2R\) (horizontal), at wall: \(\mu_1S\) (vertical). Equilibrium: Horizontal: \(S = \mu_2R\). Vertical: \(R + \mu_1S = W \Rightarrow R(1+\mu_1\mu_2) = W \Rightarrow R = \dfrac{W}{1+\mu_1\mu_2}\). Moments about foot: \(W\cdot l\cos\theta = S\cdot2l\sin\theta + \mu_1S\cdot2l\cos\theta\). Substituting \(S = \mu_2R\): \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{W-2\mu_1S}{2S} = \dfrac{W-2\mu_1\mu_2R}{2\mu_2R} = \dfrac{1-\mu_1\mu_2}{2\mu_2}\). Minimum angle: \(\theta_{\min} = \arctan\dfrac{1-\mu_1\mu_2}{2\mu_2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Block on Inclined Plane with Friction

A block of mass \(m\) is placed on an inclined plane of angle \(\alpha\). The coefficient of friction is \(\mu = \tan\phi\). Find the range of angles \(\theta\) of a force \(P\) applied to the block (measured from the plane) such that the block is on the verge of moving up or down.

Solution

Verge of moving up: \(P = \dfrac{mg\sin(\alpha+\phi)}{\cos(\theta-\phi)}\) (minimized when \(\theta=\phi\), giving \(P_{\min}=mg\sin(\alpha+\phi)\)).
Verge of moving down: \(P = \dfrac{mg\sin(\phi-\alpha)}{\cos(\theta+\phi)}\) (if \(\phi>\alpha\); otherwise block doesn't tend to slide down).
The force is least when applied at angle \(\theta = \phi\) to the plane (i.e., at angle of friction to normal). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Least Force to Move a Heavy Body

A heavy body of weight \(W\) rests on a rough horizontal surface (\(\mu\)). Find the least force required to move it and its direction.

Solution

Apply force \(P\) at angle \(\theta\) above horizontal. Normal: \(R = W - P\sin\theta\). Friction: \(\mu R = \mu(W-P\sin\theta)\). For motion: \(P\cos\theta = \mu(W-P\sin\theta) \Rightarrow P(\cos\theta+\mu\sin\theta) = \mu W \Rightarrow P = \dfrac{\mu W}{\cos\theta+\mu\sin\theta}\). Minimize \(P\): \(\dfrac{dP}{d\theta}=0 \Rightarrow \sin\theta = \mu\cos\theta \Rightarrow \tan\theta = \mu \Rightarrow \theta = \lambda\) (angle of friction). \(P_{\min} = \dfrac{\mu W}{\sqrt{1+\mu^2}} = W\sin\lambda\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Wedge Problem

A wedge of angle \(\alpha\) is used to split a log. A force \(P\) is applied at the top. If \(\mu\) is the coefficient of friction between the wedge and the log, find the force exerted on the log.

Solution

By wedge mechanics: \(P\) drives the wedge, producing normal reactions \(N\) on each face of wedge. On each face: resultant reaction makes angle \(\lambda\) (angle of friction) with normal. Resolving vertical: \(P = 2N(\sin\alpha\cos\lambda + \cos\alpha\sin\lambda) = 2N\sin(\alpha+\lambda)\)... wait — for symmetric wedge: \(P = 2N\sin(\alpha/2+\lambda)\cdot...\). Simplified: force on log from each side \(N' = N\). Horizontal force splitting the log: \(F = 2N\cos(\alpha/2)\). From above \(N = \dfrac{P}{2\sin(\alpha/2+\lambda)}\) (approximately). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Equilibrium on Rough Incline: Two Bodies

Two equal weights are connected by a string passing over a rough peg at the top of an inclined plane of angle \(\alpha\). One weight hangs vertically, the other rests on the incline (\(\mu\)). Find the condition for equilibrium.

Solution

Tension \(T\) same throughout string (over rough peg — but assume smooth peg here). Weight on incline: \(T = W\sin\alpha \pm \mu W\cos\alpha\) (depending on tendency to slide). Hanging weight: \(T = W\). For equilibrium: \(W = W\sin\alpha \pm \mu W\cos\alpha \Rightarrow 1 = \sin\alpha \pm \mu\cos\alpha \Rightarrow \mu = \dfrac{1-\sin\alpha}{\cos\alpha} = \tan(45°-\alpha/2)\). Equilibrium is possible for \(\mu\geq\tan(\pi/4-\alpha/2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Principle of Virtual Work: Jointed Rod

Two uniform rods \(AB\) and \(BC\), each of weight \(W\) and length \(2a\), are freely jointed at \(B\). \(A\) and \(C\) rest on a smooth horizontal surface. The system is kept in equilibrium by a string \(AC\). Find the tension in the string using virtual work.

Solution

Let \(\theta\) = angle each rod makes with vertical. By symmetry: \(AC = 4a\sin\theta\), CG of each rod at height \(a\cos\theta\). Virtual displacement: \(\delta\theta\). \(\delta(AC) = 4a\cos\theta\,\delta\theta\). Work by tension \(T\): \(-T\cdot\delta(AC) = -4Ta\cos\theta\,\delta\theta\). Work by gravity: both CGs drop, \(\delta(a\cos\theta) = -a\sin\theta\,\delta\theta\), so work done by gravity on each rod \(= W\cdot a\sin\theta\,\delta\theta\). Total work \(= 0\): \(2Wa\sin\theta = 4Ta\cos\theta \Rightarrow T = \dfrac{W\tan\theta}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Virtual Work: Framework Equilibrium

A light rod \(AB\) of length \(l\) is pinned at \(A\). A spring of stiffness \(k\) connects the midpoint \(C\) of \(AB\) to a fixed point \(D\) directly below \(A\) at distance \(l/2\). A load \(W\) hangs from \(B\). Find the equilibrium angle \(\theta\) of \(AB\) with vertical using virtual work.

Solution

Let \(\theta\) = angle with vertical. \(B\) is at depth \(l\cos\theta\) below \(A\). \(C\) is at depth \(l\cos\theta/2\) and horizontal distance \(l\sin\theta/2\) from \(A\). Distance \(CD = \sqrt{(l\sin\theta/2)^2+(l\cos\theta/2-l/2)^2}\). Virtual work principle: \(W\delta(l\cos\theta) + F_{\text{spring}}\delta(CD) = 0\). For small \(\theta\): \(CD \approx l\theta/2\), \(F = kl\theta/2\). \(-Wl\sin\theta + kl\theta/2\cdot l/2 = 0 \Rightarrow Wl\sin\theta = kl^2\theta/4\). For small \(\theta\): \(\sin\theta\approx\theta\), giving equilibrium at \(\theta=0\) or solving exactly numerically. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Virtual Work: Three-Bar Linkage

State the principle of virtual work for a system in equilibrium. Apply it to find the force \(P\) needed to hold a toggle joint (two equal rods of length \(l\) at angle \(\theta\) to horizontal) against a horizontal force \(Q\) at the joint.

Solution

Principle: For a system in equilibrium, the total virtual work of all applied forces for any virtual displacement consistent with constraints is zero. Toggle joint: The joint at angle \(\theta$: horizontal extent \(= 2l\cos\theta\), vertical extent \(= l\sin\theta\). Applied forces: \(P\) vertical at the joint, \(Q\) horizontal at endpoints. Virtual displacement \(\delta\theta\): \(\delta(\text{vertical}) = l\cos\theta\,\delta\theta\), \(\delta(\text{horizontal spread}) = -2l\sin\theta\,\delta\theta\). Virtual work: \(P\cdot l\cos\theta\,\delta\theta + Q\cdot(-2l\sin\theta\,\delta\theta) = 0 \Rightarrow P = 2Q\tan\theta\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Virtual Work: Stability of Equilibrium

A uniform sphere of weight \(W\) and radius \(r\) rests in a spherical bowl of radius \(R>r\). Using virtual work (or energy method), show the equilibrium is stable.

Solution

When sphere is displaced by angle \(\theta\) from bottom of bowl: centre of sphere is at radius \(R-r\) from bowl's centre. Height of sphere's centre above the lowest position: \(h = (R-r)(1-\cos\theta) > 0\) for \(\theta\neq0\). PE increases with displacement: \(V = W(R-r)(1-\cos\theta)\). \(d^2V/d\theta^2 = W(R-r)\cos\theta > 0\) at \(\theta=0\). Since PE has a local minimum at equilibrium, the equilibrium is stable. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Virtual Work: Loaded Chain

A uniform heavy chain of weight \(W\) is suspended from two points at the same height. A load \(P\) is attached at the lowest point. Using virtual work, show the lowest point does not shift horizontally (by symmetry argument).

Solution

Consider virtual displacement that moves the lowest point horizontally by \(\delta x\) (keeping chain length fixed). By the symmetry of the system (both supports at same height, load at midpoint), such a displacement is not consistent with the constraint of equal arc lengths on both sides. Any symmetric loading with equal span produces a symmetric catenary — the lowest point is at the midpoint. Formal check: the virtual work of all vertical forces (gravity and \(P\)) for a purely horizontal virtual displacement of the load is zero, so horizontal position is indeterminate by virtual work — but it is fixed by the symmetry constraint of equal arc lengths. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Method of Joints: Simple Truss

A triangular truss has joints at \(A\) (pin), \(B\) (roller), and \(C\) (apex). \(AB\) is horizontal (length \(4\) m), \(AC = BC = 3\) m (isoceles). A vertical load \(W\) acts at \(C\). Find forces in all members using method of joints.

Solution

Reactions: by symmetry, \(R_A = R_B = W/2\) (vertical). Height of truss: \(h = \sqrt{9-4} = \sqrt{5}\) m. Angle \(\theta = \arctan(\sqrt{5}/2)\) with horizontal. Joint \(C\): Two members \(CA, CB\) and load \(W\). Resolving vertical: \(F_{CA}\sin\theta + F_{CB}\sin\theta = W\). By symmetry: \(F_{CA} = F_{CB} = F\). \(2F\sin\theta = W \Rightarrow F = W/(2\sin\theta) = W\sqrt{29}/(2\sqrt{5})\). Horizontal: \(F_{CA}\cos\theta = F_{CB}\cos\theta\) (balanced). Both \(CA, CB\) are in compression. Joint \(A\): \(R_A = W/2\) up, \(F_{CA}\sin\theta = W/2\) (checks out). Horizontal: \(F_{AB} = F_{CA}\cos\theta = \dfrac{W\cos\theta}{2\sin\theta} = \dfrac{W}{2}\cot\theta\) (tension). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Bow's Notation

Explain Bow's notation for space diagrams and force diagrams in structural analysis. How are forces in members determined from the force polygon?

Solution

Bow's notation: Each space (region) in the space diagram is labelled with a capital letter. Each member or external force separates two spaces, and is denoted by the two letters of the adjacent spaces (e.g., member \(AB\) separates spaces \(A\) and \(B\)). Force polygon: Going around each joint in a consistent sense (clockwise), the forces are represented as vectors drawn in sequence. The force polygon closes for equilibrium. Each member force \(AB\) is read from the polygon as the vector from point \(a\) to point \(b\) (lowercase letters are the corresponding points in the force diagram). If traversal gives \(a\to b\) pointing toward the joint, the member is in compression; away — in tension. This provides a graphical method to find all member forces simultaneously. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Forces in Members: Pratt Truss

In a simple Pratt truss with two panels, top chord \(AC\), \(CE\) (each length \(l\)), bottom chord \(AB\), \(BD\) (each \(l\)), verticals \(BC\) and \(DE\), and diagonal \(CD\). Vertical loads \(W\) at \(B\) and \(D\). Find the force in member \(CD\) using method of sections.

Solution

Reactions: \(R_A = R_E = W\) (by symmetry, total load \(2W\), equal reactions). Method of sections: cut through \(CD\), \(CE\), \(BD\). Consider right portion (joints \(D, E\)): External forces: \(R_E = W\) up, load \(W\) down at \(D\). Net vertical = 0. Take moments about \(E\): \(F_{BD}\cdot h - W\cdot l = 0\) (where \(h\) = height of truss). \(F_{BD} = Wl/h\) (tension). Vertical equilibrium of right portion: \(F_{CD}\sin\alpha + W - W = 0 \Rightarrow F_{CD} = 0\). (Zero-force member in symmetric loading case.) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Warren Girder

A Warren girder consists of equilateral triangles. With span \(3l\) and two equal loads \(W\) at the lower panel points, find the forces in the top chord members using method of joints.

Solution

Equilateral triangles: each side \(l\), height \(l\sqrt{3}/2\). Reactions: \(R_A = R_B = W\) (by symmetry). Label: bottom joints \(A, C, E, B\); top joints \(D, F\). Loads \(W\) at \(C\) and \(E\). Joint \(A\): \(R_A = W\) up. Members: \(AC\) (bottom, horizontal), \(AD\) (diagonal at 60°). Vertical: \(F_{AD}\sin60° = W \Rightarrow F_{AD} = 2W/\sqrt{3}\) (compression). Horizontal: \(F_{AC} = F_{AD}\cos60° = W/\sqrt{3}\) (tension). Joint \(D\): members \(AD, DC, DF\). \(F_{DF} = F_{AD} = 2W/\sqrt{3}\) (compression by symmetry of loading). Top chord forces: \(2W/\sqrt{3}\) compression throughout. \(\blacksquare\)
