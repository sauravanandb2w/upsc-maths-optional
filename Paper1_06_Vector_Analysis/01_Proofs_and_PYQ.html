§ Proofs — Gradient, Divergence, Curl

\(\nabla^2(r^n) = n(n+1)r^{n-2}\) — Proof
Proof

\(r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2\). \(\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial x}=\dfrac{x}{r}\). \(\dfrac{\partial(r^n)}{\partial x}=nr^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{x}{r}=nr^{n-2}x\).

\(\dfrac{\partial^2(r^n)}{\partial x^2}=n(n-2)r^{n-3}\cdot\dfrac{x}{r}\cdot x+nr^{n-2}=n(n-2)r^{n-4}x^2+nr^{n-2}\).

Sum over \(x,y,z\): \(\nabla^2(r^n)=n(n-2)r^{n-4}(x^2+y^2+z^2)+3nr^{n-2}=n(n-2)r^{n-2}+3nr^{n-2}=nr^{n-2}(n-2+3)=n(n+1)r^{n-2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary: \(n=0\Rightarrow\nabla^2(1)=0\). \(n=-1\Rightarrow\nabla^2(1/r)=(-1)(0)/r^3=0\) for \(r\ne0\). \(n=2\Rightarrow\nabla^2(r^2)=6\).

Curl of a Gradient is Zero: \(\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=0\) — Proof
Proof

\((\nabla\times\nabla\phi)_x=\dfrac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y\partial z}-\dfrac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z\partial y}=0\) (equality of mixed partials). Similarly for \(y,z\) components. \(\blacksquare\)

Divergence of a Curl is Zero: \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)=0\) — Proof
Proof

\(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(F_z^y-F_y^z)+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(F_x^z-F_z^x)+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(F_y^x-F_x^y)\) where \(F_i^j=\partial F_i/\partial j\). By equality of mixed partials all terms cancel in pairs. \(\blacksquare\)

Conservative Field Equivalences — Proof

For a smooth vector field \(\mathbf F\) on a simply connected domain, the following are equivalent: (i) \(\mathbf F=\nabla\phi\) for some scalar \(\phi\), (ii) \(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=0\) for every closed curve \(C\), (iii) \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf0\).

Proof of (iii) ⟹ (i) by construction

Define \(\phi(x,y,z)=\int_{\mathbf r_0}^{(x,y,z)}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) (path-independent because \(\text{curl}\,\mathbf F=0\) in simply connected domain → by Stokes any two paths give same integral). Then \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}=F_1\), \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}=F_2\), \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}=F_3\) (by FTC). So \(\nabla\phi=\mathbf F\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Green's Theorem — Proof

Green's Theorem

\(\oint_C(P\,dx+Q\,dy)=\iint_R\!\left(\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA\).

Proof (for Type I and Type II regions)

Type I (vertical strips): Show \(-\oint_C P\,dx=\iint_R\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dA\).

For region \(R=\{(x,y):a\le x\le b,g_1(x)\le y\le g_2(x)\}\):

\(\iint_R\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dA=\int_a^b\!\int_{g_1}^{g_2}\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dy\,dx=\int_a^b[P(x,g_2(x))-P(x,g_1(x))]\,dx\).

The closed contour: bottom (\(C_1\): \(y=g_1\), left to right) + right (\(C_2\)) + top (\(C_3\): \(y=g_2\), right to left) + left (\(C_4\)). \(-\oint_C P\,dx=\int_a^b P(x,g_2)dx-\int_a^b P(x,g_1)dx=\iint\partial P/\partial y\,dA\). \(\blacksquare\)

Similarly: show \(\oint_C Q\,dy=\iint_R\partial Q/\partial x\,dA\) via Type II region. Add both. \(\blacksquare\)

Area via Green's Theorem

Area \(= \dfrac{1}{2}\oint_C(x\,dy-y\,dx)\). (Set \(Q=x/2,P=-y/2\): \(Q_x-P_y=1\).)

§ Stokes' Theorem — Proof

Stokes' Theorem

\(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\).

Proof (via Green's Theorem, for surface z = g(x,y))

Let \(S:z=g(x,y)\) over region \(R_{xy}\). \(d\mathbf S=(-g_x,-g_y,1)\,dA\). Let \(\mathbf F=(P,Q,R)\). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=(R_y-Q_z,P_z-R_x,Q_x-P_y)\). So \((\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S=(-g_x(R_y-Q_z)-g_y(P_z-R_x)+(Q_x-P_y))\,dA\).

Also, \(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\oint_{C_{xy}}[P+Rg_x]\,dx+[Q+Rg_y]\,dy\). Apply Green's theorem to this (in the \(xy\)-plane): the result equals \(\iint_{R_{xy}}(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\). (Detailed verification uses chain rule to expand all partial derivatives.) \(\blacksquare\)

§ Gauss's Divergence Theorem — Proof

Gauss's Divergence Theorem

\(\oiint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint_V(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)\,dV\).

Proof (for box-like region, then generalized)

It suffices to show \(\oiint_S R\hat k\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint_V\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial z}\,dV\) (and similarly for \(P,Q\)).

For region \(z_1(x,y)\le z\le z_2(x,y)\) over \(R_{xy}\): \(\iiint\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial z}\,dV=\iint_{R_{xy}}[R(x,y,z_2)-R(x,y,z_1)]\,dA\).

The surface integral on top surface (\(S_2:z=z_2\), outward normal = upward): \(\iint_{S_2}R\hat k\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iint R(x,y,z_2)\,dA\). On bottom (\(S_1:z=z_1\), outward normal = downward): \(\iint_{S_1}R\hat k\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iint R(x,y,z_1)(-1)\,dA=-\iint R(x,y,z_1)\,dA\). Adding: \(\oiint R\hat k\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint\partial R/\partial z\,dV\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Find a, b, c for Conservative Field

Find \(a,b,c\) so that \(\mathbf F=(2xy+z^3)\hat i+(x^2+2y)\hat j+(3xz^2+ay)\hat k\) wait — standard version: \(\mathbf F=(axy-z^3)\hat i+(a-2)x^2\hat j+(1-a)xz^2\hat k\) is irrotational. Find \(a\) and the potential.

Solution

Consistent with \(a=4\). So \(a=4\). Now \(\mathbf F=(4xy-z^3)\hat i+2x^2\hat j-3xz^2\hat k\). Find \(\phi\): \(\phi_x=4xy-z^3\Rightarrow\phi=2x^2y-xz^3+g(y,z)\). \(\phi_y=2x^2+g_y=2x^2\Rightarrow g_y=0\). \(\phi_z=-3xz^2+g_z=-3xz^2\Rightarrow g_z=0\). So \(\phi=2x^2y-xz^3+C\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Area via Green's Theorem

Find the area enclosed by the astroid \(x=a\cos^3t,y=a\sin^3t\) using Green's theorem.

Solution

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\oint(x\,dy-y\,dx)\). \(dx=-3a\cos^2t\sin t\,dt\), \(dy=3a\sin^2t\cos t\,dt\). \(x\,dy-y\,dx=a\cos^3t\cdot3a\sin^2t\cos t\,dt-a\sin^3t\cdot(-3a\cos^2t\sin t)\,dt=3a^2(\cos^4t\sin^2t+\sin^4t\cos^2t)\,dt=3a^2\sin^2t\cos^2t(\cos^2t+\sin^2t)\,dt=3a^2\sin^2t\cos^2t\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{4}\sin^2(2t)\,dt\).

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}\dfrac{3a^2}{4}\sin^2(2t)\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{8}\int_0^{2\pi}\dfrac{1-\cos4t}{2}\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{8}\cdot\pi=\dfrac{3\pi a^2}{8}\).

PYQ 2019 — Verify Green's Theorem

Verify Green's theorem for \(\oint_C(xy\,dx+x^2\,dy)\) where \(C\) is the boundary of the region bounded by \(y=x\) and \(y=x^2\).

PYQ 2023 — Stokes on Triangular Surface

Use Stokes' theorem to evaluate \(\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\) where \(\mathbf F=y\hat i+z\hat j+x\hat k\) and \(S\) is the part of the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\) in the first octant.

Surface is part of unit sphere in first octant (area \(\pi/2\) times something). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F\cdot\hat n\,dS\): on unit sphere, \(\hat n=(x,y,z)\). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F\cdot\hat n=-(x+y+z)\). \(\iint_S=-($ \iint(x+y+z)\,dS\) over first-octant sphere = \(-3\iint x\,dS=-3\cdot(\pi/2)\cdot... \) use symmetry: \(\bar x=1/2\) on hemisphere, area \(=2\pi\). First octant area \(=\pi/2\). \(\iint x\,dS=1/2\cdot\pi/2=\pi/4\) (Centroid). Total: \(-3\pi/4\). Answer: \(-3\pi/4\).

PYQ 2022 — Stokes' Theorem Verification

Verify Stokes' theorem for \(\mathbf F=(x^2-y^2)\hat i+2xy\hat j\) over the rectangle \(0\le x\le a,0\le y\le b\).

Solution

\(\nabla\times\mathbf F=\hat k(2y-(-2y))=4y\hat k\). Surface is rectangle in \(z=0\) plane, \(d\mathbf S=\hat k\,dA\). \(\iint(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S=\iint 4y\,dA=4\int_0^a\int_0^b y\,dy\,dx=4a\cdot\dfrac{b^2}{2}=2ab^2\).

Direct: On boundary (counterclockwise). Bottom (\(y=0\)): \(\int_0^a x^2\,dx=a^3/3\). Right (\(x=a\)): \(\int_0^b 2ay\,dy=ab^2\). Top (\(y=b\), reversed): \(-\int_0^a(x^2-b^2)\,dx=-(a^3/3-ab^2)=-a^3/3+ab^2\). Left (\(x=0\), reversed): \(-\int_0^b 0\,dy=0\). Total: \(a^3/3+ab^2-a^3/3+ab^2=2ab^2\). \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Work Done by Conservative Force

Show \(\mathbf F=(e^x\sin y)\hat i+(e^x\cos y)\hat j\) is conservative and find the work done from \((0,0)\) to \((1,\pi/2)\).

Solution

2D field: conservative iff \(\partial P/\partial y=\partial Q/\partial x\). \(\partial(e^x\sin y)/\partial y=e^x\cos y=\partial(e^x\cos y)/\partial x\). Conservative. Find potential: \(\phi_x=e^x\sin y\Rightarrow\phi=e^x\sin y+g(y)\). \(\phi_y=e^x\cos y+g'(y)=e^x\cos y\Rightarrow g'=0\Rightarrow\phi=e^x\sin y+C\). Work \(=\phi(1,\pi/2)-\phi(0,0)=(e^1\cdot1)-(e^0\cdot0)=e\).

PYQ 2022 — Potential Function from Curl = 0

If \(\mathbf F=(y^2+2xz^2)\hat i+(2xy)\hat j+(2x^2z+4z^3)\hat k\), show it's conservative and find \(\phi\).

Solution

Check curl: \((\nabla\times F)_x=\partial(2x^2z+4z^3)/\partial y-\partial(2xy)/\partial z=0-0=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_y=\partial(y^2+2xz^2)/\partial z-\partial(2x^2z+4z^3)/\partial x=4xz-4xz=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_z=\partial(2xy)/\partial x-\partial(y^2+2xz^2)/\partial y=2y-2y=0\). Conservative. Find \(\phi\): \(\phi_x=y^2+2xz^2\Rightarrow\phi=xy^2+x^2z^2+g(y,z)\). \(\phi_y=2xy+g_y=2xy\Rightarrow g_y=0\). \(\phi_z=2x^2z+g_z=2x^2z+4z^3\Rightarrow g_z=4z^3\Rightarrow g=z^4+h(y)\). Since \(g_y=0\): \(h'=0\). \(\phi=xy^2+x^2z^2+z^4+C\). \(\blacksquare\)

State the Helmholtz decomposition theorem for a vector field \(\mathbf F\) on \(\mathbb R^3\).

Theorem: Any sufficiently smooth, rapidly decaying vector field \(\mathbf F\) can be uniquely decomposed as \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\phi+\nabla\times\mathbf A\), where \(\phi\) is a scalar potential (irrotational part) and \(\mathbf A\) is a vector potential (solenoidal part). PYQ 2012 — Jacobian for Spherical Coordinates

Derive the Jacobian \(J=r^2\sin\theta\) for the transformation from Cartesian to spherical coordinates \((x,y,z)\to(r,\theta,\phi)\).

Solution

\(x=r\sin\theta\cos\phi,\,y=r\sin\theta\sin\phi,\,z=r\cos\theta\). The Jacobian \(J=\left|\dfrac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(r,\theta,\phi)}\right|\). Computing: \[\begin{vmatrix}\sin\theta\cos\phi & r\cos\theta\cos\phi & -r\sin\theta\sin\phi\\\sin\theta\sin\phi & r\cos\theta\sin\phi & r\sin\theta\cos\phi\\\cos\theta & -r\sin\theta & 0\end{vmatrix}\]. Expanding along third row: \(=\cos\theta[r^2\cos\theta\sin\theta(\cos^2\phi+\sin^2\phi)]+r\sin\theta[r\sin^2\theta(\cos^2\phi+\sin^2\phi)]=r^2\cos^2\theta\sin\theta+r^2\sin^3\theta=r^2\sin\theta\). Hence \(dV=r^2\sin\theta\,dr\,d\theta\,d\phi\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Surface Area of Torus

Find the surface area of the torus generated by rotating the circle \((x-b)^2+z^2=a^2\) (with \(b>a\)) about the \(z\)-axis.

Solution

Parametrize: \(x=(b+a\cos v)\cos u,\,y=(b+a\cos v)\sin u,\,z=a\sin v\) for \(u,v\in[0,2\pi]\). The distance from the \(z\)-axis is \(r=b+a\cos v\). Pappus's theorem: Surface area \(=2\pi\bar r\cdot L\) where \(L=2\pi a\) (circumference of circle) and \(\bar r=b\) (distance of center from axis). Thus \(A=2\pi b\cdot2\pi a=4\pi^2ab\). Direct computation also gives the same: \(||\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v||=a(b+a\cos v)\), \(A=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^{2\pi}a(b+a\cos v)\,du\,dv=2\pi a\cdot2\pi b=4\pi^2ab\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Pappus's Centroid Theorem

State and prove Pappus's centroid theorem for surface area: the area of a surface of revolution equals the product of the arc length and the distance traveled by the centroid.

Solution

Theorem: If a plane curve \(C\) of arc length \(L\) is rotated about an external axis in its plane, the surface area generated is \(A=2\pi\bar r\cdot L\), where \(\bar r\) is the distance from the centroid of \(C\) to the axis.
Proof: Let the curve be parametrized in the \((x,z)\)-plane, rotating about the \(z\)-axis. The strip of arc element \(ds\) at distance \(x\) generates a band of area \(dA=2\pi x\,ds\). Integrating: \(A=\int_C 2\pi x\,ds=2\pi\bar x\cdot L\) where \(\bar x=\tfrac{1}{L}\int_C x\,ds\) is the \(x\)-coordinate of the centroid. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Contravariant & Covariant Transformation Laws

State the transformation laws for contravariant components \(A^i\) and covariant components \(A_i\) of a vector under a change of coordinates \(x^i\to\bar x^i\).

Solution

Under \(\bar x^i=\bar x^i(x^1,\ldots,x^n)\):
Contravariant: \(\bar A^i=\dfrac{\partial\bar x^i}{\partial x^j}A^j\) (transforms like \(d\bar x^i=\dfrac{\partial\bar x^i}{\partial x^j}dx^j\)).
Covariant: \(\bar A_i=\dfrac{\partial x^j}{\partial\bar x^i}A_j\) (transforms like \(\partial/\partial\bar x^i\)).
The invariant scalar product \(A^iB_i=\bar A^i\bar B_i\) (Einstein summation) is unchanged under coordinate transformation. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Metric Tensor in Cylindrical Coordinates

Find the metric tensor \(g_{ij}\) and its inverse \(g^{ij}\) for cylindrical coordinates \((r,\theta,z)\).

Solution

Position: \(\mathbf r=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,z)\). Basis vectors: \(\mathbf e_1=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta,0),\,\mathbf e_2=(-r\sin\theta,r\cos\theta,0),\,\mathbf e_3=(0,0,1)\). Metric \(g_{ij}=\mathbf e_i\cdot\mathbf e_j\): \[g_{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&r^2&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.\] Inverse: \(g^{ij}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1/r^2&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.\) Line element: \(ds^2=g_{ij}dx^idx^j=dr^2+r^2d\theta^2+dz^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Christoffel Symbols for Spherical Coordinates

Compute the nonzero Christoffel symbols of the second kind \(\Gamma^k_{ij}\) for spherical coordinates \((r,\theta,\phi)\).

Solution

Metric: \(g_{11}=1,g_{22}=r^2,g_{33}=r^2\sin^2\theta\) (others zero). Using \(\Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}g^{kl}(\partial_i g_{jl}+\partial_j g_{il}-\partial_l g_{ij})\). Nonzero symbols: \(\Gamma^1_{22}=-r\), \(\Gamma^1_{33}=-r\sin^2\theta\), \(\Gamma^2_{12}=\Gamma^2_{21}=1/r\), \(\Gamma^2_{33}=-\sin\theta\cos\theta\), \(\Gamma^3_{13}=\Gamma^3_{31}=1/r\), \(\Gamma^3_{23}=\Gamma^3_{32}=\cot\theta\). These encode the curvature of the coordinate system. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Covariant Differentiation

Define the covariant derivative \(\nabla_j A^i\) of a contravariant vector \(A^i\). Write the formula involving Christoffel symbols.

Solution

The covariant derivative of a contravariant vector \(A^i\) with respect to \(x^j\) is: \[\nabla_j A^i = \frac{\partial A^i}{\partial x^j} + \Gamma^i_{kj}A^k.\] The extra term \(\Gamma^i_{kj}A^k\) corrects for the change of basis vectors. For a covariant vector \(A_i\): \[\nabla_j A_i = \frac{\partial A_i}{\partial x^j} - \Gamma^k_{ij}A_k.\] These transform as tensors of one higher covariant rank. The covariant derivative reduces to the ordinary partial derivative in Cartesian coordinates (\(\Gamma=0\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Riemann Curvature Tensor

Write the components \(R^l{}_{kij}\) of the Riemann curvature tensor in terms of Christoffel symbols. State when a space is flat.

Solution

\[R^l{}_{kij}=\frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{kj}}{\partial x^i}-\frac{\partial\Gamma^l_{ki}}{\partial x^j}+\Gamma^l_{mi}\Gamma^m_{kj}-\Gamma^l_{mj}\Gamma^m_{ki}.\] A space is flat (\(\equiv\) Euclidean, or curved background has zero curvature) iff \(R^l{}_{kij}=0\) identically. In that case coordinates exist where the metric is constant (Cartesian-like) and all Christoffel symbols vanish. For \(\mathbb{R}^n\) with Cartesian coordinates, \(R=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Symmetric & Antisymmetric Decomposition

Show that any rank-2 tensor \(T_{ij}\) can be uniquely decomposed into a symmetric part \(S_{ij}\) and an antisymmetric part \(A_{ij}\).

Solution

Define \(S_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(T_{ij}+T_{ji})\) and \(A_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(T_{ij}-T_{ji})\). Then \(S_{ij}+A_{ij}=T_{ij}\). Symmetry: \(S_{ji}=\tfrac{1}{2}(T_{ji}+T_{ij})=S_{ij}\). Antisymmetry: \(A_{ji}=-A_{ij}\). Uniqueness: if \(T_{ij}=S'_{ij}+A'_{ij}\) with \(S'\) symmetric and \(A'\) antisymmetric, then \(S'_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(T_{ij}+T_{ji})=S_{ij}\) and similarly \(A'_{ij}=A_{ij}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Quotient Law for Tensors

State and explain the Quotient Law: if \(A_{ij}B^j=C_i\) for all tensors \(B^j\), then \(A_{ij}\) is a tensor of type \((0,2)\).

Solution

Quotient Law: If an array \(A_{ij}\) is such that \(A_{ij}B^j\) is a covariant tensor \(C_i\) for every contravariant vector \(B^j\), then \(A_{ij}\) is a covariant tensor of rank 2.
Proof sketch: In barred coordinates, \(\bar C_i=(\partial x^k/\partial\bar x^i)C_k=(\partial x^k/\partial\bar x^i)A_{kl}B^l=(\partial x^k/\partial\bar x^i)A_{kl}(\partial x^l/\partial\bar x^m)\bar B^m\). Since this must equal \(\bar A_{im}\bar B^m\) for all \(\bar B^m\), we get \(\bar A_{im}=(\partial x^k/\partial\bar x^i)(\partial x^l/\partial\bar x^m)A_{kl}\), the covariant transformation law. \(\blacksquare\)

Express the cross product \((\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)_k=\varepsilon_{ijk}A^iB^j\) using the Levi-Civita tensor \(\varepsilon_{ijk}\). State its properties.

Cross product: \((\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)_k=\varepsilon_{ijk}A^iB^j\). Properties: (1) Totally antisymmetric: \(\varepsilon_{ijk}=-\varepsilon_{jik}=\varepsilon_{jki}\). PYQ 2016 — Exterior Derivative: 2-Form in R³

Compute the exterior derivative \(d\omega\) of the 1-form \(\omega=P\,dx+Q\,dy+R\,dz\) in \(\mathbb R^3\). Relate to curl.

\(d\omega=dP\wedge dx+dQ\wedge dy+dR\wedge dz\). Expanding \(dP=\partial_x P\,dx+\partial_y P\,dy+\partial_z P\,dz\) etc., and using \(dx\wedge dx=0\), \(dy\wedge dx=-dx\wedge dy\): \[d\omega=\left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}\right)dy\wedge dz+\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial R}{\partial x}\right)dz\wedge dx+\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dx\wedge dy.\] The coefficients are exactly the components of \(\nabla\times\mathbf F\) where \(\mathbf F=(P,Q,R)\). PYQ 2015 — Covariant Divergence & Gradient

Write the covariant forms of \(\nabla f\) and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A\) in general curvilinear coordinates using the metric tensor \(g_{ij}\) with \(g=\det(g_{ij})\).

PYQ 2013 — Bianchi Identities

State the Bianchi identity for the Riemann tensor: \(\nabla_{[l}R_{|mn|ij]}=0\). Write the contracted form (related to Einstein tensor).

Solution

First Bianchi identity: \(R_{klij}+R_{kijl}+R_{kjli}=0\) (algebraic, from definition). Second (differential) Bianchi identity: \(\nabla_l R_{kmij}+\nabla_i R_{kmjl}+\nabla_j R_{kmlj}=0\). PYQ 2012 — Einstein Summation Convention

Explain the Einstein summation convention. Evaluate \(g_{ij}dx^idx^j\) and \(\delta^i_i\) in \(n\) dimensions as examples.

Solution

Convention: A repeated index (one up, one down) in a product implies summation over all values \(1,\ldots,n\). E.g. \(A^iB_i\equiv\sum_{i=1}^nA^iB_i\). Rules: (1) A free index appears once per term. (2) A dummy (summation) index appears exactly twice. (3) No index may appear more than twice.

Solution

Volume integral: \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=1+1+x=2+x\). In cylindrical coordinates \((r,\theta,z)\) with \(x=r\cos\theta\): \[\iiint_V(2+x)\,dV=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^2\!\int_0^3(2+r\cos\theta)\,r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta=3\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^2(2r+r^2\cos\theta)\,dr\,d\theta.\] \[\int_0^2(2r+r^2\cos\theta)\,dr=\left[r^2+\frac{r^3\cos\theta}{3}\right]_0^2=4+\frac{8\cos\theta}{3}.\] \[3\int_0^{2\pi}\left(4+\frac{8\cos\theta}{3}\right)d\theta=3\left[4\theta+\frac{8\sin\theta}{3}\right]_0^{2\pi}=3\cdot 8\pi=24\pi.\]
Surface integral: Three parts — curved surface \(r=2\), top \(z=3\), bottom \(z=0\). Bottom \((z=0,\hat{n}=-\hat{k})\): flux \(=-\iint xz\,dA|_{z=0}=0\). Top \((z=3,\hat{n}=+\hat{k})\): flux \(=\iint 3x\,dA=3\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^2 r\cos\theta\cdot r\,dr\,d\theta=0\). Curved surface \((r=2,\hat{n}=(x,y,0)/2)\): flux \(=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^3\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{n}\cdot 2\,dz\,d\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^3[(x+y^2)x/2+y^2/2]\cdot 2\,dz\,d\theta\). On \(r=2\): \(x=2\cos\theta,y=2\sin\theta\). \(\mathbf{F}\cdot\hat{n}=\frac{(2\cos\theta+4\sin^2\theta)(2\cos\theta)+(2\sin\theta)(2\sin\theta)}{2}\). Integrating gives total \(24\pi\). Both sides equal \(24\pi\). ✓ \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 Q8c-i (10m) — Vector Identity: \(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{G})=\mathbf{G}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})-\mathbf{F}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})\)

Prove the vector identity \(\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{G})=\mathbf{G}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})-\mathbf{F}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})\).

Solution

Proof using index notation: \((\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{G})_i=\varepsilon_{ijk}F_jG_k\). Thus: \[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{G})=\partial_i(\varepsilon_{ijk}F_jG_k)=\varepsilon_{ijk}(\partial_i F_j)G_k+\varepsilon_{ijk}F_j(\partial_i G_k).\] For the first term: \(\varepsilon_{ijk}(\partial_i F_j)G_k=G_k(\varepsilon_{kij}\partial_i F_j)=G_k(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_k=\mathbf{G}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\) (using \(\varepsilon_{ijk}=\varepsilon_{kij}\)). For the second term: \(\varepsilon_{ijk}F_j(\partial_i G_k)=F_j\varepsilon_{jki}\partial_i G_k=F_j(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})_j\cdot(-1)\) — note \(\varepsilon_{ijk}=-\varepsilon_{jik}=-\varepsilon_{jki}\cdot(-1)^2\); more carefully \(\varepsilon_{ijk}F_j\partial_i G_k=-\varepsilon_{jik}F_j\partial_i G_k=-F_j(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})_j=-\mathbf{F}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{G})\). Combining: \[\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{G})=\mathbf{G}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})-\mathbf{F}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{G}).\;\blacksquare\]

PYQ 2026 Q8c-ii (10m) — Differentiation of a Vector Function and Cross Product

If \(\mathbf{r}(t)=t^2\hat{i}+\sin t\,\hat{j}+e^t\hat{k}\), find \(\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\), \(\frac{d^2\mathbf{r}}{dt^2}\), and the unit tangent vector \(\hat{T}\) at \(t=0\). Also find \(\mathbf{r}\times\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\) at \(t=0\).

Solution

First derivative: \(\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}=(2t,\;\cos t,\;e^t)\). At \(t=0\): \(\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\big|_0=(0,1,1)\).
Second derivative: \(\frac{d^2\mathbf{r}}{dt^2}=(2,\;-\sin t,\;e^t)\). At \(t=0\): \(\frac{d^2\mathbf{r}}{dt^2}\big|_0=(2,0,1)\).
Unit tangent at \(t=0\): \(\hat{T}=\frac{(0,1,1)}{|(0,1,1)|}=\frac{(0,1,1)}{\sqrt{2}}=\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\).
Cross product at \(t=0\): \(\mathbf{r}(0)=(0,\sin 0,e^0)=(0,0,1)\). \[\mathbf{r}\times\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\bigg|_0=(0,0,1)\times(0,1,1)=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k}\\0&0&1\\0&1&1\end{vmatrix}=(0\cdot1-1\cdot1)\hat{i}-(0\cdot1-1\cdot0)\hat{j}+(0\cdot1-0\cdot0)\hat{k}=-\hat{i}.\] So \(\mathbf{r}\times\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\big|_{t=0}=-\hat{i}\). \(\blacksquare\)
