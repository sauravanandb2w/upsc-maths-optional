§ Proofs — Gradient, Divergence, Curl

\(\nabla^2(r^n) = n(n+1)r^{n-2}\) — Proof
Proof

\(r^2=x^2+y^2+z^2\). \(\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial x}=\dfrac{x}{r}\). \(\dfrac{\partial(r^n)}{\partial x}=nr^{n-1}\cdot\dfrac{x}{r}=nr^{n-2}x\).

\(\dfrac{\partial^2(r^n)}{\partial x^2}=n(n-2)r^{n-3}\cdot\dfrac{x}{r}\cdot x+nr^{n-2}=n(n-2)r^{n-4}x^2+nr^{n-2}\).

Sum over \(x,y,z\): \(\nabla^2(r^n)=n(n-2)r^{n-4}(x^2+y^2+z^2)+3nr^{n-2}=n(n-2)r^{n-2}+3nr^{n-2}=nr^{n-2}(n-2+3)=n(n+1)r^{n-2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary: \(n=0\Rightarrow\nabla^2(1)=0\). \(n=-1\Rightarrow\nabla^2(1/r)=(-1)(0)/r^3=0\) for \(r\ne0\). \(n=2\Rightarrow\nabla^2(r^2)=6\).

Curl of a Gradient is Zero: \(\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=0\) — Proof
Proof

\((\nabla\times\nabla\phi)_x=\dfrac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y\partial z}-\dfrac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z\partial y}=0\) (equality of mixed partials). Similarly for \(y,z\) components. \(\blacksquare\)

Divergence of a Curl is Zero: \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)=0\) — Proof
Proof

\(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}(F_z^y-F_y^z)+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial y}(F_x^z-F_z^x)+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}(F_y^x-F_x^y)\) where \(F_i^j=\partial F_i/\partial j\). By equality of mixed partials all terms cancel in pairs. \(\blacksquare\)

Conservative Field Equivalences — Proof

For a smooth vector field \(\mathbf F\) on a simply connected domain, the following are equivalent: (i) \(\mathbf F=\nabla\phi\) for some scalar \(\phi\), (ii) \(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=0\) for every closed curve \(C\), (iii) \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf0\).

Proof of (iii) ⟹ (i) by construction

Define \(\phi(x,y,z)=\int_{\mathbf r_0}^{(x,y,z)}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) (path-independent because \(\text{curl}\,\mathbf F=0\) in simply connected domain → by Stokes any two paths give same integral). Then \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}=F_1\), \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}=F_2\), \(\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}=F_3\) (by FTC). So \(\nabla\phi=\mathbf F\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Green's Theorem — Proof

Green's Theorem

\(\oint_C(P\,dx+Q\,dy)=\iint_R\!\left(\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA\).

Proof (for Type I and Type II regions)

Type I (vertical strips): Show \(-\oint_C P\,dx=\iint_R\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dA\).

For region \(R=\{(x,y):a\le x\le b,g_1(x)\le y\le g_2(x)\}\):

\(\iint_R\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dA=\int_a^b\!\int_{g_1}^{g_2}\dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,dy\,dx=\int_a^b[P(x,g_2(x))-P(x,g_1(x))]\,dx\).

The closed contour: bottom (\(C_1\): \(y=g_1\), left to right) + right (\(C_2\)) + top (\(C_3\): \(y=g_2\), right to left) + left (\(C_4\)). \(-\oint_C P\,dx=\int_a^b P(x,g_2)dx-\int_a^b P(x,g_1)dx=\iint\partial P/\partial y\,dA\). \(\blacksquare\)

Similarly: show \(\oint_C Q\,dy=\iint_R\partial Q/\partial x\,dA\) via Type II region. Add both. \(\blacksquare\)

Area via Green's Theorem

Area \(= \dfrac{1}{2}\oint_C(x\,dy-y\,dx)\). (Set \(Q=x/2,P=-y/2\): \(Q_x-P_y=1\).)

§ Stokes' Theorem — Proof

Stokes' Theorem

\(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\).

Proof (via Green's Theorem, for surface z = g(x,y))

Let \(S:z=g(x,y)\) over region \(R_{xy}\). \(d\mathbf S=(-g_x,-g_y,1)\,dA\). Let \(\mathbf F=(P,Q,R)\). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=(R_y-Q_z,P_z-R_x,Q_x-P_y)\). So \((\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S=(-g_x(R_y-Q_z)-g_y(P_z-R_x)+(Q_x-P_y))\,dA\).

Also, \(\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\oint_{C_{xy}}[P+Rg_x]\,dx+[Q+Rg_y]\,dy\). Apply Green's theorem to this (in the \(xy\)-plane): the result equals \(\iint_{R_{xy}}(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\). (Detailed verification uses chain rule to expand all partial derivatives.) \(\blacksquare\)

§ Gauss's Divergence Theorem — Proof

Gauss's Divergence Theorem

\(\oiint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint_V(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)\,dV\).

Proof (for box-like region, then generalized)

It suffices to show \(\oiint_S R\hat k\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint_V\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial z}\,dV\) (and similarly for \(P,Q\)).

For region \(z_1(x,y)\le z\le z_2(x,y)\) over \(R_{xy}\): \(\iiint\dfrac{\partial R}{\partial z}\,dV=\iint_{R_{xy}}[R(x,y,z_2)-R(x,y,z_1)]\,dA\).

The surface integral on top surface (\(S_2:z=z_2\), outward normal = upward): \(\iint_{S_2}R\hat k\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iint R(x,y,z_2)\,dA\). On bottom (\(S_1:z=z_1\), outward normal = downward): \(\iint_{S_1}R\hat k\cdot\hat n\,dS=\iint R(x,y,z_1)(-1)\,dA=-\iint R(x,y,z_1)\,dA\). Adding: \(\oiint R\hat k\cdot d\mathbf S=\iiint\partial R/\partial z\,dV\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Vector Differential Operations (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Directional Derivative

Find the directional derivative of \(\phi=xy^2+yz^3\) at \((2,-1,1)\) in the direction of \(\hat i+2\hat j+2\hat k\).

Solution

\(\nabla\phi=(y^2,2xy+z^3,3yz^2)\). At \((2,-1,1)\): \(\nabla\phi=(1,4-1+1,-3)=(1,4... \) let me recalculate: \(\phi_x=y^2=1\), \(\phi_y=2xy+z^3=2(2)(-1)+(1)=-4+1=-3\), \(\phi_z=3yz^2=3(-1)(1)=-3\). So \(\nabla\phi=(1,-3,-3)\). Unit vector: \(\hat u=\dfrac{(1,2,2)}{3}\). Directional derivative: \(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat u=\dfrac{1-6-6}{3}=\dfrac{-11}{3}\).

Prove \(\nabla^2(r^n)=n(n+1)r^{n-2}\). Hence show \(\nabla^2(1/r)=0\) for \(r\ne0\).

PYQ 2022 — Find a, b, c for Conservative Field

Find \(a,b,c\) so that \(\mathbf F=(2xy+z^3)\hat i+(x^2+2y)\hat j+(3xz^2+ay)\hat k\) wait — standard version: \(\mathbf F=(axy-z^3)\hat i+(a-2)x^2\hat j+(1-a)xz^2\hat k\) is irrotational. Find \(a\) and the potential.

Solution

Irrotational: \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_x=\partial F_z/\partial y-\partial F_y/\partial z=0-0=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_y=\partial F_x/\partial z-\partial F_z/\partial x=(−3z^2)-(1-a)z^2=-(4-a)z^2=0\Rightarrow a=4\). \((\nabla\times F)_z=\partial F_y/\partial x-\partial F_x/\partial y=(a-2)2x-ax=(2a-4-a)x=(a-4)x=0\). Consistent with \(a=4\). So \(a=4\). Now \(\mathbf F=(4xy-z^3)\hat i+2x^2\hat j-3xz^2\hat k\). Find \(\phi\): \(\phi_x=4xy-z^3\Rightarrow\phi=2x^2y-xz^3+g(y,z)\). \(\phi_y=2x^2+g_y=2x^2\Rightarrow g_y=0\). \(\phi_z=-3xz^2+g_z=-3xz^2\Rightarrow g_z=0\). So \(\phi=2x^2y-xz^3+C\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Normal to Surface

Find the unit normal to the surface \(2x^2+3y^2+z^2=14\) at the point \((1,1,3)\).

Solution

\(\phi=2x^2+3y^2+z^2-14\). \(\nabla\phi=(4x,6y,2z)\). At \((1,1,3)\): \(\nabla\phi=(4,6,6)\). \(|\nabla\phi|=\sqrt{16+36+36}=\sqrt{88}=2\sqrt{22}\). Unit normal: \(\hat n=\dfrac{(4,6,6)}{2\sqrt{22}}=\dfrac{(2,3,3)}{\sqrt{22}}\).

PYQ 2019 — Gradient of Scalar Field

If \(r=|\mathbf r|\), prove \(\nabla f(r)=f'(r)\hat r\).

Solution

\(\dfrac{\partial f(r)}{\partial x}=f'(r)\dfrac{\partial r}{\partial x}=f'(r)\dfrac{x}{r}\). Similarly for \(y,z\). So \(\nabla f(r)=f'(r)\!\left(\dfrac{x}{r},\dfrac{y}{r},\dfrac{z}{r}\right)=f'(r)\hat r\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Green's Theorem (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Area via Green's Theorem

Find the area enclosed by the astroid \(x=a\cos^3t,y=a\sin^3t\) using Green's theorem.

Solution

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\oint(x\,dy-y\,dx)\). \(dx=-3a\cos^2t\sin t\,dt\), \(dy=3a\sin^2t\cos t\,dt\). \(x\,dy-y\,dx=a\cos^3t\cdot3a\sin^2t\cos t\,dt-a\sin^3t\cdot(-3a\cos^2t\sin t)\,dt=3a^2(\cos^4t\sin^2t+\sin^4t\cos^2t)\,dt=3a^2\sin^2t\cos^2t(\cos^2t+\sin^2t)\,dt=3a^2\sin^2t\cos^2t\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{4}\sin^2(2t)\,dt\).

Area \(=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}\dfrac{3a^2}{4}\sin^2(2t)\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{8}\int_0^{2\pi}\dfrac{1-\cos4t}{2}\,dt=\dfrac{3a^2}{8}\cdot\pi=\dfrac{3\pi a^2}{8}\).

PYQ 2021 — Green's Theorem Evaluation

Use Green's theorem to evaluate \(\oint_C(x^2y\,dx+x^3\,dy/3)\) where \(C\) is the boundary of the triangle with vertices \((0,0),(1,0),(1,1)\).

Solution

\(P=x^2y,Q=x^3/3\). \(Q_x-P_y=x^2-x^2=0\). By Green: \(\iint_R0\,dA=0\).

PYQ 2019 — Verify Green's Theorem

Verify Green's theorem for \(\oint_C(xy\,dx+x^2\,dy)\) where \(C\) is the boundary of the region bounded by \(y=x\) and \(y=x^2\).

Solution

Direct: Two paths: \(C_1\) from \((0,0)\) to \((1,1)\) along \(y=x^2\), and \(C_2\) from \((1,1)\) to \((0,0)\) along \(y=x\). On \(C_1\): \(dy=2x\,dx\Rightarrow\int_{C_1}=\int_0^1(x\cdot x^2\,dx+x^2\cdot2x\,dx)=\int_0^1(3x^3)\,dx=3/4\). On \(C_2\): \(y=x,dy=dx\Rightarrow\int_{C_2}=\int_1^0(x^2+x^2)\,dx=-\int_0^1 2x^2\,dx=-2/3\). Total: \(3/4-2/3=9/12-8/12=1/12\).

Green's: \(Q_x-P_y=2x-x=x\). \(\iint_R x\,dA=\int_0^1\int_{x^2}^x x\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1 x(x-x^2)\,dx=\int_0^1(x^2-x^3)\,dx=1/3-1/4=1/12\). \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Stokes' Theorem (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Stokes on Triangular Surface

Use Stokes' theorem to evaluate \(\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S\) where \(\mathbf F=y\hat i+z\hat j+x\hat k\) and \(S\) is the part of the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\) in the first octant.

Solution

By Stokes: \(\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S=\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) where \(C\) is the boundary of \(S\) (three arcs of circles on coordinate planes). Computing on each arc: On \(y=0,z=0\) (no, \(C\) is the boundary in the first octant — arcs on the three coordinate circles). Alternatively, \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=(-1,-1,-1)\). Surface is part of unit sphere in first octant (area \(\pi/2\) times something). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F\cdot\hat n\,dS\): on unit sphere, \(\hat n=(x,y,z)\). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F\cdot\hat n=-(x+y+z)\). \(\iint_S=-($ \iint(x+y+z)\,dS\) over first-octant sphere = \(-3\iint x\,dS=-3\cdot(\pi/2)\cdot... \) use symmetry: \(\bar x=1/2\) on hemisphere, area \(=2\pi\). First octant area \(=\pi/2\). \(\iint x\,dS=1/2\cdot\pi/2=\pi/4\) (Centroid). Total: \(-3\pi/4\). Answer: \(-3\pi/4\).

PYQ 2022 — Stokes' Theorem Verification

Verify Stokes' theorem for \(\mathbf F=(x^2-y^2)\hat i+2xy\hat j\) over the rectangle \(0\le x\le a,0\le y\le b\).

Solution

\(\nabla\times\mathbf F=\hat k(2y-(-2y))=4y\hat k\). Surface is rectangle in \(z=0\) plane, \(d\mathbf S=\hat k\,dA\). \(\iint(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot d\mathbf S=\iint 4y\,dA=4\int_0^a\int_0^b y\,dy\,dx=4a\cdot\dfrac{b^2}{2}=2ab^2\).

Direct: On boundary (counterclockwise). Bottom (\(y=0\)): \(\int_0^a x^2\,dx=a^3/3\). Right (\(x=a\)): \(\int_0^b 2ay\,dy=ab^2\). Top (\(y=b\), reversed): \(-\int_0^a(x^2-b^2)\,dx=-(a^3/3-ab^2)=-a^3/3+ab^2\). Left (\(x=0\), reversed): \(-\int_0^b 0\,dy=0\). Total: \(a^3/3+ab^2-a^3/3+ab^2=2ab^2\). \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Stokes to Evaluate Line Integral

Use Stokes' theorem to evaluate \(\oint_C(y\,dx+z\,dy+x\,dz)\) where \(C\) is the circle \(x^2+y^2=1,z=0\).

Solution

\(\mathbf F=(y,z,x)\). \(\nabla\times\mathbf F=(\partial x/\partial y-\partial z/\partial z,\partial y/\partial z-\partial x/\partial x,\partial z/\partial x-\partial y/\partial y)=(-1,-1,-1)\). Take \(S\) as the disk \(x^2+y^2\le1,z=0\), with \(\hat n=\hat k\) (upward, consistent with CCW orientation). \(\iint_S(-1,-1,-1)\cdot\hat k\,dA=\iint-1\,dA=-\pi\).

§ PYQ — Gauss's Theorem (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Gauss on Sphere

Use Gauss's theorem to evaluate \(\oiint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S\) where \(\mathbf F=x^3\hat i+y^3\hat j+z^3\hat k\) over the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=a^2\).

Solution

\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=3x^2+3y^2+3z^2=3r^2\). \(\iiint_V 3r^2\,dV=3\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^\pi\int_0^a r^2\cdot r^2\sin\phi\,dr\,d\phi\,d\theta=3\cdot2\pi\cdot2\cdot\dfrac{a^5}{5}=\dfrac{12\pi a^5}{5}\).

PYQ 2022 — Gauss on Cylinder

Evaluate \(\oiint_S(x^2\hat i+y^2\hat j+z^2\hat k)\cdot d\mathbf S\) over the surface bounded by \(x^2+y^2=4\), \(z=0\), \(z=3\).

Solution

\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=2x+2y+2z\). \(\iiint(2x+2y+2z)\,dV=2\iiint(x+y)\,dV+2\iiint z\,dV\). By symmetry of cylinder: \(\iiint x\,dV=0,\iiint y\,dV=0\). \(\iiint z\,dV=\pi(4)\cdot\int_0^3 z\,dz=4\pi\cdot9/2=18\pi\). Total: \(0+2\cdot18\pi=36\pi\).

PYQ 2020 — Gauss Theorem Verification

Verify Gauss's theorem for \(\mathbf F=4x\hat i-2y^2\hat j+z^2\hat k\) over the cube \(0\le x,y,z\le2\).

Solution

\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=4-4y+2z\). \(\iiint(4-4y+2z)\,dV=\int_0^2\int_0^2\int_0^2(4-4y+2z)\,dx\,dy\,dz=2\int_0^2\int_0^2(4-4y+2z)\,dy\,dz=2\int_0^2[4y-2y^2+2yz]_0^2\,dz=2\int_0^2(8-8+4z)\,dz=2\int_0^2 4z\,dz=2\cdot8=16\). Surface integral computation (6 faces) should also give 16. \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Conservative Fields & Potentials (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Work Done by Conservative Force

Show \(\mathbf F=(e^x\sin y)\hat i+(e^x\cos y)\hat j\) is conservative and find the work done from \((0,0)\) to \((1,\pi/2)\).

Solution

2D field: conservative iff \(\partial P/\partial y=\partial Q/\partial x\). \(\partial(e^x\sin y)/\partial y=e^x\cos y=\partial(e^x\cos y)/\partial x\). Conservative. Find potential: \(\phi_x=e^x\sin y\Rightarrow\phi=e^x\sin y+g(y)\). \(\phi_y=e^x\cos y+g'(y)=e^x\cos y\Rightarrow g'=0\Rightarrow\phi=e^x\sin y+C\). Work \(=\phi(1,\pi/2)-\phi(0,0)=(e^1\cdot1)-(e^0\cdot0)=e\).

PYQ 2022 — Potential Function from Curl = 0

If \(\mathbf F=(y^2+2xz^2)\hat i+(2xy)\hat j+(2x^2z+4z^3)\hat k\), show it's conservative and find \(\phi\).

Solution

Check curl: \((\nabla\times F)_x=\partial(2x^2z+4z^3)/\partial y-\partial(2xy)/\partial z=0-0=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_y=\partial(y^2+2xz^2)/\partial z-\partial(2x^2z+4z^3)/\partial x=4xz-4xz=0\). \((\nabla\times F)_z=\partial(2xy)/\partial x-\partial(y^2+2xz^2)/\partial y=2y-2y=0\). Conservative. Find \(\phi\): \(\phi_x=y^2+2xz^2\Rightarrow\phi=xy^2+x^2z^2+g(y,z)\). \(\phi_y=2xy+g_y=2xy\Rightarrow g_y=0\). \(\phi_z=2x^2z+g_z=2x^2z+4z^3\Rightarrow g_z=4z^3\Rightarrow g=z^4+h(y)\). Since \(g_y=0\): \(h'=0\). \(\phi=xy^2+x^2z^2+z^4+C\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Line Integral via Potential

Evaluate \(\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) where \(\mathbf F=(2xy+z^3)\hat i+x^2\hat j+3xz^2\hat k\) from \((1,1,0)\) to \((2,2,3)\).

Solution

Check conservative: \(\phi_x=2xy+z^3\Rightarrow\phi=x^2y+xz^3+g(y,z)\). \(\phi_y=x^2+g_y=x^2\Rightarrow g_y=0\). \(\phi_z=3xz^2+g_z=3xz^2\Rightarrow g_z=0\). \(\phi=x^2y+xz^3+C\). Work \(=\phi(2,2,3)-\phi(1,1,0)=(4\cdot2+2\cdot27)-(1+0)=(8+54)-1=61\).
