§ Proofs — Analytic Functions

Cauchy-Riemann Equations — Necessary Conditions for Differentiability

If \(f=u+iv\) is differentiable at \(z_0\), then \(u_x=v_y\) and \(u_y=-v_x\) at \(z_0\).

Proof

\(f'(z_0)=\lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(z_0+h)-f(z_0)}{h}\) exists and is path-independent.

Path 1: \(h=\Delta x\) (real): \(f'=\lim_{\Delta x\to0}\dfrac{u(x_0+\Delta x,y_0)-u(x_0,y_0)}{\Delta x}+i\dfrac{v(x_0+\Delta x,y_0)-v(x_0,y_0)}{\Delta x}=u_x+iv_x\).

Path 2: \(h=i\Delta y\) (imaginary): \(f'=\lim_{\Delta y\to0}\dfrac{u(x_0,y_0+\Delta y)-u_0}{i\Delta y}+i\dfrac{v(x_0,y_0+\Delta y)-v_0}{i\Delta y}=\dfrac{u_y}{i}+\dfrac{iv_y}{i}=v_y-iu_y\).

Equate real and imaginary parts: \(u_x=v_y\), \(v_x=-u_y\). \(\blacksquare\)

CR Equations — Sufficient Conditions (Proof)

If \(u,v\) have continuous first-order partial derivatives at \((x_0,y_0)\) satisfying CR equations, then \(f=u+iv\) is differentiable at \(z_0\).

Proof (Total differential approach)

By differentiability of \(u\) and \(v\): \(\Delta u=u_x\Delta x+u_y\Delta y+\epsilon_1|\Delta z|\), \(\Delta v=v_x\Delta x+v_y\Delta y+\epsilon_2|\Delta z|\) where \(\epsilon_i\to0\). So \(\Delta f=\Delta u+i\Delta v=(u_x+iv_x)\Delta x+(u_y+iv_y)\Delta y+\epsilon|\Delta z|\). Using CR: \(u_y=-v_x,v_y=u_x\). Let \(A=u_x+iv_x\). Then \(\Delta f=A\Delta x+i(-\bar A)\cdot\frac{\Delta y}{1}...\ \) using \((u_y+iv_y)=(-v_x+iu_x)=i(u_x+iv_x)=iA\). So \(\Delta f=A\Delta x+iA\Delta y+\epsilon|\Delta z|=A(\Delta x+i\Delta y)+\epsilon|\Delta z|=A\Delta z+\epsilon|\Delta z|\). Hence \(\dfrac{\Delta f}{\Delta z}\to A=u_x+iv_x\). Differentiable. \(\blacksquare\)

Harmonic Functions: u analytic ⟹ u is harmonic
Proof

If \(f=u+iv\) is analytic: CR gives \(u_x=v_y,u_y=-v_x\). Differentiate: \(u_{xx}=v_{yx},u_{yy}=-v_{xy}\). By equality of mixed partials (analyticity ⟹ \(C^\infty\)): \(v_{xy}=v_{yx}\Rightarrow u_{xx}+u_{yy}=v_{yx}-v_{xy}=0\). Similarly \(v_{xx}+v_{yy}=0\). So \(u,v\) are harmonic. \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Cauchy's Theorem & CIF

Cauchy's Theorem — Proof (via Green's theorem, for simply connected domain)

If \(f\) is analytic in a simply connected domain \(D\), then \(\oint_C f(z)\,dz=0\) for any closed curve \(C\subset D\).

Proof

\(\oint_C f\,dz=\oint_C(u+iv)(dx+i\,dy)=\oint_C(u\,dx-v\,dy)+i\oint_C(v\,dx+u\,dy)\).

By Green's theorem (for smooth \(C\)): \(\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA\). First integral: \(P=u,Q=-v\): \(\iint(-v_x-u_y)\,dA=0\) by CR (\(u_y=-v_x\)). Second integral: \(P=v,Q=u\): \(\iint(u_x-v_y)\,dA=0\) by CR. So \(\oint_C f\,dz=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Cauchy's Integral Formula — Proof

If \(f\) is analytic in a simply connected domain containing \(C\) and \(z_0\) is inside \(C\): \(f(z_0)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C\dfrac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\,dz\).

Proof

The function \(g(z)=\dfrac{f(z)}{z-z_0}\) has a singularity at \(z_0\). Deform \(C\) to a small circle \(C_\epsilon:|z-z_0|=\epsilon\) (by Cauchy's deformation theorem — valid since \(g\) is analytic except at \(z_0\)).

\(\oint_C\dfrac{f(z)}{z-z_0}dz=\oint_{C_\epsilon}\dfrac{f(z)}{z-z_0}dz=\oint_{C_\epsilon}\dfrac{f(z_0)+(f(z)-f(z_0))}{z-z_0}dz\).

First part: \(f(z_0)\oint_{C_\epsilon}\dfrac{dz}{z-z_0}=f(z_0)\cdot2\pi i\). Second part: \(\left|\oint_{C_\epsilon}\dfrac{f(z)-f(z_0)}{z-z_0}dz\right|\le\dfrac{\sup|f(z)-f(z_0)|}{\epsilon}\cdot2\pi\epsilon\to0\) as \(\epsilon\to0\) (by continuity of \(f\)). \(\blacksquare\)

Liouville's Theorem — Proof

Every bounded entire function is constant.

Proof

Let \(|f(z)|\le M\). By CIF for derivative: \(f'(z_0)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-z_0|=R}\dfrac{f(z)}{(z-z_0)^2}dz\). So \(|f'(z_0)|\le\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\dfrac{M}{R^2}\cdot2\pi R=\dfrac{M}{R}\to0\) as \(R\to\infty\). Hence \(f'(z_0)=0\) for all \(z_0\). So \(f\) is constant. \(\blacksquare\)

Fundamental Theorem of Algebra — via Liouville

Every non-constant polynomial with complex coefficients has a root in \(\mathbb{C}\).

Proof

Let \(p(z)\) have no root. Then \(f(z)=1/p(z)\) is entire. As \(|z|\to\infty\): \(|p(z)|\to\infty\Rightarrow|f(z)|\to0\). So \(f\) is bounded on \(\mathbb{C}\) (bounded at \(\infty\) and continuous on compact set). By Liouville: \(f\) is constant, so \(p\) is constant — contradiction. \(\blacksquare\)

Laurent's Theorem — Proof sketch

If \(f\) is analytic in \(r<|z-z_0|

Proof (via CIF on annular region)

For \(z\) in the annulus, apply CIF with two circles \(C_1:|w-z_0|=R_1r\): \(f(z)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_1}\dfrac{f(w)}{w-z}dw-\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{C_2}\dfrac{f(w)}{w-z}dw\). Expand each using geometric series: \(\dfrac{1}{w-z}=\dfrac{1}{w-z_0}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-(z-z_0)/(w-z_0)}\) for outer circle (converges since \(|z-z_0|<|w-z_0|\)); and \(\dfrac{-1}{z-w}=\dfrac{-1}{z-z_0}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-(w-z_0)/(z-z_0)}\) for inner circle. The first gives positive powers, the second gives negative powers. \(\blacksquare\)

Classifying Singularities via Laurent Series
  • Removable singularity: no negative powers (or \(\lim_{z\to z_0}(z-z_0)f(z)=0\))
  • Pole of order \(m\): principal part has finitely many terms, lowest power \((z-z_0)^{-m}\)
  • Essential singularity: principal part has infinitely many terms

§ Proofs — Residues & Contour Integration

Residue Theorem — Proof

If \(f\) is analytic inside and on \(C\) except at isolated singularities \(z_1,\ldots,z_n\) inside \(C\): \(\oint_C f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{k=1}^n\text{Res}(f,z_k)\).

Proof

By Cauchy's deformation: replace \(C\) by small circles \(\gamma_k\) around each \(z_k\): \(\oint_C f\,dz=\sum_k\oint_{\gamma_k}f\,dz\). On \(\gamma_k\), expand \(f\) as Laurent series: \(\oint_{\gamma_k}f\,dz=\oint_{\gamma_k}\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty a_n^{(k)}(z-z_k)^n\,dz\). Only the \(n=-1\) term contributes (all others integrate to 0 over closed circle). \(\oint_{\gamma_k}a_{-1}^{(k)}(z-z_k)^{-1}dz=2\pi i\cdot a_{-1}^{(k)}=2\pi i\cdot\text{Res}(f,z_k)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Residue Formulas

Simple pole: \(\text{Res}(f,z_0)=\lim_{z\to z_0}(z-z_0)f(z)\).

Pole of order \(m\): \(\text{Res}(f,z_0)=\dfrac{1}{(m-1)!}\lim_{z\to z_0}\dfrac{d^{m-1}}{dz^{m-1}}[(z-z_0)^m f(z)]\).

Quotient form: If \(f=p/q\), simple zero of \(q\) at \(z_0\), \(p(z_0)\ne0\): \(\text{Res}=p(z_0)/q'(z_0)\).

§ PYQ — Analyticity & Harmonic Functions (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Check Analyticity

Test whether \(f(z)=|z|^2\) is analytic.

Solution

\(f=x^2+y^2+0i\). \(u=x^2+y^2,v=0\). CR: \(u_x=2x,v_y=0\). Need \(u_x=v_y\Rightarrow2x=0\). Only at \(x=0\). And \(u_y=2y=-v_x=0\Rightarrow y=0\). CR satisfied only at origin. But CR is not continuous-with-partials elsewhere to give differentiability. \(f\) is differentiable only at \(z=0\), not analytic anywhere.

PYQ 2023 — Harmonic Conjugate

If \(u=x^3-3xy^2\), find the harmonic conjugate \(v\) and write \(f(z)=u+iv\) in terms of \(z\).

Solution

\(u_x=3x^2-3y^2,u_y=-6xy\). CR: \(v_y=u_x=3x^2-3y^2\Rightarrow v=3x^2y-y^3+\phi(x)\). \(v_x=6xy+\phi'(x)=-u_y=6xy\Rightarrow\phi'(x)=0\Rightarrow\phi=C\). \(v=3x^2y-y^3+C\). \(f(z)=(x^3-3xy^2)+i(3x^2y-y^3)=x^3+3ix^2y-3xy^2-iy^3=(x+iy)^3=z^3\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Milne-Thomson Method

Find \(f(z)\) given that \(\text{Im}(f)=e^x\sin y+xy\).

Solution

Let \(v=e^x\sin y+xy\). \(v_x=e^x\sin y+y\), \(v_y=e^x\cos y+x\). By CR: \(u_x=v_y=e^x\cos y+x\), \(u_y=-v_x=-(e^x\sin y+y)\). Check: these are consistent (verify \(u_{xy}=u_{yx}\)). Using Milne-Thomson: \(f(z)=f'(z)dz\)... method: \(f'(z)=u_x+iv_x=(e^x\cos y+x)+i(e^x\sin y+y)\). At \(y=0\): \(f'(x+i0)=(e^x+x)+ie^x\cdot0=e^x+x\). In complex form: \(f'(z)=e^z+z\). Integrate: \(f(z)=e^z+z^2/2+C\). Verify: \(e^z=e^x\cos y+ie^x\sin y\), \(z^2/2=(x^2-y^2)/2+ixy\). \(\text{Im}(f)=e^x\sin y+xy\). Matches! \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Real Part Given

Show \(u=e^x\cos y\) is harmonic and find the corresponding analytic function.

Solution

\(u_x=e^x\cos y,u_{xx}=e^x\cos y\). \(u_y=-e^x\sin y,u_{yy}=-e^x\cos y\). \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\). Harmonic. \(v_y=u_x=e^x\cos y\Rightarrow v=e^x\sin y+\phi(x)\). \(v_x=e^x\sin y+\phi'(x)=-u_y=e^x\sin y\Rightarrow\phi'=0\). \(f(z)=e^x\cos y+ie^x\sin y=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)=e^xe^{iy}=e^{x+iy}=e^z\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Find a, b for Analyticity

Find \(a,b\in\mathbb{R}\) such that \(f(z)=(x^2+axy+by^2)+iv\) is analytic, if \(v=2xy-y^2\)... actually: find \(a,b\) for \(u=ax^2+bxy+y^2\) to be harmonic.

Solution

\(u_{xx}=2a,u_{yy}=2\). Harmonic requires \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\Rightarrow2a+2=0\Rightarrow a=-1\). \(b\) can be anything (since \(u_{xy}=b\) and Laplace doesn't directly constrain \(b\)). But \(b=0\) is typical unless stated otherwise. So \(a=-1\). Then \(u=-x^2+y^2=-(x^2-y^2)\). This is \(-\text{Re}(z^2)\), consistent with \(f(z)=-z^2+C\) (analytic).

§ PYQ — Cauchy Theorem & CIF (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — CIF Application

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=2}\frac{e^z}{z(z-1)}\,dz\).

Solution

Singularities at \(z=0\) and \(z=1\), both inside \(|z|=2\). By residue theorem (or CIF): \(\oint=2\pi i(\text{Res at }0+\text{Res at }1)\). Res at 0: \(\lim_{z\to0}z\cdot\dfrac{e^z}{z(z-1)}=\dfrac{e^0}{0-1}=-1\). Res at 1: \(\lim_{z\to1}(z-1)\cdot\dfrac{e^z}{z(z-1)}=\dfrac{e^1}{1}=e\). Total: \(2\pi i(-1+e)=2\pi i(e-1)\).

PYQ 2022 — Cauchy's Theorem Verification

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{\sin z}{z}\,dz\).

Solution

\(g(z)=\sin z/z\): the singularity at \(z=0\) is removable (Laurent series: \(\sin z/z=1-z^2/6+\cdots\) — no negative powers). By extended Cauchy theorem: \(\oint g\,dz=2\pi i\cdot\text{Res}(g,0)=2\pi i\cdot0=0\). (Alternatively: since \(g\) is entire after defining \(g(0)=1\), Cauchy gives 0.) Answer: \(\mathbf{0}\).

PYQ 2020 — Derivatives via CIF

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_{|z|=3}\frac{\cos\pi z}{(z-1)^2(z+2)}\,dz\).

Solution

Singularities: pole of order 2 at \(z=1\), simple pole at \(z=-2\). Both inside \(|z|=3\).

Res at \(-2\): \(\dfrac{\cos(-2\pi)}{(-2-1)^2}=\dfrac{1}{9}\). Res at \(1\): \(\dfrac{d}{dz}\!\left[\dfrac{\cos\pi z}{z+2}\right]_{z=1}=\dfrac{-\pi\sin\pi z(z+2)-\cos\pi z}{(z+2)^2}\bigg|_{z=1}=\dfrac{-\pi\sin\pi\cdot3-\cos\pi}{9}=\dfrac{0+1}{9}=\dfrac{1}{9}\). Total: \(2\pi i\left(\dfrac{1}{9}+\dfrac{1}{9}\right)=\dfrac{4\pi i}{9}\).

§ PYQ — Laurent Series & Singularities (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Laurent Series in Two Annuli

Expand \(f(z)=\dfrac{1}{z(z-1)(z-2)}\) in Laurent series for \(1<|z|<2\).

Solution

Partial fractions: \(f=\dfrac{-1/2}{z}+\dfrac{1}{z-1}+\dfrac{-1/2}{z-2}\). In region \(1<|z|<2\): (i) \(1/z\): already in Laurent form, negative power of \(z\). (ii) \(1/(z-1)=\dfrac{1}{z}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-1/z}=\dfrac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-n}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-n-1}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-n}\) (since \(|1/z|<1\)). (iii) \(-1/(z-2)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-z/2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty(z/2)^n=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}\) (since \(|z/2|<1\)). Combining: \(f=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}-\dfrac{1}{2z}+\sum_{n=1}^\infty z^{-n}-\dfrac{1}{z}\).

PYQ 2022 — Classify Singularities

Classify singularities of \(f(z)=\dfrac{\sin z}{z^3}\).

Solution

\(\sin z=z-z^3/6+z^5/120-\cdots\). So \(f(z)=\dfrac{1}{z^2}-\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{z^2}{120}-\cdots\). Laurent series around \(z=0\): lowest power is \(z^{-2}\). So \(z=0\) is a pole of order 2. \(\text{Res}(f,0)=\) coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) = 0 (there is no \(z^{-1}\) term). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Essential Singularity

Show that \(z=0\) is an essential singularity of \(f(z)=e^{1/z}\) and find the residue.

Solution

\(e^{1/z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\dfrac{1}{n!\,z^n}=1+\dfrac{1}{z}+\dfrac{1}{2z^2}+\cdots\). Infinitely many negative powers → essential singularity. Residue = coefficient of \(z^{-1}\) = 1. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Poles of Complex Function

Find the poles and residues of \(f(z)=\dfrac{z^2}{(z+1)^2(z^2+1)}\).

Solution

Poles: \(z=-1\) (order 2), \(z=i\) (simple), \(z=-i\) (simple). Res at \(i\): \(\dfrac{i^2}{(i+1)^2(2i)}=\dfrac{-1}{(2i)(-1+1+2i)}=\dfrac{-1}{2i(2i)}=\dfrac{-1}{-4}=\dfrac{1}{4}\). Res at \(-i\): \(\dfrac{-1}{(-i+1)^2(-2i)}=\cdots=\dfrac{1}{4}\). Res at \(-1\): \(\dfrac{d}{dz}\!\left[\dfrac{z^2}{z^2+1}\right]_{z=-1}=\dfrac{2z(z^2+1)-z^2\cdot2z}{(z^2+1)^2}\bigg|_{-1}=\dfrac{-2\cdot2+(-1)\cdot(-2)}{4}=\dfrac{-4+2}{4}=\dfrac{-1}{2}\).

§ PYQ — Residue Theorem & Real Integrals (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Trigonometric Integral

Evaluate \(\displaystyle I=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{5-4\cos\theta}\).

Solution

Let \(z=e^{i\theta}\): \(\cos\theta=\dfrac{z+z^{-1}}{2}\), \(d\theta=dz/(iz)\). \(I=\oint_{|z|=1}\dfrac{1}{5-2(z+z^{-1})}\cdot\dfrac{dz}{iz}=\oint\dfrac{dz}{iz(5-2z-2/z)}=\oint\dfrac{dz}{i(5z-2z^2-2)}=\oint\dfrac{dz}{-i(2z^2-5z+2)}\). Factor: \(2z^2-5z+2=(2z-1)(z-2)\). Poles at \(z=1/2\) (inside) and \(z=2\) (outside). Res at \(z=1/2\): \(\dfrac{1}{-i\cdot2\cdot(1/2-2)}=\dfrac{1}{-i\cdot2\cdot(-3/2)}=\dfrac{1}{3i}\). \(I=2\pi i\cdot\dfrac{1}{3i}=\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Improper Integral

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{x^2}{(x^2+1)(x^2+4)}\,dx\).

Solution

Use semicircular contour (upper half plane). Poles in UHP: \(z=i\) and \(z=2i\). Res at \(i\): \(\dfrac{i^2}{(2i)(i^2+4)}=\dfrac{-1}{2i\cdot3}=\dfrac{-1}{6i}\). Res at \(2i\): \(\dfrac{(2i)^2}{(2i+1\cdot1)... }\) use formula: \(\dfrac{(2i)^2}{(4i^2+1)(4i)}=\dfrac{-4}{(-3)(4i)}=\dfrac{-4}{-12i}=\dfrac{1}{3i}\). Total residue: \(\dfrac{-1}{6i}+\dfrac{1}{3i}=\dfrac{-1+2}{6i}=\dfrac{1}{6i}\). \(I=2\pi i\cdot\dfrac{1}{6i}=\dfrac{\pi}{3}\).

PYQ 2022 — Integral with Exponential

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos x}{x^2+a^2}\,dx\) for \(a>0\).

Solution

Consider \(\oint_C\dfrac{e^{iz}}{z^2+a^2}dz\) over upper semicircle. Pole in UHP: \(z=ia\). Res: \(\dfrac{e^{i(ia)}}{2ia}=\dfrac{e^{-a}}{2ia}\). By Jordan's lemma: integral over arc \(\to0\). So \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\dfrac{e^{ix}}{x^2+a^2}dx=2\pi i\cdot\dfrac{e^{-a}}{2ia}=\dfrac{\pi e^{-a}}{a}\). Taking real part: \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\dfrac{\cos x}{x^2+a^2}dx=\dfrac{\pi e^{-a}}{a}\). Since integrand is even: \(\int_0^\infty\dfrac{\cos x}{x^2+a^2}dx=\dfrac{\pi e^{-a}}{2a}\).

PYQ 2020 — Integral via Residues

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\theta}{a+b\cos\theta}\), \(a>b>0\).

Solution

Standard substitution: \(z=e^{i\theta}\). Denominator becomes \(a+b(z+z^{-1})/2=\dfrac{bz^2+2az+b}{2z}\). Integral becomes \(\oint_{|z|=1}\dfrac{2\,dz}{i(bz^2+2az+b)}\). Roots of \(bz^2+2az+b=0\): \(z=\dfrac{-a\pm\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{b}\). Since \(a>b>0\): \(z_1=\dfrac{-a+\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}{b}\) has \(|z_1|<1\). Res at \(z_1\): \(\dfrac{2}{i\cdot(2bz_1+2a)}=\dfrac{1}{i(bz_1+a)}=\dfrac{1}{i\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\). \(I=2\pi i\cdot\dfrac{1}{i\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}=\dfrac{2\pi}{\sqrt{a^2-b^2}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Real Integral via Contour

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dx}{(1+x^2)^2}\).

Solution

Pole in UHP: \(z=i\) (order 2). Res at \(i\): \(\dfrac{d}{dz}\!\left[\dfrac{1}{(z+i)^2}\right]_{z=i}=\dfrac{-2}{(2i)^3}=\dfrac{-2}{-8i}=\dfrac{1}{4i}\). \(I=2\pi i\cdot\dfrac{1}{4i}=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Indented Contour

Show \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx=\frac{\pi}{2}\) using contour integration.

Solution

Consider \(f(z)=e^{iz}/z\). Integrate over indented semicircle (upper half plane, indentation at 0 of radius \(\epsilon\), large circle of radius \(R\)). By Cauchy's theorem (no poles inside): \(\int_{-R}^{-\epsilon}+\int_{\gamma_\epsilon}+\int_\epsilon^R+\int_{\Gamma_R}=0\). As \(\epsilon\to0,R\to\infty\): arc \(\Gamma_R\to0\) (Jordan); indentation \(\gamma_\epsilon\to-\pi i\cdot\text{Res}(f,0)=-\pi i\cdot1=-\pi i\). The real-axis parts: \(2i\int_0^\infty\dfrac{\sin x}{x}dx\). So \(2i\int_0^\infty\dfrac{\sin x}{x}dx-\pi i=0\Rightarrow\int_0^\infty\dfrac{\sin x}{x}dx=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Conformal Mapping (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Bilinear Transformation via 3 Points

Find the bilinear transformation mapping \(z=0,1,\infty\) to \(w=-1,0,1\).

Solution

Use cross-ratio: \(\dfrac{(w-w_1)(w_2-w_3)}{(w-w_3)(w_2-w_1)}=\dfrac{(z-z_1)(z_2-z_3)}{(z-z_3)(z_2-z_1)}\). With \((z_1,z_2,z_3)=(0,1,\infty)\) and \((w_1,w_2,w_3)=(-1,0,1)\): \(\dfrac{(w+1)(0-1)}{(w-1)(0+1)}=\dfrac{(z-0)(1-\infty)}{(z-\infty)(1-0)}\). For \(z_3=\infty\): \(\dfrac{z-0}{z-\infty}\cdot\dfrac{1-\infty}{1-0}\to\dfrac{z}{1}=z\) (limit as \(z_3\to\infty\)). So \(\dfrac{-(w+1)}{w-1}=z\Rightarrow-(w+1)=z(w-1)\Rightarrow-w-1=zw-z\Rightarrow-w-zw=1-z\Rightarrow w(-1-z)=(1-z)\Rightarrow w=\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}\). Verify: \(z=0\to w=-1\), \(z=1\to w=0\), \(z\to\infty\to w=1\). \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Map Upper Half-Plane to Disk

Find the bilinear transformation mapping the upper half-plane \(\text{Im}(z)>0\) to the unit disk \(|w|<1\).

Solution

Standard map: \(w=\dfrac{z-z_0}{z-\bar{z}_0}\) where \(z_0\) is in the UHP (maps \(z_0\mapsto0\)). Choosing \(z_0=i\): \(w=\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\). Check: \(z=0\to w=-1\), \(z=1\to w=\dfrac{1-i}{1+i}=\dfrac{(1-i)^2}{2}=\dfrac{-2i}{2}=-i\) (on circle). \(z=i\to w=0\) (center). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Conformal Mapping: w = z²

PYQ 2024/2023 — Taylor Series

(a) Expand \(e^z\) in a Taylor series about \(z=i\). (b) Expand \(\dfrac{\sin z}{z-\pi}\) about \(z=\pi\).

Solution

(a) \(e^z=e^i\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(z-i)^n}{n!}\), valid for all \(z\). (b) Let \(w=z-\pi\). \(\sin z=\sin(\pi+w)=-\sin w=-\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n w^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\). So the singularity at \(z=\pi\) is removable with value \(-1\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Laurent Series of 1/(z(z−1))

Find the Laurent series of \(\dfrac{1}{z(z-1)}\) in (a) \(0<|z|<1\) and (b) \(|z|>1\).

Solution

Partial fractions: \(\frac{1}{z(z-1)}=\frac{-1}{z}+\frac{1}{z-1}\).

(a) \(0<|z|<1\): \(\frac{1}{z-1}=\frac{-1}{1-z}=-\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n\). So \(f=\frac{-1}{z}-\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^n=-z^{-1}-1-z-z^2-\cdots\)

(b) \(|z|>1\): \(\frac{1}{z-1}=\frac{1}{z}\cdot\frac{1}{1-1/z}=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-n}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-(n+1)}\). So \(f=-z^{-1}+\sum_{n=0}^\infty z^{-(n+1)}=z^{-2}+z^{-3}+\cdots=\sum_{n=2}^\infty z^{-n}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Laurent Series in 1<|z|<∞

Find the Laurent series of \(\dfrac{1}{z^2-z}=\dfrac{1}{z(z-1)}\) valid for \(1<|z|<\infty\).

Solution

Same as above (b): \(\frac{1}{z(z-1)}=\sum_{n=2}^\infty \frac{1}{z^n}=\frac{1}{z^2}+\frac{1}{z^3}+\frac{1}{z^4}+\cdots\), valid for \(|z|>1\). The principal part (negative powers of \(z\)) is the entire series here, but since these are \(z^{-n}\) for \(n\geq2\), there is no \(z^{-1}\) term, so the residue at \(\infty\) is related to the \(z^{-1}\) coefficient, which is 0 in this expansion. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Laurent Series of e^{1/z}

Find the Laurent series of \(e^{1/z}\) at \(z=0\) and classify the singularity.

Solution

\(e^{1/z}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{1}{n!z^n}=1+\frac{1}{z}+\frac{1}{2!z^2}+\frac{1}{3!z^3}+\cdots\), valid for \(z\neq0\). By Picard's great theorem, \(e^{1/z}\) takes every complex value except possibly one in every punctured neighbourhood of \(0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Solution

PYQ 2010 — Zeros of Analytic Functions

Show that the zeros of a non-constant analytic function are isolated.

Solution

Suppose \(f\) is analytic at \(a\) and \(f(a)=0\). If \(f\not\equiv0\) near \(a\), expand: \(f(z)=(z-a)^m g(z)\) where \(g(a)\neq0\) and \(m\geq1\). PYQ 2009 — Argument Principle

State the argument principle and the formula \(N-P=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C\dfrac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz\).

Solution

Argument Principle: If \(f\) is meromorphic inside and on a simple closed contour \(C\) (with no zeros or poles on \(C\)), and \(N\) = number of zeros, \(P\) = number of poles (counted with multiplicity) inside \(C\), then \(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_C\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}dz=N-P\). The LHS equals \(\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta_C\arg f(z)\), the total change in argument of \(f\) as \(z\) traverses \(C\). Proof: near a zero of order \(m\), \(f'/f\) has a simple pole with residue \(+m\); near a pole of order \(p\), residue is \(-p\). Sum gives \(N-P\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Rouché's Theorem (Proof Outline)

State and prove Rouché's theorem.

Solution

Rouché's Theorem: If \(f,g\) are analytic inside and on a simple closed contour \(C\), and \(|g(z)|<|f(z)|\) on \(C\), then \(f\) and \(f+g\) have the same number of zeros inside \(C\). Proof: Let \(h(z)=1+g(z)/f(z)\). On \(C\): \(|g/f|<1\), so \(h(C)\) lies in \(\text{Re}(w)>0\) (open right half-plane), which excludes \(0\) and the negative real axis. By the argument principle, number of zeros of \(f+g\) minus poles \(=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{(f+g)'}{f+g}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{f'}{f}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint\frac{h'}{h}\). The last integral is zero since \(h\) has no zeros/poles (i.e.\ the winding number of \(h(C)\) around \(0\) is 0). So zeros of \(f+g=\) zeros of \(f\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Identity Theorem

State the identity theorem for analytic functions and give an application.

Solution

Identity Theorem: If \(f\) and \(g\) are analytic on a connected open set \(\Omega\) and \(f(z_n)=g(z_n)\) for a sequence \(z_n\to z_0\in\Omega\) with \(z_n\neq z_0\), then \(f\equiv g\) on \(\Omega\). Proof: \(h=f-g\) is analytic and has \(z_0\) as a non-isolated zero, so \(h\equiv0\). Application: Since \(\sin^2z+\cos^2z=1\) holds on the real axis (a sequence accumulating everywhere), it holds for all \(z\in\mathbb{C}\). Similarly \(e^{z_1+z_2}=e^{z_1}e^{z_2}\) extends from reals to all of \(\mathbb{C}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2006 — Schwarz Reflection Principle

State the Schwarz reflection principle.

Solution

Schwarz Reflection Principle: If \(f\) is analytic in a region \(\Omega\) in the upper half-plane, continuous on the portion of the real axis bounding \(\Omega\), and real-valued on that real boundary, then \(f\) extends analytically to the reflection \(\Omega^*=\{\bar{z}:z\in\Omega\}\) by \(f(\bar{z})=\overline{f(z)}\). More generally applies to reflection across any arc. Application: If \(f\) maps an arc of the real axis to the real axis, it extends across by the formula above. Used to extend power series from the real axis and in conformal mapping. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Schwarz-Christoffel Transformation

Describe the Schwarz-Christoffel transformation and its purpose.

Solution

The Schwarz-Christoffel formula maps the upper half-plane conformally onto a polygon. If the polygon has vertices \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) with interior angles \(\alpha_k\pi\), the mapping is \(f'(z)=A\prod_{k=1}^n(z-x_k)^{\alpha_k-1}\) where \(x_k\) are the preimages on the real axis and \(A\) is a complex constant. Integrating gives \(f\). Application: maps UHP to rectangles (elliptic integrals), triangles, slits. For a rectangle with angles \(\pi/2\): involves the Schwarz-Christoffel integral \(\int(1-t^2)^{-1/2}(1-k^2t^2)^{-1/2}dt\) — an elliptic integral. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Bilinear Map: Upper Half-Plane to Itself

Find a bilinear map sending the upper half-plane to itself, fixing \(0\) and \(i\).

Solution

Maps from UHP to itself have the form \(w=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\) with \(a,b,c,d\) real and \(ad-bc>0\). Fixed points: \(z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\Rightarrow cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0\). Fixing \(z=0\): \(0=\frac{a\cdot0+b}{c\cdot0+d}=\frac{b}{d}\Rightarrow b=0\). Then \(w=\frac{az}{cz+d}\). Fixing \(z=i\): \(i=\frac{ai}{ci+d}\Rightarrow i(ci+d)=ai\Rightarrow-c+di=ai\). Real part: \(-c=0\Rightarrow c=0\). Imaginary: \(d=a\). So \(w=\frac{az}{a}=z\) (identity). The only bilinear map fixing both \(0\) and \(i\) in the UHP is the identity. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — w=sin z: Image of Vertical Strip

Solution

Write \(z=x+iy\) with \(|x|<\pi/2\). Then \(w=\sin(x+iy)=\sin x\cosh y+i\cos x\sinh y\). Set \(u=\sin x\cosh y,\,v=\cos x\sinh y\). For fixed \(x\neq0\): \(\frac{u^2}{\sin^2x}-\frac{v^2}{\cos^2 x}=\cosh^2y-\sinh^2y=1\) (hyperbola in \(w\)-plane). For fixed \(y\): \(\frac{u^2}{\cosh^2y}+\frac{v^2}{\sinh^2y}=1\) (ellipse). The strip maps onto the entire \(w\)-plane. The boundary \(x=\pi/2\): \(u=\cosh y,\,v=0\), giving \([1,\infty)\) on the real axis (a slit). So \(\sin z\) maps the open strip conformally onto \(\mathbb{C}\setminus((-\infty,-1]\cup[1,\infty))\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Conformality: Preservation of Angles

Prove that analytic functions with non-zero derivative are conformal (angle-preserving).

Solution

Let \(f\) be analytic at \(z_0\) with \(f'(z_0)\neq0\). Two smooth curves \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) meet at \(z_0\) with tangent directions \(\tau_1,\tau_2\). Under \(f\), the image curves have tangent directions \(f'(z_0)\tau_1\) and \(f'(z_0)\tau_2\). The angle between image curves is \(\arg\frac{f'(z_0)\tau_1}{f'(z_0)\tau_2}=\arg\frac{\tau_1}{\tau_2}\) — unchanged. So angles and their orientation are preserved. Where \(f'(z_0)=0\), angles are multiplied by the order of the zero: the map is not conformal there. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Riemann Mapping Theorem

State the Riemann mapping theorem.

Solution

Riemann Mapping Theorem: Every simply connected proper open subset \(\Omega\subsetneq\mathbb{C}\) is conformally equivalent to the open unit disk \(\mathbb{D}=\{|z|<1\}\). That is, there exists a bijective analytic function \(f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}\) with analytic inverse. Moreover, if \(z_0\in\Omega\), the map is unique if we require \(f(z_0)=0\) and \(f'(z_0)>0\). Note: \(\mathbb{C}\) itself is excluded (Liouville: any bounded entire function is constant, so no biholomorphism \(\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{D}\) exists). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Harmonic Measure

Define harmonic measure and describe its role in solving the Dirichlet problem.

Solution

Let \(\Omega\) be a domain and \(E\subset\partial\Omega\). The harmonic measure \(\omega(z,E,\Omega)\) is the harmonic function on \(\Omega\) with boundary values \(1\) on \(E\) and \(0\) on \(\partial\Omega\setminus E\). Interpretation: \(\omega(z,E,\Omega)\) is the probability that Brownian motion starting at \(z\) first hits \(\partial\Omega\) in \(E\). For the Dirichlet problem with boundary data \(\phi\) on \(\partial\Omega\): \(u(z)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\phi(\zeta)\,d\omega(z,\zeta,\Omega)\). For the disk: the harmonic measure is given by the Poisson kernel. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Dirichlet Problem via Conformal Mapping

Explain how conformal mappings can be used to solve the Dirichlet problem in a complicated domain.

Solution

Strategy: (1) Find a conformal map \(f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}\) (Riemann mapping theorem). (2) Solve the Dirichlet problem in \(\mathbb{D}\) with the transformed boundary data using the Poisson integral formula: \(u(re^{i\theta})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{(1-r^2)\phi(e^{it})}{1-2r\cos(\theta-t)+r^2}dt\). (3) Pull back: \(\tilde{u}=u\circ f\) is harmonic in \(\Omega\) (harmonic functions compose with conformal maps). Boundary values are preserved. This works because harmonicity is invariant under conformal mappings. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Joukowski Airfoil: Flow Past Cylinder

Briefly describe how the Joukowski transformation is used to study flow past an airfoil.

Solution

The complex potential for uniform flow past a cylinder of radius \(a\) is \(F(z)=U(z+a^2/z)\) (superposition of uniform flow and doublet). The Joukowski map \(w=z+c^2/z\) maps the cylinder \(|z|=a\) to an airfoil shape in the \(w\)-plane. The complex velocity in the \(w\)-plane is \(dF/dw=(dF/dz)/(dw/dz)\), giving the flow field around the airfoil. The Kutta condition (smooth flow at trailing edge) determines the circulation, and the Kutta-Joukowski theorem gives lift \(=\rho U\Gamma\) per unit span. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Electrostatics via Conformal Mapping

Explain how conformal mapping can solve electrostatic problems (potential in a transformed domain).

Solution

The electrostatic potential \(\phi\) satisfies Laplace's equation \(\nabla^2\phi=0\). If \(w=f(z)\) is conformal, the composition \(\phi\circ f^{-1}\) is also harmonic. Strategy: map the given electrode configuration to a simpler geometry (e.g.\ parallel plates), solve there, then pull back. Example: Two concentric cylinders \(r=a,b\) — map to a strip via \(w=\log z\), where the potential is linear. Pull back gives \(\phi=A\log r+B\) (familiar coaxial result). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — w=z+1/z: Critical Points and Circles

Find the critical points of \(w=z+1/z\) and describe what happens to circles \(|z|=r\).

Solution

Critical points: \(dw/dz=1-1/z^2=0\Rightarrow z=\pm1\). At these points conformality fails. For the circle \(|z|=r\): \(w=(r+1/r)\cos\theta+i(r-1/r)\sin\theta\). PYQ 2010 — Hyperbolic Functions: cosh(iz)=cos z

Prove that \(\cosh(iz)=\cos z\) and \(\sinh(iz)=i\sin z\).

Solution

\(\cosh(iz)=\frac{e^{iz}+e^{-iz}}{2}=\cos z\) by Euler's formula. \(\sinh(iz)=\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2}=\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2}=i\cdot\frac{e^{iz}-e^{-iz}}{2i}=i\sin z\). Conversely: \(\cos(iz)=\cosh z\) and \(\sin(iz)=i\sinh z\). These identities allow trigonometric identities to be converted to hyperbolic ones: e.g.\ \(\cos^2z+\sin^2z=1\Rightarrow\cosh^2z-\sinh^2z=1\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2007 — Complex Roots of Unity

Prove that the sum of all \(n\)-th roots of unity is zero, and find their product.

Solution

The \(n\)-th roots of unity are \(\omega_k=e^{2\pi ik/n}\) for \(k=0,1,\ldots,n-1\). They are the roots of \(z^n-1=0\). By Vieta's formulas: sum \(=0\) (coefficient of \(z^{n-1}\) in \(z^n-1\) is \(0\)). Product \(=(-1)^n\cdot(-1)=(-1)^{n+1}\)... More carefully: product of roots of \(z^n-1\) is \((-1)^n\cdot(-1)=(-1)^{n+1}\)... Actually by Vieta, product \(=(-1)^n\cdot\frac{-1}{1}=(-1)^{n+1}\). For even \(n\): product \(=(-1)^{n+1}=-1\).
