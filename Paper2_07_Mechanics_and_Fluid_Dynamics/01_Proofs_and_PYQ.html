§ Proofs — Lagrangian & Hamiltonian Mechanics

Lagrange's Equations of Motion — Derivation

For a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates \(q_i\), the equations of motion are \(\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}_i}\right)-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0\) where \(L=T-V\).

Derivation via Hamilton's Principle

Step 1 Hamilton's principle: \(\delta\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L(q_i,\dot q_i,t)\,dt=0\).

Step 2 Vary: \(\delta\int L\,dt=\int\!\left(\sum_i\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}\delta q_i+\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\delta\dot q_i\right)dt\).

Step 3 Integrate second term by parts (\(\delta\dot q_i=\frac{d}{dt}\delta q_i\)): \(\int\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\delta\dot q_i\,dt=\left[\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\delta q_i\right]_{t_1}^{t_2}-\int\!\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\right)\delta q_i\,dt\). Boundary: \(\delta q_i=0\) at endpoints.

Step 4 So \(\delta\int L\,dt=\int\sum_i\!\left[\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}-\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\right)\right]\delta q_i\,dt=0\). Since \(\delta q_i\) are arbitrary: \(\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\right)-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Hamilton's Equations — Derivation from Lagrangian

With \(p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i\) and \(H=\sum p_i\dot q_i-L\): \(\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i\) and \(\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i\).

Derivation (Legendre Transform)

\(dH=\sum(p_i\,d\dot q_i+\dot q_i\,dp_i)-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}\,dq_i-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\,d\dot q_i\). Since \(p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i\): \(p_i\,d\dot q_i-\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}d\dot q_i=0\). So \(dH=\sum(\dot q_i\,dp_i-\dot p_i\,dq_i)\) (using Lagrange eqs: \(\dot p_i=\partial L/\partial q_i\)). Comparing with \(dH=\sum\!\left(\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}dp_i+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}dq_i\right)\): \(\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i\) and \(\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i\). \(\blacksquare\)

Conservation of Energy (Hamiltonian = Total Energy)

If \(L\) does not depend explicitly on time, then \(H=T+V=\) total energy is conserved.

Proof

\(\dfrac{dH}{dt}=\sum\!\left(\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}\dot q_i+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\dot p_i\right)+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t}=\sum(-\dot p_i\dot q_i+\dot q_i\dot p_i)+\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t}=\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t}\). If \(L\) (and hence \(H\)) does not depend on \(t\): \(dH/dt=0\Rightarrow H=\text{const}\). For holonomic systems with time-independent constraints: \(H=T+V\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Fluid Kinematics

Equation of Continuity — Derivation

For incompressible fluid: \(\text{div}(\mathbf{v})=0\), i.e., \(\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y}+\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial z}=0\).

Derivation (Control Volume)

Consider a small parallelepiped \([x,x+\delta x]\times[y,y+\delta y]\times[z,z+\delta z]\). Mass flux out in \(x\)-direction: \(\rho(u+\partial_x(\rho u)\delta x)\delta y\delta z-\rho u\delta y\delta z=\partial_x(\rho u)\delta x\delta y\delta z\). Similarly for \(y,z\). Total rate of mass increase inside: \(-\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}\delta x\delta y\delta z\). Conservation: \(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\text{div}(\rho\mathbf{v})=0\). For incompressible (\(\rho=\text{const}\)): \(\text{div}(\mathbf{v})=\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y}+\dfrac{\partial w}{\partial z}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Irrotational Flow ↔ Velocity Potential

Flow is irrotational (\(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=0\)) iff there exists a scalar \(\phi\) (velocity potential) such that \(\mathbf{v}=\nabla\phi\).

Proof

If \(\mathbf{v}=\nabla\phi\): \(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=\nabla\times\nabla\phi=0\) (curl of gradient is zero). Conversely, if \(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=0\) in a simply-connected domain: by Poincaré lemma there exists \(\phi\) with \(\mathbf{v}=\nabla\phi\). \(\blacksquare\)

For incompressible irrotational: \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi)=0\Rightarrow\nabla^2\phi=0\) (Laplace equation). So \(\phi\) is harmonic.

§ Proofs — Bernoulli's Theorem

Bernoulli's Equation — Proof

Along a streamline in steady, irrotational, incompressible flow: \(p+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2+\rho gz=\text{constant}\).

Proof (via Euler's equation)

Step 1 Euler's equation of motion for inviscid flow: \(\rho\dfrac{D\mathbf{v}}{Dt}=-\nabla p+\rho\mathbf{g}\). For steady flow: \(\dfrac{D\mathbf{v}}{Dt}=(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\).

Step 2 Use identity: \((\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=\nabla(v^2/2)-\mathbf{v}\times(\nabla\times\mathbf{v})\). For irrotational flow: \(\nabla\times\mathbf{v}=0\). So Euler's equation: \(\rho\nabla(v^2/2)=-\nabla p-\rho\nabla(gz)\) (taking \(\mathbf{g}=-g\hat{z}\)).

Step 3 \(\nabla\!\left(\dfrac{p}{\rho}+\dfrac{v^2}{2}+gz\right)=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{p}{\rho}+\dfrac{v^2}{2}+gz=\text{const}\). Multiply by \(\rho\): \(p+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2+\rho gz=C\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Fluid Dynamics — Stream Function & Potential

Stream Function for 2D Incompressible Flow

For 2D incompressible flow with continuity \(\partial u/\partial x+\partial v/\partial y=0\): there exists \(\psi\) (stream function) with \(u=\partial\psi/\partial y,v=-\partial\psi/\partial x\). Streamlines: \(\psi=\text{const}\).

Proof

The continuity equation \(\partial u/\partial x+\partial v/\partial y=0\) is the compatibility condition for existence of \(\psi\) with \(u=\psi_y,v=-\psi_x\). Check: \(\psi_{yx}-(-\psi_{xy})=0\) automatically (mixed partials equal). For irrotational flow additionally: \(\partial v/\partial x-\partial u/\partial y=0\Rightarrow-\psi_{xx}-\psi_{yy}=0\Rightarrow\nabla^2\psi=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Complex potential: \(w(z)=\phi+i\psi\) is analytic, \(dw/dz=u-iv\) (complex velocity).

§ PYQ — Lagrangian Mechanics (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Lagrangian for Atwood Machine

Set up Lagrange's equation for an Atwood machine (masses \(m_1,m_2\) connected by inextensible string over pulley).

Solution

Generalized coordinate: \(q=x\) (descent of \(m_1\)). \(T=\dfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)\dot x^2\). \(V=-m_1gx+m_2gx=(m_2-m_1)gx\). \(L=T-V=\dfrac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2)\dot x^2-(m_2-m_1)gx\). Lagrange's equation: \((m_1+m_2)\ddot x=(m_1-m_2)g\Rightarrow\ddot x=\dfrac{(m_1-m_2)g}{m_1+m_2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Simple Pendulum via Lagrangian

Derive the equation of motion of a simple pendulum of length \(l\) using Lagrange's method.

Solution

Coordinate: \(\theta\) (angle from vertical). \(T=\dfrac{1}{2}ml^2\dot\theta^2\), \(V=-mgl\cos\theta\). \(L=\dfrac{ml^2\dot\theta^2}{2}+mgl\cos\theta\). \(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\theta}=ml^2\dot\theta\), \(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}=-mgl\sin\theta\). Lagrange: \(ml^2\ddot\theta+mgl\sin\theta=0\Rightarrow\ddot\theta+\dfrac{g}{l}\sin\theta=0\). For small \(\theta\): \(\ddot\theta+\dfrac{g}{l}\theta=0\) (SHM with \(\omega^2=g/l\), period \(T=2\pi\sqrt{l/g}\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Conservation Law: Cyclic Coordinate

For a particle moving in a central field, show that angular momentum is conserved using Lagrangian mechanics.

Solution

Use polar coordinates: \(T=\dfrac{m}{2}(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2)\), \(V=V(r)\). \(L=\dfrac{m}{2}(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\theta^2)-V(r)\). Since \(\theta\) does not appear in \(L\) (cyclic): \(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot\theta}\right)=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{d}{dt}(mr^2\dot\theta)=0\Rightarrow L_z=mr^2\dot\theta=\text{const}\) (angular momentum). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Hamilton's Equations

Find Hamilton's equations for a harmonic oscillator with Lagrangian \(L=\dfrac{m\dot x^2}{2}-\dfrac{kx^2}{2}\).

Solution

Generalized momentum: \(p=\partial L/\partial\dot x=m\dot x\Rightarrow\dot x=p/m\). Hamiltonian: \(H=p\dot x-L=\dfrac{p^2}{m}-\left(\dfrac{p^2}{2m}-\dfrac{kx^2}{2}\right)=\dfrac{p^2}{2m}+\dfrac{kx^2}{2}=T+V\). Hamilton's equations: \(\dot x=\partial H/\partial p=p/m\), \(\dot p=-\partial H/\partial x=-kx\). Eliminating \(p\): \(\ddot x=-kx/m\Rightarrow\) SHM. \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Fluid Flow & Potential (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Velocity Potential

Show that \(\phi=x^2-y^2\) is a velocity potential for 2D flow. Find the velocity and stream function.

Solution

Check: \(\nabla^2\phi=2-2=0\). \(\checkmark\) (\(\phi\) is harmonic). Velocity: \(u=\phi_x=2x,v=\phi_y=-2y\). Stream function: \(\psi_y=u=2x\Rightarrow\psi=2xy+f(x)\). \(\psi_x=-v=2y\Rightarrow2y+f'(x)=2y\Rightarrow f'=0\Rightarrow f=\text{const}\). Stream function: \(\psi=2xy\). Complex potential: \(w=\phi+i\psi=(x^2-y^2)+2ixy=(x+iy)^2=z^2\). Streamlines: \(2xy=C\) (rectangular hyperbolas). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Irrotational Flow

For the flow \(u=ax,v=-ay\) (where \(a>0\)), verify irrotationality, find velocity potential and stream function.

Solution

Continuity: \(\partial u/\partial x+\partial v/\partial y=a-a=0\). \(\checkmark\) Irrotationality: \(\partial v/\partial x-\partial u/\partial y=0-0=0\). \(\checkmark\) Velocity potential: \(\phi_x=u=ax\Rightarrow\phi=ax^2/2+g(y)\). \(\phi_y=g'(y)=v=-ay\Rightarrow g=-ay^2/2\). \(\phi=a(x^2-y^2)/2\). Stream function: \(\psi=axy\) (by analogous calculation, or since \(w=az^2/2\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Source and Sink

A source of strength \(m\) is at the origin in a uniform stream with velocity \(U\) in \(x\)-direction. Find the complex potential and stagnation point.

Solution

Complex potential: \(w=Uz+\dfrac{m}{2\pi}\ln z\). Velocity: \(\dfrac{dw}{dz}=U+\dfrac{m}{2\pi z}=u-iv\). Stagnation point: \(dw/dz=0\Rightarrow U=-m/(2\pi z)\Rightarrow z=-m/(2\pi U)\). Stagnation point at \((-m/(2\pi U),0)\) on negative \(x\)-axis. \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Bernoulli & Applications (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Venturimeter

A venturimeter has inlet diameter 20 cm and throat 10 cm. If the pressure difference is \(h=25\) cm of mercury, find the flow rate. (Take \(C_d=0.98\).)

Solution

\(A_1=\pi(0.1)^2=0.01\pi\) m², \(A_2=\pi(0.05)^2=0.0025\pi\) m². \(h=0.25\times13.6=3.4\) m of water. Flow rate: \(Q=C_d\dfrac{A_1A_2\sqrt{2gh}}{\sqrt{A_1^2-A_2^2}}\). \(\sqrt{2gh}=\sqrt{2\times9.81\times3.4}=\sqrt{66.708}=8.168\) m/s. \(A_1^2-A_2^2=(0.01\pi)^2-(0.0025\pi)^2=\pi^2(10^{-4}-6.25\times10^{-6})=\pi^2\times9.375\times10^{-5}\). \(\sqrt{A_1^2-A_2^2}=\pi\times9.682\times10^{-3}\). \(Q=0.98\times\dfrac{0.01\pi\times0.0025\pi\times8.168}{\pi\times9.682\times10^{-3}}=0.98\times\dfrac{2.5\times10^{-5}\pi\times8.168}{9.682\times10^{-3}}\approx0.98\times0.006624\approx0.00649\) m³/s \(=6.49\) litres/sec. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Torricelli's Theorem

Prove Torricelli's theorem: speed of efflux from a small hole at depth \(h\) below the free surface is \(v=\sqrt{2gh}\).

Solution

Apply Bernoulli between free surface (point A) and hole (point B). At A: \(p_A=p_0\) (atmosphere), \(v_A\approx0\) (large tank), height \(=h\) above hole. At B: \(p_B=p_0,v_B=v\), height \(=0\). Bernoulli: \(p_0+0+\rho gh=p_0+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2+0\Rightarrow\rho gh=\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2\Rightarrow v=\sqrt{2gh}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Equation of Continuity Application

Water flows through a pipe that narrows from cross-section \(A_1=0.04\) m² to \(A_2=0.01\) m². If velocity at wide end is \(v_1=2\) m/s, find \(v_2\) and the pressure difference (taking \(\rho=1000\) kg/m³).

Solution

Continuity: \(A_1v_1=A_2v_2\Rightarrow v_2=\dfrac{A_1v_1}{A_2}=\dfrac{0.04\times2}{0.01}=8\) m/s. Bernoulli (horizontal pipe): \(p_1+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_1^2=p_2+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v_2^2\Rightarrow p_1-p_2=\dfrac{1}{2}\rho(v_2^2-v_1^2)=\dfrac{1000}{2}(64-4)=30000\) Pa \(=30\) kPa. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Doublet & Flow Past Cylinder

Show that the complex potential \(w=Uz+\dfrac{Ua^2}{z}\) represents uniform flow past a cylinder of radius \(a\). Find the pressure distribution on the cylinder.

Solution

\(w=U\!\left(z+\dfrac{a^2}{z}\right)=U\!\left(re^{i\theta}+\dfrac{a^2}{r}e^{-i\theta}\right)=U\!\left(r+\dfrac{a^2}{r}\right)\cos\theta+iU\!\left(r-\dfrac{a^2}{r}\right)\sin\theta\). Stream function: \(\psi=U\!\left(r-\dfrac{a^2}{r}\right)\sin\theta\). On \(r=a\): \(\psi=0\). So \(r=a\) is a streamline — the surface of the cylinder. \(\checkmark\) Velocity: \(\dfrac{dw}{dz}=U\!\left(1-\dfrac{a^2}{z^2}\right)\). On \(r=a\): \(|dw/dz|^2=4U^2\sin^2\theta\Rightarrow v^2=4U^2\sin^2\theta\). Bernoulli: \(p+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho v^2=p_\infty+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho U^2\). So \(p=p_\infty+\dfrac{1}{2}\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\). \(\blacksquare\) Note: D'Alembert's paradox — net drag = 0 for ideal fluid.

§ PYQ — Lagrangian: Advanced Systems (2000–2024)

PYQ 2018 — Bead on Rotating Wire

A bead of mass \(m\) slides without friction on a straight wire rotating in a horizontal plane with constant angular velocity \(\omega\). Write the Lagrangian and find the equation of motion.

Solution

Let \(r\) = distance of bead from origin (generalized coordinate). \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}m(\dot r^2+r^2\omega^2)\), \(V=0\) (horizontal plane). \(L=\tfrac{1}{2}m(\dot r^2+r^2\omega^2)\). Lagrange equation: \(\ddot r-\omega^2 r=0\). Solution: \(r=Ae^{\omega t}+Be^{-\omega t}\). The bead moves outward exponentially (centrifugal effect). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Double Pendulum (Small Oscillations)

A double pendulum has two masses \(m\) each on rods of length \(l\) each. Set up the Lagrangian and find the frequencies of small oscillations.

Solution

Coordinates: \(\theta_1,\theta_2\) (angles of upper, lower rods). For small angles: \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}m[2l^2\dot\theta_1^2+l^2\dot\theta_2^2+2l^2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2]\), \(V=mgl[2(1-\cos\theta_1)+(1-\cos\theta_2)]\approx mgl[\theta_1^2+\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_2^2]\times$ (approx)$. More precisely: \(V\approx mgl\theta_1^2+\tfrac{mgl}{2}\theta_2^2\) (small angle). Equations: \(2\ddot\theta_1+\ddot\theta_2+\tfrac{2g}{l}\theta_1=0\), \(\ddot\theta_1+\ddot\theta_2+\tfrac{g}{l}\theta_2=0\). For normal modes \(\theta_i=A_i e^{i\omega t}\): characteristic equation \(\omega^4-4\tfrac{g}{l}\omega^2+2\tfrac{g^2}{l^2}=0\). Frequencies: \(\omega^2=\tfrac{g}{l}(2\pm\sqrt{2})\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2014 — Spherical Pendulum

Obtain the Lagrangian for a spherical pendulum (mass \(m\), length \(l\)) and write the equations of motion.

Solution

Generalized coordinates: \(\theta\) (polar), \(\phi\) (azimuthal). \(T=\tfrac{ml^2}{2}(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2)\), \(V=-mgl\cos\theta\). \(L=\tfrac{ml^2}{2}(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi^2)+mgl\cos\theta\). Lagrange equations: \(ml^2\ddot\theta=ml^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\dot\phi^2-mgl\sin\theta\); \(\dfrac{d}{dt}(ml^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi)=0\Rightarrow l_z=ml^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\phi=\text{const}\) (conserved angular momentum about vertical). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2012 — Rolling Disk on Inclined Plane

A disk of mass \(m\), radius \(R\) rolls without slipping down an inclined plane of angle \(\alpha\). Find the acceleration using Lagrangian mechanics.

Solution

Generalized coordinate: \(x\) (distance down the plane). Rolling constraint: \(\dot x=R\dot\phi\Rightarrow\phi=x/R\). \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}m\dot x^2+\tfrac{1}{2}I\dot\phi^2\) where \(I=\tfrac{1}{2}mR^2\). So \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}m\dot x^2+\tfrac{1}{4}m\dot x^2=\tfrac{3}{4}m\dot x^2\). \(V=-mgx\sin\alpha\). Lagrange: \(\tfrac{3}{2}m\ddot x=mg\sin\alpha\Rightarrow\ddot x=\tfrac{2g\sin\alpha}{3}\). (Slower than free slide, due to rotational inertia.) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2010 — Charged Particle in EM Field

Write the Lagrangian for a particle of charge \(q\), mass \(m\) in electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) and magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}\). Show that Lagrange's equations give the Lorentz force law.

Solution

Lagrangian: \(L=\tfrac{1}{2}m\dot{\mathbf{r}}^2-q\phi+q\dot{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\mathbf{A}\) where \(\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\phi-\partial\mathbf{A}/\partial t\), \(\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}\). Generalized momentum: \(p_i=m\dot x_i+qA_i\). Lagrange equation for \(x\)-component: \(\dfrac{d}{dt}(m\dot x+qA_x)=q\dfrac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}+q\dot{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{A}}{\partial x}\). Expanding \(\dfrac{dA_x}{dt}=\partial_t A_x+(\dot{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\nabla)A_x\) and using vector identities: \(m\ddot x=q(E_x+(\dot{\mathbf{r}}\times\mathbf{B})_x)\). Hence \(m\ddot{\mathbf{r}}=q(\mathbf{E}+\dot{\mathbf{r}}\times\mathbf{B})\) — the Lorentz force. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2008 — Degrees of Freedom

A rigid body moves freely in space. State the number of degrees of freedom. For \(N\) particles with \(k\) holonomic constraints, what is the number of generalized coordinates needed?

Solution

A free rigid body has 6 degrees of freedom: 3 translational (position of centre of mass) + 3 rotational (orientation). For \(N\) particles: \(3N\) Cartesian coordinates. With \(k\) independent holonomic constraints (each reduces DOF by 1): number of generalized coordinates \(= 3N - k\). E.g., rigid body of \(N\) particles has \(3N\) coordinates but \(\binom{N}{2}\) distances fixed → reduces to 6 DOF. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2006 — Lagrangian for Coupled Spring System

Two masses \(m\) are connected by springs of stiffness \(k\) (wall–mass1–spring–mass2–wall). Find the Lagrangian and normal mode frequencies.

Solution

Let \(x_1,x_2\) be displacements from equilibrium. \(T=\tfrac{m}{2}(\dot x_1^2+\dot x_2^2)\), \(V=\tfrac{k}{2}(x_1^2+(x_2-x_1)^2+x_2^2)=\tfrac{k}{2}(2x_1^2-2x_1x_2+2x_2^2)\). Equations: \(m\ddot x_1+k(2x_1-x_2)=0\), \(m\ddot x_2+k(2x_2-x_1)=0\). For \((x_1,x_2)\propto e^{i\omega t}\): \(\det\begin{pmatrix}2k-m\omega^2&-k\\-k&2k-m\omega^2\end{pmatrix}=0\Rightarrow(2k-m\omega^2)^2-k^2=0\Rightarrow m\omega^2=k\text{ or }3k\). Frequencies: \(\omega_1=\sqrt{k/m}\) (symmetric mode), \(\omega_2=\sqrt{3k/m}\) (antisymmetric). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2004 — Particle on Rotating Hoop

A bead of mass \(m\) is constrained to a hoop of radius \(R\) rotating with angular velocity \(\omega\) about its vertical diameter. Find the equilibrium angle and stability condition.

Solution

Coordinate: \(\theta\) (angle from bottom). \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}mR^2(\dot\theta^2+\omega^2\sin^2\theta)\), \(V=-mgR\cos\theta\). Effective potential: \(V_{\text{eff}}=-mgR\cos\theta-\tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2R^2\sin^2\theta\). Equation of motion: \(mR^2\ddot\theta=mR^2\omega^2\sin\theta\cos\theta-mgR\sin\theta=mR\sin\theta(R\omega^2\cos\theta-g)\). Equilibria: (i) \(\theta=0\) (bottom), (ii) \(\theta=\pi\) (top), (iii) \(\cos\theta=g/(R\omega^2)\) (for \(\omega^2>g/R\)). Bottom equilibrium is stable iff \(\omega^2g/R\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Hamiltonian Mechanics & Poisson Brackets (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Poisson Brackets

Show that for any function \(f(q,p,t)\), \(\dfrac{df}{dt}=\{f,H\}+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}\), where \(\{f,H\}\) is the Poisson bracket.

Solution

\(\dfrac{df}{dt}=\sum_i\!\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial q_i}\dot q_i+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\dot p_i\right)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}\). Using Hamilton's equations \(\dot q_i=\partial H/\partial p_i\), \(\dot p_i=-\partial H/\partial q_i\): \(\dfrac{df}{dt}=\sum_i\!\left(\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial q_i}\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}-\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial p_i}\dfrac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}\right)+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\{f,H\}+\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}\). If \(f\) is a constant of motion: \(\{f,H\}+\partial f/\partial t=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Canonical Transformation

Show that the transformation \(Q=p, P=-q\) is canonical. Find the generating function.

Solution

A transformation is canonical if it preserves the Poisson bracket structure: \(\{Q,P\}=1\). Check: \(\{Q,P\}=\{p,-q\}=\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial q}\dfrac{\partial(-q)}{\partial p}-\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial p}\dfrac{\partial(-q)}{\partial q}=0-1\cdot(-1)=1\). \(\checkmark\) Generating function \(F_1(q,Q)\): \(p=\partial F_1/\partial q\), \(P=-\partial F_1/\partial Q\). Since \(Q=p\): \(p=Q\Rightarrow\partial F_1/\partial q=Q\Rightarrow F_1=qQ\). Check: \(P=-\partial F_1/\partial Q=-q\). \(\checkmark\) So \(F_1=qQ\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Phase Space and Liouville's Theorem

State Liouville's theorem for Hamiltonian systems. Show that the phase space volume is conserved under Hamiltonian flow.

Solution

Liouville's theorem: The phase space distribution function \(\rho(q_i,p_i,t)\) satisfies \(d\rho/dt=0\) — the density of representative points in phase space is constant along trajectories. Proof: The divergence of the Hamiltonian flow field \((\dot q_i,\dot p_i)\) is \(\sum_i\!\left(\dfrac{\partial\dot q_i}{\partial q_i}+\dfrac{\partial\dot p_i}{\partial p_i}\right)=\sum_i\!\left(\dfrac{\partial^2 H}{\partial q_i\partial p_i}-\dfrac{\partial^2 H}{\partial p_i\partial q_i}\right)=0\). By the continuity equation in phase space: \(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v})=0\Rightarrow d\rho/dt=0\). Hence phase volume is conserved. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Hamilton-Jacobi Equation

Write the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for a particle of mass \(m\) moving in a potential \(V(q)\). Show that for a harmonic oscillator it separates.

Solution

The Hamilton-Jacobi equation: \(H\!\left(q,\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial q},t\right)+\dfrac{\partial S}{\partial t}=0\). For time-independent \(H\): put \(S=W(q)-Et\). Then \(\dfrac{1}{2m}\!\left(\dfrac{dW}{dq}\right)^2+V(q)=E\Rightarrow\dfrac{dW}{dq}=\sqrt{2m(E-V(q))}\). For harmonic oscillator \(V=\tfrac{1}{2}kq^2\): \(W=\int\sqrt{2m(E-\tfrac{1}{2}kq^2)}\,dq\), which is elementary. This separates cleanly into a 1D integral. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Action-Angle Variables

Define action variable \(J=\oint p\,dq\) for a 1D harmonic oscillator \(H=p^2/(2m)+kq^2/2\). Show \(H=\omega J/(2\pi)\).

Solution

Phase curve: \(p^2/(2m)+kq^2/2=E\Rightarrow\dfrac{q^2}{2E/k}+\dfrac{p^2}{2mE}=1\) — an ellipse. Axes: \(a=\sqrt{2E/k},b=\sqrt{2mE}\). \(J=\oint p\,dq=\pi ab=\pi\sqrt{2E/k}\cdot\sqrt{2mE}=2\pi E\sqrt{m/k}=2\pi E/\omega\) where \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\). Hence \(E=H=J\omega/(2\pi)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Binet's Equation

Derive Binet's equation for a particle moving under a central force \(f(r)\). Hence find the orbit for inverse-square force.

Solution

In polar coords with \(l=mr^2\dot\theta\) (angular momentum per unit mass \(h=r^2\dot\theta\)), let \(u=1/r\). Then \(\dot r=-h\dfrac{du}{d\theta},\ddot r=-hu^2\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}\cdot h\). Radial equation: \(\ddot r-r\dot\theta^2=f(r)/m\). Substituting: \(-h^2u^2\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}-\dfrac{h^2u^2}{1/u}u^2=-h^2u^2\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}... \) Tidying: \(\dfrac{d^2u}{d\theta^2}+u=-\dfrac{f(1/u)}{mh^2u^2}\) — Binet's equation. For \(f=-\mu m/r^2=-\mu mu^2\): RHS \(=\mu/(h^2)\). So \(u''+u=\mu/h^2\). Solution: \(u=\mu/h^2+A\cos\theta\Rightarrow r=\dfrac{h^2/\mu}{1+e\cos\theta}\) — a conic (ellipse for \(e<1\)). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Kepler's Third Law

Derive Kepler's third law: the square of the period of an elliptical orbit is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.

Solution

Ellipse: semi-major \(a\), semi-minor \(b\). Area swept per unit time \(=h/2\) (Kepler II). Period: \(T=\dfrac{\text{Area}}{h/2}=\dfrac{\pi ab}{h/2}=\dfrac{2\pi ab}{h}\). For ellipse: \(b^2=a^2(1-e^2)\), \(h^2=\mu a(1-e^2)\) (from orbit equation). So \(h=\sqrt{\mu a(1-e^2)}\), \(b=a\sqrt{1-e^2}\). \(T=\dfrac{2\pi a\cdot a\sqrt{1-e^2}}{\sqrt{\mu a(1-e^2)}}=\dfrac{2\pi a^2\sqrt{1-e^2}}{\sqrt{\mu a}\sqrt{1-e^2}}=\dfrac{2\pi a^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\mu}}\). Hence \(T^2=\dfrac{4\pi^2}{\mu}a^3\propto a^3\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Apsidal Distances

For a particle in a central force \(F=-k/r^2\), find the apsidal distances (closest and farthest points of orbit) in terms of energy \(E\) and angular momentum \(L\).

Solution

At apsidal points \(\dot r=0\). Energy: \(E=\dfrac{1}{2}m\dot r^2+\dfrac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\dfrac{mk}{r}=\dfrac{L^2}{2mr^2}-\dfrac{mk}{r}\). Multiply by \(2mr^2/m\): \(2Er^2+2kr\cdot m-L^2/m=0\Rightarrow 2Er^2+2mkr-L^2/m=0\). Wait — correctly: \(Er^2+mkr-\dfrac{L^2}{2m}=0\). Apsidal distances: \(r=\dfrac{-mk\pm\sqrt{m^2k^2+2EL^2/m}}{2E}\). For bound orbits (\(E<0\)): \(r_{\min},r_{\max}=\dfrac{mk\mp\sqrt{m^2k^2-2|E|L^2/m}}{2|E|}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Stability of Circular Orbits

For a central force \(F(r)=-k/r^n\), find the condition on \(n\) for stability of circular orbits.

Solution

Effective potential: \(V_{\text{eff}}=\dfrac{L^2}{2mr^2}+V(r)\) where \(F=-dV/dr\). For circular orbit at \(r_0\): \(dV_{\text{eff}}/dr=0\Rightarrow-L^2/(mr_0^3)+F(r_0)/m=0\) (with force being centripetal). Stability: \(d^2V_{\text{eff}}/dr^2>0\) at \(r_0\). PYQ 2021 — Principal Axes and Moment of Inertia

Find the principal moments of inertia and principal axes of a uniform rectangular lamina of sides \(a\) and \(b\), mass \(M\), about the centre.

Solution

Similarly \(I_{yy}=\dfrac{Ma^2}{12}\), \(I_{zz}=\dfrac{M(a^2+b^2)}{12}\) (perpendicular axis theorem). Products of inertia: \(I_{xy}=0\) (symmetry). So these axes are already principal axes. Principal moments: \(I_1=Mb^2/12,\; I_2=Ma^2/12,\; I_3=M(a^2+b^2)/12\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Kinetic Energy of Rigid Body

Show that the kinetic energy of a rigid body rotating about a fixed point is \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{L}\) where \(\mathbf{L}\) is angular momentum. Express in principal axes.

Solution

\(T=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_\alpha m_\alpha v_\alpha^2=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_\alpha m_\alpha(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{r}_\alpha)^2\). Using \(|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\times\mathbf{r}|^2=\Omega^2r^2-(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{r})^2\): \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_\alpha m_\alpha[\Omega^2r_\alpha^2-(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{r}_\alpha)^2]=\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{I}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\). In principal axes: \(T=\tfrac{1}{2}(I_1\Omega_1^2+I_2\Omega_2^2+I_3\Omega_3^2)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Heavy Symmetric Top: Precession

For a symmetric top spinning with angular velocity \(\Omega_s\) and spinning axis at angle \(\theta\) to vertical, find the precession rate \(\dot\phi\) (slow precession).

Solution

For the heavy symmetric top (one point fixed), in steady precession (fast spin, \(\dot\theta=0\)): torque about fixed point \(= Mgl\sin\theta\) (horizontal). Angular momentum (dominated by spin) \(= I_3\Omega_s\) along symmetry axis. Rate of change of \(\mathbf{L}\): \(|\dot{\mathbf{L}}|=L_\text{spin}\sin\theta\cdot\dot\phi=I_3\Omega_s\sin\theta\cdot\dot\phi\). PYQ 2014 — Euler Angles

Define the three Euler angles \((\phi,\theta,\psi)\). Express the angular velocity components along body-fixed principal axes in terms of Euler angles and their derivatives.

Solution

The three Euler angles describe orientation: \(\phi\) (precession — rotation about space \(z\)-axis), \(\theta\) (nutation — rotation about nodal line), \(\psi\) (spin — rotation about body \(z\)-axis). PYQ 2009 — Momental Ellipsoid

Define the momental ellipsoid (Poinsot ellipsoid). State Poinsot's theorem for torque-free rigid body motion.

Solution

The momental ellipsoid at a point \(O\) is the surface \(\mathbf{r}^T\mathbf{I}\mathbf{r}=1\) in body coordinates, i.e., \(I_1x^2+I_2y^2+I_3z^2=1\) (principal axes). For a system with kinetic matrix \(\mathbf{T}\) and potential matrix \(\mathbf{V}\), show that normal mode frequencies satisfy \(\det(\mathbf{V}-\omega^2\mathbf{T})=0\).

Derive \(E=mc^2\) from relativistic mechanics. Show that the total relativistic energy is \(E=\gamma m_0c^2\).

Two events are simultaneous in frame \(S\). Show they are generally not simultaneous in \(S'\). Under what condition is causal order preserved in all frames?

Events \(A(t,x_1)\) and \(B(t,x_2)\) are simultaneous in \(S\) (\(\Delta t=0\)). In \(S'\): \(\Delta t'=\gamma(\Delta t-v\Delta x/c^2)=-\gamma v\Delta x/c^2\neq0\) (unless \(\Delta x=0\)). PYQ 2024 — Kelvin's Circulation Theorem

State and prove Kelvin's circulation theorem: for an ideal fluid, the circulation \(\Gamma=\oint_C\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}\) around a material loop is conserved.

Solution

\(\dfrac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=\dfrac{D}{Dt}\oint_C\mathbf{v}\cdot d\mathbf{l}=\oint_C\dfrac{D\mathbf{v}}{Dt}\cdot d\mathbf{l}+\oint_C\mathbf{v}\cdot\dfrac{D(d\mathbf{l})}{Dt}\). State Helmholtz's vortex theorems for ideal fluid. Show that vortex lines move with the fluid.

Solution

Helmholtz's theorems: (1) The strength of a vortex tube (flux of vorticity \(\boldsymbol{\omega}=\nabla\times\mathbf{v}\) through any cross-section) is constant along the tube. (2) Vortex tubes move with the fluid (material lines); vortex lines cannot end in the fluid interior. (3) Strength of vortex tube is conserved in time. The vorticity equation: \(\dfrac{D\boldsymbol{\omega}}{Dt}=(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\) (for incompressible ideal fluid). This is the same equation as for material line elements, showing vortex lines are frozen into the fluid. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Vorticity Equation

Derive the vorticity equation \(\dfrac{D\boldsymbol{\omega}}{Dt}=(\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\) for incompressible inviscid flow.

PYQ 2023 — Navier-Stokes Equations

Write the Navier-Stokes equations for viscous incompressible flow. State the physical meaning of each term.

Solution

\[\rho\!\left(\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\right)=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2\mathbf{v}+\rho\mathbf{g}\] Terms: \(\rho\partial_t\mathbf{v}\) — inertial (unsteady); \(\rho(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\) — convective inertia (nonlinear); \(-\nabla p\) — pressure gradient; \(\mu\nabla^2\mathbf{v}\) — viscous diffusion (\(\mu\) = dynamic viscosity); \(\rho\mathbf{g}\) — body force. PYQ 2021 — Poiseuille's Formula (Pipe Flow)

Derive Poiseuille's formula for laminar viscous flow through a circular pipe of radius \(R\) and length \(L\) under pressure difference \(\Delta p\).

Solution

Fully developed laminar flow: \(v=v(r)\hat{z}\). Navier-Stokes reduces to: \(\dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{d}{dr}\!\left(r\dfrac{dv}{dr}\right)=-\dfrac{\Delta p}{\mu L}\equiv-\dfrac{P}{\mu}\). PYQ 2019 — Stokes' Law

State Stokes' law for drag on a sphere at low Reynolds number. Derive the terminal velocity of a sphere falling in a viscous fluid.

Stokes' law: For a sphere of radius \(a\) moving slowly (\(Re\ll1\)) in fluid of viscosity \(\mu\) with velocity \(U\): drag force \(F_D=6\pi\mu a U\). Terminal velocity: At terminal velocity, drag + buoyancy = weight. \(6\pi\mu a v_t+\tfrac{4}{3}\pi a^3\rho_f g=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi a^3\rho_s g\). \[v_t=\dfrac{2a^2(\rho_s-\rho_f)g}{9\mu}\]. Derivation of Stokes' law: solve Stokes equations (\(\nabla p=\mu\nabla^2\mathbf{v},\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\)) around sphere → stream function solution → integrate surface stress → \(F=6\pi\mu aU\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Reynolds Number

Define Reynolds number. What is its physical significance? State the critical value separating laminar from turbulent flow in a pipe.

Solution

\(Re=\dfrac{\rho v L}{\mu}=\dfrac{vL}{\nu}\) where \(\nu=\mu/\rho\) is kinematic viscosity, \(L\) a characteristic length scale, \(v\) a characteristic velocity. Physical significance: \(Re=\dfrac{\text{inertial forces}}{\text{viscous forces}}\). Small \(Re\): viscous dominates → laminar, smooth, predictable. Large \(Re\): inertia dominates → turbulence, chaotic mixing. For pipe flow: critical \(Re\approx2300\). Below 2300: laminar (Poiseuille). Above 4000: turbulent. 2300–4000: transitional. \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2015 — Couette Flow

Viscous fluid flows between two parallel plates: lower plate fixed, upper plate moving at speed \(U\). Find the velocity profile (Couette flow).

Solution

Let \(y\in[0,h]\), flow in \(x\)-direction, no pressure gradient. N-S: \(\mu d^2v/dy^2=0\Rightarrow v=Ay+B\). BCs: \(v(0)=0\Rightarrow B=0\); \(v(h)=U\Rightarrow A=U/h\). \[v(y)=\dfrac{Uy}{h}\] — linear profile. Shear stress: \(\tau=\mu dv/dy=\mu U/h\) (constant throughout). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2013 — Euler's Equation of Motion for Ideal Fluid

Derive Euler's equation of motion for an ideal (inviscid) fluid from Newton's second law applied to a fluid element.

Solution

Consider a fluid element of volume \(\delta V\) at position \(\mathbf{r}\). Mass: \(\delta m=\rho\delta V\). Forces: pressure \(-\nabla p\cdot\delta V\), gravity \(\rho\mathbf{g}\delta V\) (no viscosity for ideal fluid). Newton's second law: \(\rho\delta V\dfrac{D\mathbf{v}}{Dt}=(-\nabla p+\rho\mathbf{g})\delta V\). Dividing by \(\delta V\): \[\rho\dfrac{D\mathbf{v}}{Dt}=\rho\!\left(\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}\right)=-\nabla p+\rho\mathbf{g}\]. Using \(\mathbf{g}=-\nabla(gz)\): \(\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{v}=-\dfrac{\nabla p}{\rho}-\nabla(gz)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2011 — Equation of Continuity: Derivation

Derive the equation of continuity for a compressible fluid. Reduce to the incompressible case.

Solution

Rate of mass increase in control volume \(V\): \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_V\rho\,dV=-\oint_S\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{n}\,dA\) (mass flux out). By divergence theorem: \(\int_V\!\left(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v})\right)dV=0\). Since this holds for any \(V\): \[\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v})=0\]. Expanding: \(\dfrac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\rho+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{D\rho}{Dt}+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\). For incompressible (\(D\rho/Dt=0\)): \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

Explain the Magnus effect using fluid dynamics. A cylinder of radius \(a\) rotates with angular velocity \(\omega\) in a stream of speed \(U\). Find the lift.

The rotating cylinder drags fluid, creating circulation \(\Gamma=2\pi a^2\omega\). Complex potential: \(w=U(z+a^2/z)-\dfrac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z\). PYQ 2012 — D'Alembert's Paradox

Solution

D'Alembert's paradox: A body moving through an ideal (inviscid, incompressible) fluid at steady state experiences zero drag force. Proof (for cylinder): The pressure distribution \(p=p_\infty+\tfrac{1}{2}\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\) is symmetric fore-and-aft (\(\theta\to\pi-\theta\) symmetry).
