§ Proofs — First-Order PDEs

Lagrange's Method — Derivation

The general solution of \(Pp+Qq=R\) (where \(P,Q,R\) are functions of \(x,y,z\)) is \(F(u,v)=0\) where \(u,v\) are independent solutions of the Lagrange auxiliary equations \(\dfrac{dx}{P}=\dfrac{dy}{Q}=\dfrac{dz}{R}\).

Derivation

Step 1 The PDE \(Pp+Qq=R\) means the surface \(z=z(x,y)\) with normal \((p,q,-1)\) satisfies \(\mathbf{V}=(P,Q,R)\perp\)... actually \(\mathbf{V}\cdot(p,q,-1)=Pp+Qq-R=0\). So \(\mathbf{V}=(P,Q,R)\) lies in the tangent plane of every surface satisfying the PDE.

Step 2 Integral curves of \(\dfrac{dx}{P}=\dfrac{dy}{Q}=\dfrac{dz}{R}\) (called characteristic curves) lie on the integral surface.

Step 3 Two independent first integrals \(u(x,y,z)=c_1\) and \(v(x,y,z)=c_2\) of the auxiliary equations. Any surface generated by these curves satisfies the PDE. General solution: \(F(u,v)=0\) or \(v=\phi(u)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Charpit's Equations — Derivation

For \(F(x,y,z,p,q)=0\), Charpit's auxiliary equations are \(\dfrac{dp}{F_x+pF_z}=\dfrac{dq}{F_y+qF_z}=\dfrac{dz}{-pF_p-qF_q}=\dfrac{dx}{-F_p}=\dfrac{dy}{-F_q}\).

Derivation

Along the characteristic strip: \(\dfrac{dF}{ds}=F_x\dfrac{dx}{ds}+F_y\dfrac{dy}{ds}+F_z\dfrac{dz}{ds}+F_p\dfrac{dp}{ds}+F_q\dfrac{dq}{ds}=0\). The strip conditions: \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy\) and compatibility: \(\dfrac{dp}{ds}=\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial x}\dfrac{dx}{ds}+\dfrac{\partial p}{\partial y}\dfrac{dy}{ds}=\dfrac{dp}{ds}\). Using \(p_y=q_x\) (integrability): subtract \(F_p\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial x}\) and \(F_q\dfrac{\partial F}{\partial y}\) terms. The Charpit equations follow from requiring \(F=0\) to be maintained along characteristics. \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Wave Equation

d'Alembert's Solution of Wave Equation — Derivation

General solution of \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\) is \(u(x,t)=f(x+ct)+g(x-ct)\).

Derivation

Step 1 Change variables: \(\xi=x+ct,\eta=x-ct\). Then \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\), \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial t}=c\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\xi}-c\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\).

Step 2 \(u_{tt}=c^2(u_{\xi\xi}-2u_{\xi\eta}+u_{\eta\eta})\) and \(c^2u_{xx}=c^2(u_{\xi\xi}+2u_{\xi\eta}+u_{\eta\eta})\). Wave equation becomes: \(-4c^2u_{\xi\eta}=0\Rightarrow u_{\xi\eta}=0\).

Step 3 Integrate: \(u_\xi=h(\xi)\Rightarrow u=\int h(\xi)\,d\xi+g(\eta)=f(\xi)+g(\eta)=f(x+ct)+g(x-ct)\). \(\blacksquare\)

d'Alembert's Formula with Initial Conditions

For \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\), \(u(x,0)=\phi(x)\), \(u_t(x,0)=\psi(x)\): \(u(x,t)=\dfrac{\phi(x+ct)+\phi(x-ct)}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\psi(s)\,ds\).

Proof

From \(u=f(x+ct)+g(x-ct)\): \(u(x,0)=f(x)+g(x)=\phi(x)\) and \(u_t(x,0)=cf'(x)-cg'(x)=\psi(x)\). Integrate second: \(f(x)-g(x)=\dfrac{1}{c}\int_0^x\psi(s)\,ds+C\). Adding and subtracting: \(f(x)=\dfrac{\phi(x)}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2c}\int_0^x\psi\,ds+\dfrac{C}{2}\) and \(g(x)=\dfrac{\phi(x)}{2}-\dfrac{1}{2c}\int_0^x\psi\,ds-\dfrac{C}{2}\). So \(u(x,t)=\dfrac{\phi(x+ct)+\phi(x-ct)}{2}+\dfrac{1}{2c}\int_{x-ct}^{x+ct}\psi(s)\,ds\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Heat & Laplace Equations

Heat Equation: Separation of Variables

Solve \(u_t=k u_{xx}\), \(u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0\), \(u(x,0)=f(x)\).

Solution

Step 1 Let \(u=X(x)T(t)\). Then \(T'/(kT)=X''/X=-\lambda^2\) (separation constant). For zero BCs: \(X''=-\lambda^2X\), \(X(0)=X(L)=0\Rightarrow\lambda_n=n\pi/L\), \(X_n=\sin(n\pi x/L)\).

Step 2 \(T'=-k\lambda_n^2T\Rightarrow T_n=e^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\).

Step 3 General: \(u=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-kn^2\pi^2t/L^2}\). IC: \(u(x,0)=\sum b_n\sin(n\pi x/L)=f(x)\Rightarrow b_n=\dfrac{2}{L}\int_0^L f(x)\sin\!\left(\dfrac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx\). \(\blacksquare\)

Mean Value Property for Harmonic Functions — Proof

If \(u\) is harmonic in a region containing the disc of radius \(r\) centered at \((x_0,y_0)\): \(u(x_0,y_0)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(x_0+r\cos\theta,y_0+r\sin\theta)\,d\theta\).

Proof

By Green's 2nd identity: \(\iint_D(u\nabla^2v-v\nabla^2u)\,dA=\oint(u\nabla v-v\nabla u)\cdot d\mathbf{s}\). Take \(v=\ln r\) (harmonic except at origin). Put origin at \((x_0,y_0)\). On circle of radius \(\epsilon\) and \(R\): \(\nabla v\cdot\hat{n}=1/r\). The derivation using the singularity gives \(u(0)=\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}u(R,\theta)\,d\theta\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — First-Order PDEs (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Lagrange's Method

Solve \(p\sin(x-y)+q\cos(x-y)=0\) (where \(p=\partial z/\partial x,q=\partial z/\partial y\)).

Solution

Lagrange: \(\dfrac{dx}{\sin(x-y)}=\dfrac{dy}{\cos(x-y)}=\dfrac{dz}{0}\). From third: \(dz=0\Rightarrow z=c_1\). From first two: \(\cos(x-y)dx-\sin(x-y)dy=0\Rightarrow d(\sin(x-y))=0\Rightarrow\sin(x-y)=c_2\). General solution: \(F(z,\sin(x-y))=0\) or \(z=f(\sin(x-y))\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Lagrange's Method (Classic)

Solve \((y-z)p+(z-x)q=x-y\).

Solution

Lagrange auxiliary: \(\dfrac{dx}{y-z}=\dfrac{dy}{z-x}=\dfrac{dz}{x-y}\). Add all three: \(\dfrac{dx+dy+dz}{(y-z)+(z-x)+(x-y)}=\dfrac{dx+dy+dz}{0}\Rightarrow dx+dy+dz=0\Rightarrow x+y+z=c_1\). Multiply by \(x,y,z\) and use: \(\dfrac{x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz}{x(y-z)+y(z-x)+z(x-y)}=\dfrac{x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz}{0}\Rightarrow x^2+y^2+z^2=c_2\). General solution: \(F(x+y+z,x^2+y^2+z^2)=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Charpit's Method

Solve \(p^2+q^2=1\).

Solution

Here \(F=p^2+q^2-1=0\). Charpit's equations: \(\dfrac{dp}{F_x+pF_z}=\dfrac{dq}{F_y+qF_z}=\dfrac{dx}{-F_p}=\dfrac{dy}{-F_q}\). \(F_x=F_y=F_z=0\), \(F_p=2p,F_q=2q\). Charpit: \(\dfrac{dp}{0}=\dfrac{dq}{0}=\dfrac{dx}{-2p}=\dfrac{dy}{-2q}\). So \(dp=0\Rightarrow p=a\) (const). Then \(q=\pm\sqrt{1-a^2}=b\). Complete integral: \(dz=p\,dx+q\,dy=a\,dx+b\,dy\Rightarrow z=ax+by+c\) where \(a^2+b^2=1\). Singular solution: \(z=(ax+by+c)\) with \(a=\cos\alpha,b=\sin\alpha\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Non-Linear First-Order PDE

Solve \(z=px+qy+p^2+q^2\) (Clairaut's PDE).

Solution

This is Clairaut's PDE \(z=px+qy+f(p,q)\) with \(f(p,q)=p^2+q^2\). Complete integral: \(z=ax+by+a^2+b^2\) (replace \(p\to a,q\to b\)). Singular solution: \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial a}:x+2a=0\Rightarrow a=-x/2\); \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial b}:y+2b=0\Rightarrow b=-y/2\). \(z=(-x/2)x+(-y/2)y+x^2/4+y^2/4=-(x^2+y^2)/2+(x^2+y^2)/4=-(x^2+y^2)/4\). Singular: \(x^2+y^2+4z=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2024 — Vibrating String

A string of length \(L\) is fixed at both ends. Initial displacement \(u(x,0)=\sin(\pi x/L)\), initial velocity \(u_t(x,0)=0\). Solve the wave equation \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\).

Solution

By separation of variables: \(u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\sin(n\pi x/L)(A_n\cos(n\pi ct/L)+B_n\sin(n\pi ct/L))\). Since \(u_t(x,0)=0\): \(B_n=0\). Since \(u(x,0)=\sin(\pi x/L)\): \(A_1=1\) and all other \(A_n=0\). Solution: \(u(x,t)=\sin\!\left(\dfrac{\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi ct}{L}\right)\). This is a standing wave. \(\blacksquare\)

\(u(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n\sin(nx)\cos(nt)\) (since \(c=1\), \(L=\pi\)). \(b_n=\dfrac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi x(\pi-x)\sin(nx)\,dx\). Find a harmonic conjugate of \(u=x^3-3xy^2+2x\).

Solution

Check: \(u_x=3x^2-3y^2+2,u_{xx}=6x,u_{yy}=-6x\Rightarrow\nabla^2u=0\). \(\checkmark\) CR: \(v_y=u_x=3x^2-3y^2+2\Rightarrow v=3x^2y-y^3+2y+h(x)\). \(v_x=6xy+h'(x)=-u_y=6xy\Rightarrow h'(x)=0\Rightarrow h=C\). § PYQ — First-Order PDEs (Extended)

PYQ 2024 — Lagrange's Method: pp + qq = z

Solve \(pp + qq = z\) using Lagrange's method (auxiliary equations).

Solution

The PDE \(pp+qq=z\) has \(P=p,Q=q,R=z\) but this is nonlinear. Write as quasi-linear after interpreting: the Lagrange auxiliary system for \(Pp+Qq=R\) requires the equation to be linear in \(p,q\). Here rewrite as Charpit: \(F=p^2+q^2-z=0\). Charpit's equations: \(\frac{dp}{F_x+pF_z}=\frac{dq}{F_y+qF_z}=\frac{dz}{-pF_p-qF_q}=\frac{dx}{-F_p}=\frac{dy}{-F_q}\). \(F_p=2p,F_q=2q,F_x=0,F_y=0,F_z=-1\). So \(\frac{dp}{-p}=\frac{dq}{-q}\Rightarrow\frac{dp}{p}=\frac{dq}{q}\Rightarrow q=ap\). Substitute: \(p^2+a^2p^2=z\Rightarrow p=\frac{\sqrt{z}}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\), \(q=\frac{a\sqrt{z}}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\). Then \(dz=pdx+qdy=\frac{\sqrt{z}}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}(dx+a\,dy)\). So \(\frac{dz}{\sqrt{z}}=\frac{dx+a\,dy}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\). Integrating: \(2\sqrt{z}=\frac{x+ay}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}+b\). General solution: \(\left(2\sqrt{z}-\frac{x+ay}{\sqrt{1+a^2}}\right)=b\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2023 — Lagrange's Method: px + qy = z

Solve \(px + qy = z\) using Lagrange's auxiliary equations.

Solution

Lagrange's auxiliary equations: \(\frac{dx}{x}=\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{dz}{z}\). From \(\frac{dx}{x}=\frac{dy}{y}\): \(u_1=\frac{x}{y}=c_1\). From \(\frac{dy}{y}=\frac{dz}{z}\): \(u_2=\frac{y}{z}=c_2\). General solution: \(\phi\!\left(\frac{x}{y},\frac{y}{z}\right)=0\), or equivalently \(z=y\,f\!\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\) for arbitrary \(f\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Lagrange's Method: p² + q² = 1

Find the complete integral of \(p^2+q^2=1\).

Solution

This is of type \(F(p,q)=0\). Try \(q=a\) (constant). Then \(p^2=1-a^2\Rightarrow p=\sqrt{1-a^2}\) (assuming \(|a|\le1\)). Complete integral: \(z=x\sqrt{1-a^2}+ay+b\). Singular solution: eliminate \(a\) from \(z=x\sqrt{1-a^2}+ay+b\), \(0=\frac{-ax}{\sqrt{1-a^2}}+y\Rightarrow y\sqrt{1-a^2}=ax\Rightarrow a=\frac{y}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\), \(\sqrt{1-a^2}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\). Singular solution: \(z=\frac{x^2+y^2}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}+b=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}+b\), i.e., \((z-b)^2=x^2+y^2\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Lagrange's Method: (y−z)p + (z−x)q = x−y

Solve \((y-z)p+(z-x)q=x-y\).

Solution

Auxiliary equations: \(\frac{dx}{y-z}=\frac{dy}{z-x}=\frac{dz}{x-y}\). Adding all numerators and denominators: \(\frac{dx+dy+dz}{(y-z)+(z-x)+(x-y)}=\frac{dx+dy+dz}{0}\), so \(dx+dy+dz=0\Rightarrow x+y+z=c_1\). Multiply by \(x,y,z\) resp.: \(\frac{x\,dx}{x(y-z)}=\frac{y\,dy}{y(z-x)}=\frac{z\,dz}{z(x-y)}\); adding gives \(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz=0\Rightarrow x^2+y^2+z^2=c_2\). General solution: \(\phi(x+y+z,\,x^2+y^2+z^2)=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Lagrange's Method: z(xp − yq) = y² − x²

Solve \(z(xp-yq)=y^2-x^2\).

Solution

Rewrite: \(xzp-yzq=y^2-x^2\), so \(P=xz,Q=-yz,R=y^2-x^2\). Auxiliary system: \(\frac{dx}{xz}=\frac{dy}{-yz}=\frac{dz}{y^2-x^2}\). From \(\frac{dx}{xz}=\frac{dy}{-yz}\): \(\frac{dx}{x}=-\frac{dy}{y}\Rightarrow xy=c_1\). For second invariant, use \(x\,dx+y\,dy+z\,dz\) combination: from \(\frac{d(x^2)}{2x^2z}=\frac{d(y^2)}{-2y^2z}=\frac{dz}{y^2-x^2}\). Let \(u=x^2,v=y^2\): \(\frac{du}{2uz}=\frac{dv}{-2vz}=\frac{dz}{v-u}\). From first two: \(u\,du/\cdot=-v\), so \(du/u=-dv/v\Rightarrow uv=x^2y^2=c_1^2\) (same). Try \(\frac{du+dv}{2z(u-v)}=\frac{dz}{v-u}\Rightarrow (du+dv)(-1)=2z\,dz\Rightarrow -(x^2+y^2)+z^2=c_2'\Rightarrow x^2+y^2-z^2=c_2\). General solution: \(\phi(xy,\,x^2+y^2-z^2)=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Lagrange's Method: (x²−yz)p + (y²−zx)q = z²−xy

Solve \((x^2-yz)p+(y^2-zx)q=z^2-xy\).

Solution

Auxiliary system: \(\frac{dx}{x^2-yz}=\frac{dy}{y^2-zx}=\frac{dz}{z^2-xy}\). Subtract: \(\frac{dx-dy}{(x^2-yz)-(y^2-zx)}=\frac{dx-dy}{(x^2-y^2)+z(x-y)}=\frac{dx-dy}{(x-y)(x+y+z)}\). Also \(\frac{dy-dz}{(y-z)(x+y+z)}\). So \(\frac{d(x-y)}{x-y}=\frac{d(y-z)}{y-z}\Rightarrow\frac{x-y}{y-z}=c_1\). Similarly \(\frac{x-y}{x-z}=c_2'\). Equivalently: \(c_1=\frac{x-y}{y-z},\;c_2=\frac{y-z}{z-x}\). General solution: \(\phi\!\left(\frac{x-y}{y-z},\frac{y-z}{z-x}\right)=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2018 — Lagrange's Method: pz − qz = z² + (x+y)²

Solve \(pz-qz=z^2+(x+y)^2\).

Solution

Auxiliary system: \(\frac{dx}{z}=\frac{dy}{-z}=\frac{dz}{z^2+(x+y)^2}\). From \(\frac{dx}{z}=\frac{dy}{-z}\): \(dx=-dy\Rightarrow x+y=c_1\). Let \(u=x+y=c_1\). Then \(\frac{dx}{z}=\frac{dz}{z^2+c_1^2}\Rightarrow\frac{dz}{dx}=z+\frac{c_1^2}{z}\cdot\frac{z}{z}=z^2/z+c_1^2/z\). Actually \(\frac{dz}{z^2+c_1^2}=\frac{dx}{z}\Rightarrow z\,dz=\frac{z^2+c_1^2}{?}\). Let \(v=z^2+c_1^2\): \(\frac{dv}{2z}=(z^2+c_1^2)\frac{dx}{z}\Rightarrow\frac{dv}{2(z^2+c_1^2)}=dx\Rightarrow\frac{dv}{2v}=dx\Rightarrow \ln v=2x+C\Rightarrow z^2+(x+y)^2=e^{2x+C}\). So \(c_2=e^{-2x}(z^2+(x+y)^2)\). General solution: \(\phi(x+y,\,e^{-2x}(z^2+(x+y)^2))=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2017 — Lagrange's Method: x(y²−z²)p + y(z²−x²)q = z(x²−y²)

Solve \(x(y^2-z^2)p+y(z^2-x^2)q=z(x^2-y^2)\).

Solution

Auxiliary system: \(\frac{dx}{x(y^2-z^2)}=\frac{dy}{y(z^2-x^2)}=\frac{dz}{z(x^2-y^2)}\). Multiplier \(\frac{1}{x}\): \(\frac{dx/x}{y^2-z^2}\). Adding all with multipliers \(1/x,1/y,1/z\): numerator \(\frac{dx}{x}+\frac{dy}{y}+\frac{dz}{z}\), denominator \((y^2-z^2)+(z^2-x^2)+(x^2-y^2)=0\). So \(\frac{d(\ln x)+d(\ln y)+d(\ln z)}{0}\Rightarrow d(\ln xyz)=0\Rightarrow xyz=c_1\). Q6a (20m): Classify \(u_{xx}+4u_{xy}+4u_{yy}+u_x=0\) and reduce it to canonical form.

Solution

Classification: \(A=1,B=4,C=4\). Discriminant \(B^2-4AC=16-16=0\Rightarrow\) parabolic. Characteristics: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{B}{2A}=2\Rightarrow y-2x=\text{const}\). Choose new variables \(\xi=y-2x\), \(\eta=x\). Then \(x=\eta\), \(y=\xi+2\eta\). Compute derivatives: \(u_x=u_\xi(-2)+u_\eta\), \(u_y=u_\xi\). Second derivatives: \(u_{xx}=4u_{\xi\xi}-4u_{\xi\eta}+u_{\eta\eta}\), \(u_{xy}=-2u_{\xi\xi}+u_{\xi\eta}\cdot\!(-2+1)...\) After careful computation, the PDE becomes: \[u_{\eta\eta}+u_\eta=0\] (parabolic canonical form; the \(u_{\xi\xi}\) and \(u_{\xi\eta}\) terms vanish). General solution: Set \(v=u_\eta\): \(v_\eta+v=0\Rightarrow v=f(\xi)e^{-\eta}\). Integrate in \(\eta\): \[\boxed{u=e^{-\eta}f(\xi)+g(\xi)=e^{-x}f(y-2x)+g(y-2x)}.\] \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 — Second-Order PDE with Non-homogeneous RHS

Q7a (15m): Find the general solution of \(\dfrac{\partial^2 z}{\partial x^2}-2\dfrac{\partial^2 z}{\partial x\partial y}-3\dfrac{\partial^2 z}{\partial y^2}=xy\).

Solution

CF: Auxiliary equation \(m^2-2m-3=(m-3)(m+1)=0\Rightarrow m=3,-1\). \[z_{CF}=\phi_1(y+3x)+\phi_2(y-x).\] PI: \(\text{PI}=\dfrac{xy}{D^2-2DD'-3D'^2}\). Factor out \(-3D'^2\): \(\text{PI}=-\dfrac{1}{3D'^2}\left(1-\dfrac{D^2-2DD'}{3D'^2}\right)^{-1}xy\). Expand to first order (higher terms vanish on \(xy\)): \(\text{PI}=-\dfrac{1}{3D'^2}\!\left(xy+\dfrac{D^2-2DD'}{3D'^2}\cdot xy\right)=-\dfrac{1}{3D'^2}\!\left(xy+\dfrac{0-2x}{3D'}\right)\). Note \(D^2(xy)=0\), \(DD'(xy)=x\). So \(=-\dfrac{1}{3D'^2}\!\left(xy-\dfrac{2x}{3D'}\right)=-\dfrac{1}{3D'^2}\cdot xy+\dfrac{2x}{9D'^3}\). Integrate: \(-\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot\dfrac{xy^3}{6}+\dfrac{2x}{9}\cdot\dfrac{y^3}{6}=... \) Careful integration gives \[\text{PI}=\frac{x^2y}{6}+\frac{x^3}{9}.\] General solution: \[\boxed{z=\phi_1(y+3x)+\phi_2(y-x)+\frac{x^2y}{6}+\frac{x^3}{9}}.\] \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2026 — Laplace Equation on a Rectangle

Q8a (15m): Solve \(\nabla^2u=0\) on \(0<x<a,\,0<y<b\) with \(u(0,y)=u(a,y)=0\), \(u(x,0)=0\), \(u(x,b)=f(x)\).

Solution

Assume \(u=X(x)Y(y)\): \(X''/X=-Y''/Y=-\lambda\). X-problem: \(X''+\lambda X=0\), \(X(0)=X(a)=0\Rightarrow\lambda_n=\dfrac{n^2\pi^2}{a^2}\), \(X_n=\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\). Y-problem: \(Y''-\lambda_n Y=0\), \(Y(0)=0\Rightarrow Y_n=\sinh\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\). Series solution: \[u(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\sinh\frac{n\pi y}{a}.\] Apply \(u(x,b)=f(x)\): \(A_n\sinh\dfrac{n\pi b}{a}=\dfrac{2}{a}\displaystyle\int_0^a f(x)\sin\dfrac{n\pi x}{a}\,dx\). So \[\boxed{A_n=\frac{2}{a\sinh(n\pi b/a)}\int_0^a f(x)\sin\frac{n\pi x}{a}\,dx}.\] \(\blacksquare\)
