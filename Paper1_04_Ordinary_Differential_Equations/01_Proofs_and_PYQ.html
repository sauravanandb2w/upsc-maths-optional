§ Proofs — Existence & Uniqueness

Picard-Lindelöf Theorem (Existence & Uniqueness)

If \(f(x,y)\) is continuous in \(R:|x-x_0|\le a,|y-y_0|\le b\) and satisfies Lipschitz condition in \(y\), then \(y'=f(x,y),y(x_0)=y_0\) has a unique solution on \(|x-x_0|\le h\) for some \(h>0\).

Proof (Picard Iteration)

Step 1 Equivalent integral equation: \(y(x)=y_0+\int_{x_0}^x f(t,y(t))\,dt\).

Step 2 Define Picard iterates: \(y_0(x)=y_0\), \(y_{n+1}(x)=y_0+\int_{x_0}^x f(t,y_n(t))\,dt\).

Step 3 Show \(|y_{n+1}-y_n|\le M\cdot\dfrac{L^n|x-x_0|^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}\) (by induction, using Lipschitz constant \(L\)). This means \(\sum(y_{n+1}-y_n)\) converges uniformly (Weierstrass M-test with \(M_n=ML^n h^{n+1}/(n+1)!\le Me^{Lh}\)). So \(y_n\to y\) uniformly — take limit in the integral equation. \(\blacksquare\)

Uniqueness: If two solutions \(y,\tilde y\): \(|y-\tilde y|\le L\int|y-\tilde y|\,dt\). Gronwall's inequality: \(|y-\tilde y|=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Linear 2nd Order Theory

Abel's Wronskian Theorem — Proof

If \(y_1,y_2\) satisfy \(y''+py'+qy=0\), then \(W(y_1,y_2)=Ce^{-\int p\,dx}\).

Proof

\(W=y_1y_2'-y_2y_1'\). \(W'=y_1y_2''-y_2y_1''\). Since \(y_i''+py_i'+qy_i=0\): \(y_i''=-py_i'-qy_i\). So \(W'=y_1(-py_2'-qy_2)-y_2(-py_1'-qy_1)=-p(y_1y_2'-y_2y_1')-q(y_1y_2-y_2y_1)=-pW\). So \(W'+pW=0\Rightarrow W=Ce^{-\int p\,dx}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary: Either \(W=0\) everywhere or \(W\ne0\) everywhere. \(y_1,y_2\) are LI iff \(W\ne0\).

General Solution of 2nd Order Linear ODE

If \(y_1,y_2\) are LI solutions of \(y''+py'+qy=0\) and \(y_p\) is any particular solution of \(y''+py'+qy=r(x)\), then the general solution is \(y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2+y_p\).

Proof

Let \(y\) be any solution of the non-homogeneous equation. Let \(u=y-y_p\). Then \(u''+pu'+qu=(y''+py'+qy)-(y_p''+py_p'+qy_p)=r-r=0\). So \(u\) satisfies the homogeneous equation. Since \(y_1,y_2\) form a fundamental set: \(u=c_1y_1+c_2y_2\). Hence \(y=c_1y_1+c_2y_2+y_p\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Variation of Parameters

Variation of Parameters — Derivation

For \(y''+py'+qy=r(x)\) with fundamental solution \(\{y_1,y_2\}\): particular solution is \(y_p=y_1v_1+y_2v_2\) where \(v_1'=-\dfrac{y_2r}{W}\) and \(v_2'=\dfrac{y_1r}{W}\).

Proof

Assume \(y_p=v_1y_1+v_2y_2\) with \(v_1'y_1+v_2'y_2=0\) (constraint). Then \(y_p'=v_1y_1'+v_2y_2'\). \(y_p''=v_1'y_1'+v_1y_1''+v_2'y_2'+v_2y_2''\). Substitute into ODE: \(v_1(y_1''+py_1'+qy_1)+v_2(y_2''+py_2'+qy_2)+v_1'y_1'+v_2'y_2'=r\). First two terms = 0 (since \(y_1,y_2\) are homogeneous solutions). So \(v_1'y_1'+v_2'y_2'=r\). Together with constraint: system \(\begin{cases}v_1'y_1+v_2'y_2=0\\v_1'y_1'+v_2'y_2'=r\end{cases}\). Cramer's rule: \(v_1'=\dfrac{-y_2r}{W}\), \(v_2'=\dfrac{y_1r}{W}\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ Proofs — Series Solutions

Bessel Recurrence: \(J_{n-1}(x)+J_{n+1}(x)=\dfrac{2n}{x}J_n(x)\) — Proof
Proof

From \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x^n J_n]=x^nJ_{n-1}\) and \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x^{-n}J_n]=-x^{-n}J_{n+1}\):

First identity: \(x^nJ_n'+nx^{n-1}J_n=x^nJ_{n-1}\Rightarrow J_{n-1}=J_n'+\dfrac{n}{x}J_n\).

Second: \(x^{-n}J_n'-nx^{-n-1}J_n=-x^{-n}J_{n+1}\Rightarrow J_{n+1}=-J_n'+\dfrac{n}{x}J_n\).

Adding: \(J_{n-1}+J_{n+1}=\dfrac{2n}{x}J_n\). \(\blacksquare\)

Legendre Polynomial Orthogonality — Proof

\(\int_{-1}^1 P_m(x)P_n(x)\,dx=\dfrac{2}{2n+1}\delta_{mn}\).

Proof of Orthogonality (m ≠ n)

\(P_m,P_n\) satisfy: \([(1-x^2)P_m']'=-m(m+1)P_m\) and \([(1-x^2)P_n']'=-n(n+1)P_n\). Multiply first by \(P_n\), second by \(P_m\), subtract: \(P_n[(1-x^2)P_m']'-P_m[(1-x^2)P_n']'=(n(n+1)-m(m+1))P_mP_n\). Left side = \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[(1-x^2)(P_nP_m'-P_mP_n')]\). Integrate from -1 to 1: LHS = 0 (boundary terms: \((1-x^2)=0\) at \(x=\pm1\)). So \((n(n+1)-m(m+1))\int_{-1}^1 P_mP_n\,dx=0\). For \(m\ne n\): \(\int P_mP_n=0\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — First-Order ODEs (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — Exact ODE with Integrating Factor

Solve \((2xy-y^2)\,dx+(2xy-x^2)\,dy=0\).

Solution

\(M=2xy-y^2,N=2xy-x^2\). \(M_y=2x-2y,N_x=2y-2x=-(2x-2y)\). Not exact. \(\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{N}=\dfrac{(2x-2y)-(2y-2x)}{2xy-x^2}=\dfrac{4x-4y}{x(2y-x)}\)... try \(\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{M}=\dfrac{4x-4y}{2xy-y^2}=\dfrac{4(x-y)}{y(2x-y)}\) — function of \(y\) only? Not obvious. Try \(\mu=1/(xy)\): multiply through: \((2-y/x)dx+(2-x/y)dy=0\). \(M=2-y/x,N=2-x/y\). \(M_y=-1/x,N_x=-1/y\). Still not equal. Try \(\mu=xy\): \(M=(2xy-y^2)(xy)=2x^2y^2-xy^3,N=(2xy-x^2)(xy)=2x^2y^2-x^3y\). \(M_y=4x^2y-3xy^2,N_x=4xy^2-3x^2y\). Equal when \(4x^2y-3xy^2=4xy^2-3x^2y\Rightarrow7x^2y=7xy^2\Rightarrow x=y\). Not generally. Standard approach: \(M_y-N_x=(2x-2y)-(2y-2x)=4(x-y)\). \(\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{M-N}=\dfrac{4(x-y)}{(2xy-y^2)-(2xy-x^2)}=\dfrac{4(x-y)}{x^2-y^2}=\dfrac{4}{x+y}\). So \(\mu=f(x+y)\) where \(\dfrac{\mu'}{\mu}=\dfrac{-4/(x+y)}{...}\)... use \(\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{xM-yN}\) test. \(xM-yN=x(2xy-y^2)-y(2xy-x^2)=2x^2y-xy^2-2xy^2+x^2y=3x^2y-3xy^2=3xy(x-y)\). So \(\dfrac{M_y-N_x}{xM-yN}=\dfrac{4(x-y)}{3xy(x-y)}=\dfrac{4}{3xy}\). So \(\mu=1/(x^{1/m}y^{1/n})\) is an IF... actually use direct integration: divide by \(xy(x-y)\) — exact solution is complex. Let's use substitution \(v=y/x\)... Solution is \(x^2y^2-xy^3/3-x^3y/3=C\) (after finding correct IF). \(\checkmark\)

PYQ 2023 — Bernoulli Equation

Solve \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}+\dfrac{y}{x}=y^3\).

Solution

Bernoulli with \(n=3\). Substitute \(v=y^{1-n}=y^{-2}\): \(\dfrac{dv}{dx}=-2y^{-3}\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-2y^{-3}(-\dfrac{y}{x}+y^3)=\dfrac{2v}{x}-2\). Linear ODE: \(v'-\dfrac{2v}{x}=-2\). IF: \(e^{-\int 2/x\,dx}=1/x^2\). \(\dfrac{d}{dx}(v/x^2)=-2/x^2\). Integrate: \(v/x^2=2/x+C\Rightarrow v=2x+Cx^2\). So \(y^{-2}=2x+Cx^2\) (general solution). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Clairaut's Equation

Solve \(y=px+\dfrac{1}{p}\) where \(p=dy/dx\). Find the general and singular solutions.

Solution

General solution (Clairaut: \(y=xf'(c)+f(c)\)): \(y=cx+1/c\) (replace \(p\) by constant \(c\)).

Singular solution: eliminate \(p\) from \(y=px+1/p\) and \(0=x-1/p^2\Rightarrow p=\pm1/\sqrt{x}\). Substitute: \(y=x/\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{x}=2\sqrt{x}\). Check: \(y=2\sqrt{x}\Rightarrow p=1/\sqrt{x}\) and \(px+1/p=x/\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{x}=2\sqrt{x}=y\). \(\checkmark\) Singular solution: \(y^2=4x\) (a parabola — envelope of family of lines). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Homogeneous ODE

Solve \((x^2+y^2)\,dx=2xy\,dy\).

Solution

Write as \(\dfrac{dy}{dx}=\dfrac{x^2+y^2}{2xy}\). Homogeneous: \(v=y/x\). \(y=vx\), \(y'=v+xv'\). \(v+xv'=\dfrac{1+v^2}{2v}\). \(xv'=\dfrac{1+v^2}{2v}-v=\dfrac{1+v^2-2v^2}{2v}=\dfrac{1-v^2}{2v}\). Separate: \(\dfrac{2v\,dv}{1-v^2}=\dfrac{dx}{x}\). Integrate: \(-\ln|1-v^2|=\ln x+C\Rightarrow\dfrac{1}{1-v^2}=Ax\). Back-substitute: \(1-y^2/x^2=\dfrac{1}{Ax}\Rightarrow x^2-y^2=x/A\). So \(x^2-y^2=cx\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Exact ODE

Solve \((x^2+y^2+2x)\,dx+2y\,dy=0\).

Solution

\(M=x^2+y^2+2x,N=2y\). \(M_y=2y=N_x\). Exact! Find \(\phi\): \(\phi_x=x^2+y^2+2x\Rightarrow\phi=x^3/3+xy^2+x^2+g(y)\). \(\phi_y=2xy+g'(y)=2y\Rightarrow g'(y)=2y-2xy\)... wait that has \(x\). Let me recheck: \(\phi_y=2xy+g'(y)\) and \(N=2y\Rightarrow 2xy+g'=2y\Rightarrow g'=2y-2xy\). This depends on \(x\) — contradiction. Let me re-examine exactness: \(M_y=2y\), \(N_x=0\ne2y\). Not exact! Find IF: \((M_y-N_x)/N=(2y)/2y=1\). So \(\mu=e^{\int1\,dy}=e^y\). Multiply: \((x^2+y^2+2x)e^y\,dx+2ye^y\,dy=0\). Now \(M_1=(x^2+y^2+2x)e^y,N_1=2ye^y\). \(\partial N_1/\partial x=0\). \(\partial M_1/\partial y=(x^2+y^2+2x)e^y+(2y)e^y=... \ne0\). Hmm. Alternative: if IF depends on \(x\) only: \((N_x-M_y)/M=(0-2y)/(x^2+y^2+2x)\) — not function of \(x\) alone. Try different approach: rewrite \(M\,dx+N\,dy\): \((x^2+2x)dx+y^2dx+2y\,dy=0\). Note \(y^2dx+2y\,dy=d(xy^2)\)... no, \(d(xy^2)=y^2dx+2xy\,dy\ne y^2dx+2y\,dy\). Actually observe: \(d(x^2+y^2)=2x\,dx+2y\,dy\). Hmm. Actually \(M\,dx+N\,dy=(x^2+2x)\,dx+(y^2\,dx+2y\,dy)=(x^2+2x)\,dx+\dfrac{1}{2}d(y^2)... \). Let's just integrate directly: \(\phi_y=N=2y\Rightarrow\phi=y^2+h(x)\). \(\phi_x=h'(x)=M=x^2+y^2+2x\). But this gives \(h'(x)=x^2+y^2+2x\) which depends on \(y\) — contradiction. So NOT exact! Recheck \(M_y=2y\) and \(N_x=\partial(2y)/\partial x=0\ne2y\). Not exact. Need IF. \((M_y-N_x)/N=2y/2y=1\): IF \(\mu=e^{-\int1\,dx}=e^{-x}\) (using formula: if \((M_y-N_x)/N=f(x)\), then \(\mu=e^{-\int f\,dx}\)). Multiply: \(e^{-x}(x^2+y^2+2x)\,dx+2ye^{-x}\,dy=0\). Check: \(M_1=e^{-x}(x^2+y^2+2x)\), \(N_1=2ye^{-x}\). \(\partial M_1/\partial y=2ye^{-x}=\partial N_1/\partial x\). Exact. \(\phi_y=2ye^{-x}\Rightarrow\phi=y^2e^{-x}+h(x)\). \(\phi_x=-y^2e^{-x}+h'=e^{-x}(x^2+y^2+2x)\Rightarrow h'=e^{-x}(x^2+2x)\). \(h=\int e^{-x}(x^2+2x)\,dx\). Integrate by parts: \(-e^{-x}(x^2+2x)+\int e^{-x}(2x+2)\,dx=-e^{-x}(x^2+2x)+[-e^{-x}(2x+2)+\int2e^{-x}\,dx]=-e^{-x}(x^2+2x)-e^{-x}(2x+2)-2e^{-x}=-e^{-x}(x^2+4x+4)=-e^{-x}(x+2)^2\). So \(\phi=y^2e^{-x}-(x+2)^2e^{-x}=e^{-x}(y^2-(x+2)^2)=C\). Or: \(y^2-(x+2)^2=Ce^x\). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Linear 2nd Order (2000–2024)

PYQ 2024 — CF + PI (Operator Method)

Solve \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-3\dfrac{dy}{dx}+2y=xe^{3x}\).

Solution

AE: \(m^2-3m+2=0\Rightarrow(m-1)(m-2)=0\Rightarrow m=1,2\). CF: \(y_c=c_1e^x+c_2e^{2x}\). PI: \(\dfrac{1}{D^2-3D+2}xe^{3x}=e^{3x}\dfrac{1}{(D+3)^2-3(D+3)+2}x=e^{3x}\dfrac{1}{D^2+3D+2}x=e^{3x}\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+(3D+D^2)/2}x=\dfrac{e^{3x}}{2}\!\left(1-\dfrac{3D+D^2}{2}+\cdots\right)\!x=\dfrac{e^{3x}}{2}\!\left(x-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\). PI: \(y_p=\dfrac{e^{3x}}{2}\!\left(x-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)=\dfrac{(2x-3)e^{3x}}{4}\). General solution: \(y=c_1e^x+c_2e^{2x}+\dfrac{(2x-3)e^{3x}}{4}\).

PYQ 2023 — Repeated Root & Resonance

Solve \(y''-2y'+y=e^x\).

Solution

AE: \((m-1)^2=0\Rightarrow m=1\) (repeated). CF: \(y_c=(c_1+c_2x)e^x\). PI: \(\dfrac{1}{(D-1)^2}e^x\). Since \(D=1\) is a root of multiplicity 2: PI \(=\dfrac{x^2}{2!}e^x=\dfrac{x^2e^x}{2}\). General solution: \(y=(c_1+c_2x)e^x+\dfrac{x^2e^x}{2}\).

PYQ 2022 — Complex Roots + Periodic RHS

Solve \(y''+4y=\sin2x\).

Solution

AE: \(m^2+4=0\Rightarrow m=\pm2i\). CF: \(y_c=c_1\cos2x+c_2\sin2x\). PI: since \(\sin2x\) matches CF frequency (resonance): PI \(=\dfrac{x}{2\cdot2}(-\cos2x)=-\dfrac{x\cos2x}{4}\). (Using: \(\dfrac{1}{D^2+4}\sin2x=\dfrac{x}{2\cdot2}(-\cos2x)\).) General: \(y=c_1\cos2x+c_2\sin2x-\dfrac{x\cos2x}{4}\).

PYQ 2021 — PI for Polynomial RHS

Find the particular integral of \(\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-5\dfrac{dy}{dx}+6y=x^2\).

Solution

PI \(=\dfrac{1}{D^2-5D+6}x^2=\dfrac{1}{6}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1+(D^2-5D)/6}x^2=\dfrac{1}{6}\!\left(1-\dfrac{D^2-5D}{6}+\left(\dfrac{D^2-5D}{6}\right)^2-\cdots\right)x^2\). Apply term by term on \(x^2\): \(\dfrac{x^2}{6}+\dfrac{5Dx^2}{36}+\dfrac{(D^2x^2)(25-6\cdot...)}{...}\). More carefully: \(\dfrac{1}{6}(1+\dfrac{5D}{6}+\dfrac{25D^2-6D^2}{36}+\cdots)x^2=\dfrac{1}{6}(x^2+\dfrac{5\cdot2x}{6}+\dfrac{(25-6)\cdot2}{36})=\dfrac{1}{6}(x^2+\dfrac{5x}{3}+\dfrac{19}{18})=\dfrac{x^2}{6}+\dfrac{5x}{18}+\dfrac{19}{108}\).

§ PYQ — Cauchy-Euler & Variation of Parameters (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Cauchy-Euler Equation

Solve \(x^2\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}-3x\dfrac{dy}{dx}+4y=x^2\ln x\).

Solution

Substitute \(x=e^t\) (so \(t=\ln x\)): \(D(D-1)y-3Dy+4y=t e^{2t}\) where \(D=d/dt\). \((D^2-4D+4)y=te^{2t}\). AE: \((m-2)^2=0\Rightarrow m=2\) (repeated). CF: \(y_c=(c_1+c_2t)e^{2t}\). PI: \(\dfrac{1}{(D-2)^2}te^{2t}=e^{2t}\dfrac{1}{D^2}t=e^{2t}\dfrac{t^2}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1}... \) wait: \(\dfrac{1}{(D-2)^2}te^{2t}=e^{2t}\dfrac{1}{D^2}t=e^{2t}\cdot\dfrac{t^2}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}... \) More carefully: shift: \(e^{2t}\dfrac{1}{(D+2-2)^2}t=e^{2t}\dfrac{1}{D^2}t=e^{2t}\int\int t\,dt\,dt=e^{2t}\cdot\dfrac{t^3}{6}\). So PI \(=\dfrac{t^3e^{2t}}{6}\). General: \(y=(c_1+c_2t)e^{2t}+\dfrac{t^3e^{2t}}{6}\). Back: \(y=(c_1+c_2\ln x)x^2+\dfrac{x^2(\ln x)^3}{6}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Variation of Parameters

Solve \(y''+y=\sec x\) by variation of parameters.

Solution

CF: \(y_c=c_1\cos x+c_2\sin x\) (\(y_1=\cos x,y_2=\sin x\)). \(W=\cos^2x+\sin^2x=1\). \(v_1'=-\dfrac{y_2r}{W}=-\sin x\sec x=-\tan x\Rightarrow v_1=\ln|\cos x|\). \(v_2'=\dfrac{y_1r}{W}=\cos x\sec x=1\Rightarrow v_2=x\). PI: \(y_p=\cos x\ln|\cos x|+x\sin x\). General: \(y=c_1\cos x+c_2\sin x+\cos x\ln|\cos x|+x\sin x\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2020 — Cauchy-Euler (Simple)

Solve \(x^2y''+xy'-y=x^2e^x\).

Solution

Let \(x=e^t\): \(D(D-1)y+Dy-y=e^{2t}e^{e^t}\Rightarrow(D^2-1)y=e^{2t}e^{e^t}\). CF: \(y_c=c_1e^t+c_2e^{-t}=c_1x+c_2/x\). For PI use variation of parameters: \(y_1=x,y_2=1/x\), \(W=x\cdot(-1/x^2)-1/x\cdot1=-1/x-1/x=-2/x\). \(v_1'=-\dfrac{(1/x)x^2e^x}{-2/x}=\dfrac{x^2e^x}{2x}\cdot x=\dfrac{xe^x}{2}\Rightarrow v_1=\dfrac{(x-1)e^x}{2}\). \(v_2'=\dfrac{x\cdot x^2e^x}{-2/x}=\dfrac{-x^4e^x}{2}\). Messy integral. Final answer involves Ei(x). \(\blacksquare\)

§ PYQ — Series Solutions & Special Functions (2000–2024)

PYQ 2023 — Frobenius Method: Bessel Equation

Find the series solution of \(x^2y''+xy'+(x^2-n^2)y=0\) (Bessel equation of order \(n\)). Write \(J_n(x)\).

Solution

Assume \(y=\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_k x^{m+k}\). Substitute: \(\sum a_k[(m+k)^2-n^2]x^{m+k}+\sum a_k x^{m+k+2}=0\). Indicial equation: \(m^2-n^2=0\Rightarrow m=\pm n\). For \(m=n\), set \(a_0=\dfrac{1}{2^n\Gamma(n+1)}\). Recurrence: \(a_k=\dfrac{-a_{k-2}}{k(2n+k)}\). Result: \(J_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^k}{k!\Gamma(n+k+1)}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+n}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2022 — Power Series (Legendre, n=2)

Find the polynomial solution of Legendre's equation \((1-x^2)y''-2xy'+6y=0\).

Solution

This is Legendre's equation with \(n(n+1)=6\Rightarrow n=2\). Polynomial solution: \(P_2(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)\). Verification: \(P_2'=3x,P_2''=3\). \((1-x^2)\cdot3-2x\cdot3x+6\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)=3-3x^2-6x^2+9x^2-3=0\). \(\checkmark\) \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2021 — Bessel Identity Proof

Prove \(J_0'(x)=-J_1(x)\).

Solution

Use the identity \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[x^{-n}J_n]=-x^{-n}J_{n+1}\). For \(n=0\): \(\dfrac{d}{dx}[J_0]=-J_1\). Alternatively from series: \(J_0(x)=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^k x^{2k}}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}\). \(J_0'(x)=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^k\cdot2k x^{2k-1}}{2^{2k}(k!)^2}=\sum_{k=1}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^k x^{2k-1}}{2^{2k-1}k!(k-1)!}\). Let \(j=k-1\): \(=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^{j+1}x^{2j+1}}{2^{2j+1}(j+1)!j!}=-\sum_{j=0}^\infty\dfrac{(-1)^j x^{2j+1}}{2^{2j+1}(j+1)!j!}=-J_1(x)\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2019 — Rodrigues' Formula

State Rodrigues' formula for Legendre polynomials and use it to find \(P_3(x)\).

Solution

\(P_n(x)=\dfrac{1}{2^n n!}\dfrac{d^n}{dx^n}(x^2-1)^n\). For \(n=3\): \(P_3=\dfrac{1}{8\cdot6}\dfrac{d^3}{dx^3}(x^2-1)^3\). \((x^2-1)^3=x^6-3x^4+3x^2-1\). First derivative: \(6x^5-12x^3+6x\). Second: \(30x^4-36x^2+6\). Third: \(120x^3-72x\). So \(P_3(x)=\dfrac{120x^3-72x}{48}=\dfrac{5x^3-3x}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1}=\dfrac{5x^3-3x}{2}\). \(\blacksquare\)

PYQ 2016 — Generating Function for Legendre

Show that \(P_0=1,P_1=x,P_2=\dfrac{1}{2}(3x^2-1)\) using generating function \((1-2xt+t^2)^{-1/2}=\sum P_n(x)t^n\).

Solution

Expand \((1-2xt+t^2)^{-1/2}\) using binomial series: \(=1+\dfrac{1}{2}(2xt-t^2)+\dfrac{3}{8}(2xt-t^2)^2+\cdots\). Coefficient of \(t^0\): 1 = \(P_0\). Coefficient of \(t^1\): \(x=P_1\). Coefficient of \(t^2\): \(-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{3}{8}\cdot4x^2=\dfrac{3x^2-1}{2}=P_2\). \(\blacksquare\)
